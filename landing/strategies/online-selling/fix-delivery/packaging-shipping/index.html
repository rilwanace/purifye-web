You are spending more time on packaging than you think, buying supplies in a panic when you run out, and still getting occasional complaints about damaged arrivals. Meanwhile your packaging corner has bubble wrap from three different suppliers, four box sizes that do not fit anything properly, and no system whatsoever. This is fixable in a weekend. A good packaging setup is not about spending more — it is about deciding once so you do not have to decide every time.

01
Build a dedicated packaging station — one place, everything within reach

The most expensive thing about bad packaging is not the materials — it is the time. Every time you walk to another room for tape, search for a scissors, or realise you are out of bubble wrap at 9pm, you are paying a hidden cost that compounds across every order you process.

Designate a fixed spot: a table, a corner, a shelf unit. Everything lives there. Tape gun, cutter, bags, boxes, labels, pens, bubble wrap, fragile stickers. Nothing moves. When you run low on something, you notice immediately and reorder before you run out.

This is not about having a photogenic packing station. It is about eliminating decision fatigue and wasted movement. Sellers who set this up consistently report cutting their packing time by 40 to 60% just from having everything in one place.

Label your stock areas too. One section for your five box sizes. One section for polybags by dimension. One drawer for fragile wrap. When an order comes in, you know in ten seconds what container it needs.

A good system means you make packaging decisions once — not once per order.

02
Standardise to five box or bag sizes and nothing else

Most sellers accumulate box sizes organically — whatever the supplier sent, whatever was on sale, whatever was left over from another delivery. The result is a chaotic selection where nothing fits anything and you spend five minutes every order deciding what to use.

Pick five sizes that cover 90% of your products. Write them down. Buy in bulk only those five. Sell or use up everything else. If a new product does not fit your five sizes, that is useful information: it might need a custom solution, or it might be a product that is genuinely difficult to ship.

For polybag-friendly products (clothing, soft goods, most beauty items), three poly sizes cover almost everything: small, medium, and large. Add a mailer box and a medium shipping box and you have handled 95% of scenarios.

Standardising your packaging also makes your cost calculation reliable. If every small item ships in the same polybag, you know exactly what that item costs to pack. That number goes into your pricing from day one — not discovered three months later.

Five sizes decided once is better than twenty sizes decided every time.

03
Calculate your real packaging cost before you price anything

Packaging is a cost of goods sold. It is not a rounding error. For a LKR 1,500 order, your packaging might be LKR 80 to 200 depending on product size and fragility. That is 5 to 13% of your order value — absorbed by you if you did not build it into the price.

Calculate it item by item. Polybag: LKR 20. Mailer box: LKR 65. Bubble wrap layer: LKR 35. Fragile tape: LKR 10. Label: LKR 5. Courier charge: LKR 250. Total packaging and shipping cost per order: LKR 385. If you priced the product without that number, you are losing money on every delivery.

Where to source packaging cheaply in Sri Lanka: Pettah has multiple packaging wholesalers near Manning Market. Daraz has bulk polybag sellers. For boxes, Wellampitiya has paper and carton manufacturers who sell retail quantities. Buying a month's supply at once almost always beats buying per-order.

Update your product prices quarterly as packaging costs shift. Polybag prices have moved with petroleum costs. Box prices follow paper and cardboard input prices. A price you set a year ago may not cover today's packaging cost.

Every LKR you spend on packaging that is not in your price is a LKR out of your profit.

04
Protect fragile items properly — the maths make it obvious

Fragile item sellers often under-wrap because bubble wrap costs money. But one return of a broken item costs more than every bubble wrap layer you could have used in a week of orders.

Do the calculation once: your broken item cost (replacement or refund) plus courier return charge plus the time to handle the complaint equals your true cost of under-protection. For most fragile products, one return per 20 orders from bad packaging costs more than double-wrapping every order in your range.

Build fragile protection into your standard: one bubble wrap layer minimum, two for glass or ceramic, foam inserts for anything that can shift inside the box. Add a 'Handle With Care — Fragile' sticker on the outside. Some couriers deprioritise fragile handling in practice, but the sticker at minimum documents your intent and reduces your liability in a dispute.

If you are selling fragile items with COD, your rejection and return rate when items arrive damaged is particularly costly because you also absorb the outbound courier cost. Double protection for all COD fragile shipments is non-negotiable if you want the maths to work.

The cost of protection is certain. The cost of damage is uncertain — and always higher.

05
Decide courier pickup vs drop-off based on your actual volume

Courier pickup sounds convenient but most services charge for it or require minimum volumes. If you are doing 5 to 10 orders per day, dropping at a courier service point is almost always faster than waiting for pickup windows and handling the coordination.

At 20 or more orders per day, a daily pickup negotiated with your courier starts making economic sense — both in time saved and as a negotiating point for better rates. Below that, the drop-off model gives you more control over dispatch timing.

Map your nearest courier service points for your top two carriers. Time the trip once. If it takes 20 minutes and you go daily, that is 100 minutes per week. If batch-dispatching every two days halves that trip frequency without materially affecting delivery time, it is worth considering.

For Daraz orders: Daraz has its own pickup points and seller hubs. Using these instead of third-party couriers for Daraz fulfilment sometimes results in faster tracking updates and fewer complaints from buyers who can see their parcel in the system sooner.

The right dispatch method is the one that costs you the least time at your current volume.

06
Buy packaging supplies in bulk and never run out

Running out of polybags or tape mid-week is not just inconvenient — it delays orders, which delays your cash, which delays your ability to restock. The operational cost of running out of supplies is always higher than the inventory carrying cost of keeping a two-week buffer.

Calculate your average weekly consumption of each supply type. Order a four-week buffer when you drop below two weeks. This is the same logic that drives a good kitchen — you do not run to the shop mid-dinner preparation.

Bulk supply sources in Sri Lanka: polybags and mailer bags from Pettah (Manning Market area and Olcott Mawatha) or through Daraz B2B sellers. Bubble roll from carton suppliers in Wellampitiya or Kelanitissa. Tape and stationery from wholesale stationery shops near Pettah or Narahenpita.

Track your monthly packaging spend as a line item. Most sellers who do this for the first time discover they are spending 15 to 25% more than necessary because they are buying retail in small quantities in a panic. Bulk buying at wholesale prices typically cuts this by 30 to 40%.

Running out of packaging supplies is a supply chain problem — and supply chain problems compound.

Your 4-week plan

Here's how to execute everything above, week by week.

week1
Set up your packaging station
Choose a fixed location — table, shelf unit, or corner
Move all existing packaging supplies there
Identify what is missing or low stock
Write out your five standard sizes and confirm they cover your products
3 hrs
week2
Calculate your true packaging cost per product
List every component for a typical order: bag/box, wrap, tape, label
Price each component from your current suppliers
Calculate total packaging cost per product size tier
Compare against what you currently price — adjust if needed
2 hrs
week3
Source bulk supplies
Identify your two or three highest-volume supply items
Find a wholesale supplier for each (Pettah, Wellampitiya, or Daraz B2B)
Calculate the unit cost difference vs your current source
Place a four-week buffer order
4 hrs (including travel if in-person)
week4
Build your reorder triggers
Note the two-week quantity of each supply item
Label your storage: write 'reorder when this level reached' on the shelf
Set a weekly 5-minute check to scan supply levels
Add packaging cost to your monthly financial tracking
1 hr
