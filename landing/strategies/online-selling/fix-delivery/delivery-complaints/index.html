“Every bad review about delivery has your name on it — even when the courier is the one who failed.”
“You can't control how fast the van moves. You can control what happens before and after.”
“The seller who over-communicates loses fewer customers than the one who waits for the complaint to arrive.”

You packed the order perfectly, printed the slip, handed it to the courier on time. Two days later your buyer is raging because it arrived late, came damaged, or never came at all — and they are coming for you, not the courier. This is the most demoralising part of online selling in Sri Lanka, and it happens to every seller. The difference between sellers who survive it and sellers who quit is not better couriers. It is a better system for everything that surrounds the courier.

01
Set delivery expectations before the buyer sets their disappointment

Most delivery complaints are not really about the delivery. They are about unmet expectations. The buyer expected two days and got five. You never promised two days — but they assumed it.

The fix is simple: say the number first. Before the buyer can imagine their own timeline, give them yours. 'Your order will be dispatched tomorrow and typically arrives within 3 to 5 working days.' Say this in every order confirmation message you send.

For Daraz, set your processing time and handling time accurately in your seller centre — do not set 1 day if you actually need 2. Daraz factors this into the delivery estimate buyers see, and if your estimate is wrong, so is theirs.

For WhatsApp and Instagram orders, send a dispatch confirmation the moment you hand over to the courier. Include the waybill number and the courier's tracking link. Buyers who can see their parcel moving complain 70% less than buyers who are left guessing.

Managing expectations is not customer service — it is complaint prevention.

02
Choose your courier deliberately, not by habit

Most Sri Lankan sellers use whichever courier their first supplier used, or whichever was cheapest at the time. They stay there even after the problems start because switching feels complicated. But not all couriers perform equally for all routes, and using the wrong one for your product type is a slow bleed.

Koombiyo holds a 1.6 out of 5 rating on Trustpilot from Sri Lankan buyers. That number is not a secret — your buyers can see it too. If you are routing all your orders through a courier with a known reliability problem, you are borrowing their reputation.

Pronto, PickMe Flash, and smaller regional operators often have stronger track records for specific routes. If most of your buyers are in the Western Province, your calculus is different from a seller shipping to Kurunegala and Matara weekly.

Run a simple test: pick your top 20 recent orders, check which had complaints and which courier handled them. If a pattern emerges, act on it. Courier relationships can be renegotiated or split by region.

The courier you chose when you started may not be the right courier for where you are now.

03
Build a three-touch communication loop for every order

Three messages. That is all it takes to turn a potentially upset buyer into one who feels taken care of. Most sellers send zero. The best sellers send all three.

Message one: order confirmed, dispatch date, what to expect. Message two: dispatched today, waybill number, tracking link, estimated arrival window. Message three: delivered? Let me know if everything arrived safely — if anything is wrong, message me now.

This loop takes 3 minutes per order when you have a template ready. It does two things: it pre-empts complaints by keeping the buyer informed, and it gives you early warning when something went wrong so you can act before the review is written.

Buyers who hear from you before they have to ask are significantly less likely to leave a negative review — even when the delivery was delayed. Being ignored during a problem is what turns frustration into a public complaint.

You cannot fix courier failures. You can fix silence.

04
Handle delivery complaints faster than they can escalate

When a buyer messages about a delivery problem, the clock starts. Every hour you take to respond is an hour that complaint grows. The buyer who messages at 9am and gets no response by noon writes the review at 1pm.

Have a response template ready. Not a scripted brush-off — a genuine response that acknowledges the problem, tells them what you are doing right now, and gives them a specific next step. 'I am checking with the courier now and will update you within 2 hours' is infinitely better than silence or a defensive reply.

For Daraz orders, check the seller centre first — sometimes the system shows delivered when the courier has not attempted delivery. Do not argue with the buyer until you have checked. If the tracking shows delivered and the buyer says they did not receive it, open a courier dispute and tell the buyer you have done so.

The goal is not to win the argument. The goal is to convert a potentially negative review into a neutral or positive one. A buyer whose complaint was handled well is often a repeat buyer. A buyer who was ignored is not.

Speed is not just courtesy — it is review management.

05
Use courier failures as a negotiation asset, not an excuse

When something genuinely goes wrong — lost parcel, damaged item, wrong address delivered — you have a choice. You can apologise, blame the courier, and move on. Or you can actually do something that costs you a little now and saves you a lot in reputation.

Offer a partial refund or replacement before the buyer asks for one. This sounds expensive and it is not. A LKR 300 goodwill credit on a LKR 2,500 order costs you 12% of one sale. Losing that buyer permanently costs you every future order they would have placed.

Meanwhile, file the courier claim. Most couriers have a damage or loss claims process. Koombiyo, Pronto, and DHL Express all have formal claims procedures. You will not always win, but filing consistently creates a paper trail that matters if the problem becomes systematic.

Track which orders you issue goodwill refunds on and which courier handled them. If one courier is generating disproportionate goodwill spend, that is your cost-per-order calculation being inflated invisibly.

Generosity with a dissatisfied buyer is almost always cheaper than losing them.

06
Track your own delivery performance by courier and region

Most sellers know roughly how often deliveries go wrong. Almost none of them know exactly which courier, which region, and which product type is causing the problem. Without that data, every fix is a guess.

Set up a simple spreadsheet: order date, courier, destination district, delivery date, complaint (yes/no), complaint type. Fill it in weekly. After 4 to 6 weeks, patterns you could not see before become obvious.

The insight you are looking for: is courier A reliably bad for Northern Province orders but fine for Colombo? Are fragile items getting damaged regardless of courier? Is one COD route generating rejection and return costs that wipe out your margin for that district?

This data gives you real leverage. You can approach a courier with specific complaint rates and ask for better handling standards on certain routes. You can stop shipping fragile items with couriers that lack handling protocols. You can add a fragile item surcharge that covers your real damage rate.

You cannot manage what you do not measure — and delivery is where most margin disappears.

Your 4-week plan

Here's how to execute everything above, week by week.

week1
Set up your communication templates
Write your three order messages: confirmed, dispatched, delivered check-in
Save waybill number placeholder in each template
Update your Daraz processing time to be accurate
Test-send the templates to yourself to check formatting
3 hrs
week2
Audit your last 30 orders for delivery complaints
List each order: courier used, destination, complaint or not
Calculate your complaint rate per courier
Identify any pattern by region or product type
Decide whether any courier switch is warranted
2 hrs
week3
Start your complaint handling system
Write a complaint response template — acknowledge, act, update
Set a response SLA for yourself: respond within 2 hours during operating hours
Pin the complaint template in WhatsApp for quick access
For Daraz: bookmark the courier claims process page
2 hrs
week4
Build your ongoing delivery tracking log
Set up a simple spreadsheet with the columns: date, courier, district, delivered, complaint
Fill in retroactively from memory for the past 2 weeks
Commit to updating it weekly going forward
Review the data monthly to spot emerging patterns
2 hrs
After 4 weeks, you'll have:
✓
Three communication templates live and in use for every order
✓
A clear view of which courier is causing which problems
✓
A complaint response system that acts before reviews are written
✓
A delivery tracking log that gives you real data to negotiate with
✓
Buyers who feel communicated with, not chased for updates
Delivery complaints do not stop. But with this system, most of them stop becoming reviews.

The courier will always be a variable you cannot fully control. Your communication, your response speed, and your tracking are all completely within your control — and they are what buyers actually remember.

Start with the three-message communication loop. Set it up today. Every order you dispatch without it is a complaint waiting to happen.
