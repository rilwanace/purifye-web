COD is both the most important feature of Sri Lankan e-commerce and its most painful reality. Only 400,000 Sri Lankans use QR payments despite 89% bank account ownership. Until that changes, you need to build COD into your pricing, reduce rejection rates, and shift buyers to prepay wherever you can.

01
Understand why COD dominates — and do the maths on what it costs

Only 400,000 Sri Lankans are using QR-based payments despite 89% adult bank account ownership. The reason: deep distrust of online payment among a significant portion of the population. COD isn't just preference — for many buyers, it's the only acceptable option.

The cost of COD rejections: COD return rates of 15–30% are common for new sellers, especially for outstation deliveries and impulse categories. For a Rs.1,000 item with a Rs.300 delivery fee and 20% rejection rate: for every 5 COD orders you dispatch, 1 comes back. That returned order costs you Rs.300 in return shipping (you pay both ways). Effective delivery cost per successful sale: Rs.360, not Rs.300.

Full maths example: Rs.1,000 selling price. Product cost Rs.400. Delivery Rs.300 (×5 orders = Rs.1,500). Return delivery Rs.300 (×1 rejection). COD platform fee Rs.50. Net: Rs.1,000×4 = Rs.4,000 revenue from 4 successful sales. Costs: Rs.400×5 + Rs.1,500 + Rs.300 + Rs.50×5 = Rs.5,050. Gross profit: —Rs.1,050. This is not unusual for new sellers with high rejection rates.

Building in a COD buffer: if you expect 20% rejection, add the expected return cost per unit to your pricing. Rs.300 return × 20% = Rs.60 per item sold. That Rs.60 must come from somewhere — either higher product price or lower margin expectation.

02
Build COD rejection buffer into your pricing from day one

Most sellers set prices based on product cost + desired margin. Few explicitly calculate COD buffer. This is why their profit calculations always look wrong at end of month.

COD buffer formula: Expected rejection rate × (return shipping cost + packaging cost). If your courier charges Rs.250 return, you have Rs.100 in packaging, and your rejection rate is 20%: COD buffer = 20% × Rs.350 = Rs.70 per unit. Add Rs.70 to your selling price, or accept it as a cost of doing business.

Setting the buffer at price level: if your item costs Rs.500 and you want 30% margin with 20% COD rejection rate: base price = Rs.500/(1-0.30) = Rs.714. Add COD buffer Rs.70. Minimum price = Rs.784. Round to Rs.800.

Sellers who don't build this in are subsidising their buyers' rejection decisions. Every returned package is a transfer of money from you to the courier company.

03
Shift buyers to prepay — specific tactics that work in SL

Small discount for bank transfer: Rs.50–100 off for prepaid orders is enough to shift a meaningful percentage of buyers, especially those who were going to pay anyway and appreciate the saving. Announce it explicitly: 'Pay by bank transfer and get Rs.100 off.'

LANKAQR: The government's national QR payment system has zero merchant fees for transactions under Rs.5,000. Merchants and users are offered lottery incentives under the 2024 National QR Payment Adoption Programme. Setup takes 15 minutes. Worth having as an option even if few buyers use it yet.

Build trust first, then push prepay: Buyers choose COD because they don't trust sellers to deliver. Social proof (reviews, testimonials, customer photos), visible Facebook/Instagram profiles with consistent posting history, and prompt order confirmations reduce the trust deficit — making buyers more willing to prepay on future orders.

PayHere payment link: For social commerce sales (Instagram DM, Facebook), share a PayHere payment link instead of your bank account number. This eliminates the screenshot step entirely — buyers pay online, you get a confirmed payment notification. No screenshot fraud possible.

Trust is the real barrier to prepayment. Every review, every on-time delivery, and every professional interaction is a deposit in the trust account that eventually lets you ask buyers to pay upfront.

05
Reduce rejection rates through better pre-dispatch qualification

High rejection rates often indicate a mismatch between what buyers expected and what they received. Common causes: product photos that look significantly different from the actual product, size/colour descriptions that are inaccurate, COD orders placed impulsively at 2am that the buyer regrets by delivery day.

Pre-dispatch WhatsApp confirmation: For orders above Rs.1,500 or from first-time buyers, send a confirmation message before dispatching: 'Hi, just confirming your order of [product] for Rs.[price] to be delivered to [area]. This will be dispatched tomorrow. Please confirm your address is correct.' Buyers who would have rejected are more likely to acknowledge or cancel now rather than reject at the door.

Delay dispatch for suspicious patterns: Multiple COD orders of the same product from the same area in a short period. First-time buyers for high-value items. Orders to hard-to-verify remote addresses. Adding a 4–8 hour window before dispatching gives you time to confirm legitimacy.

Build a reject list: Track buyers who reject COD orders. Add them to an internal 'prepay only' list. When they order again, message: 'We require bank transfer for this order based on our delivery records.' This isn't punishment — it's business policy.

Every COD rejection you prevent is a double win: you keep the margin, and the courier slot goes to a real order.

06
Track COD performance as a core business metric

Your COD rejection rate is one of the most important metrics in your business. If you don't know it, you're flying blind. Track: total COD orders dispatched, COD orders successfully delivered, COD orders rejected, return cost, and net COD performance (revenue from successful deliveries minus all COD-related costs).

Target: below 15% rejection rate for established sellers with social proof. Above 25% consistently means something is wrong — either product photos are misleading, your target audience is too impulse-driven for your product type, or you're sourcing COD orders from low-trust channels.

Monthly COD review: which products have the highest rejection rates? Which delivery areas have the most rejections? Are first-time buyers rejecting more than repeat buyers (answer: almost always yes).

The insight most sellers miss: rejection rates go down as trust goes up. More reviews, longer posting history, more repeat buyers — all reduce rejection rates. Your COD problem is partly a marketing problem.

A 5-percentage-point improvement in COD rejection rate adds more to your monthly profit than a 10% price increase.
