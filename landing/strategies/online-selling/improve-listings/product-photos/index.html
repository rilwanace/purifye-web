Your product is good. Your price is fair. Your listing is up. And still buyers scroll past without clicking. In most cases the photos are the problem — not because they are technically bad, but because they are not doing the job photos are supposed to do: answer every buyer question without words, make the product look desirable, and remove the doubt that stops someone from pressing Buy. You do not need a photographer or a studio. You need a system, natural light, and a clean background.

01
Understand what your photo needs to do, not just show

A product photo has one job: reduce the perceived risk of buying. Buyers cannot hold your product, so every doubt they have — how big is it really, what does the texture look like, does it match my skin tone, will this fit — must be answered by the photo. If your photo does not answer those questions, the buyer does not buy.

Before you photograph anything, list the top three doubts your specific buyers have about this product. For clothing: size and fit. For skincare: colour and consistency. For accessories: actual size versus perceived size in photo. For electronics: ports, buttons, build quality. Your photo set needs to answer all three.

The complete photo set for a Daraz or Instagram listing: one clean main shot (white or light background), one lifestyle shot (product in use or in context), one detail shot (texture, material, label), one size reference shot (next to a common object or on a person), one packaging shot (what arrives at the door). Five photos answer more doubts than one.

Sellers who think about what the buyer is worried about before shooting take better photos than sellers who focus on making the product look good. These are different objectives, and the buyer-worry objective converts better.

A photo that eliminates doubt converts. A photo that just shows the product does not.

02
Master natural light — it is free and better than studio lights

The single biggest improvement most Sri Lankan sellers can make to their product photos costs nothing: move to a window. Natural light — specifically indirect natural light from a window, not direct sunlight — is flattering, consistent, and free.

Direct sunlight creates harsh shadows and blown-out highlights that make products look flat or cheap. Indirect light (bright but not in direct sun path) is soft, even, and shows texture and colour accurately. Find the brightest window in your home or workspace. Photograph on the side of the window, not facing it.

Time of day matters in Sri Lanka given the intensity of direct sun: 7am to 9am and 3pm to 5pm give softer light on clear days. Overcast days are actually ideal — clouds act as a giant natural diffuser and give you consistent, shadow-free light without any equipment.

If you need to shoot in the evening, two identical light sources (two lamps with daylight bulbs, placed on either side of the product) gives even light without harsh shadows. Daylight bulbs (5000K to 6500K colour temperature) render colours accurately — warm bulbs make everything look orange and throw off colour-sensitive products like skincare or clothing.

Photographers pay for studio lights to replicate what your nearest window gives you for free.

03
Build a simple, reusable photography setup

A photography setup you can set up and break down in 10 minutes is worth more than a perfect studio you use twice. The goal is repeatability — every batch of photos looks consistent because the setup is always the same.

For a flat-lay or tabletop setup: a large sheet of white poster board (available from any stationery shop for under LKR 100) as your background, placed on a table near your light window. Product placed in the middle. Phone on a simple phone stand or propped on books to shoot straight down or straight on. This setup produces clean, professional-looking shots that Daraz and Instagram both perform well on.

For lifestyle shots: choose one or two locations in your home or environment that match your brand feel — a clean wooden table, a marble-effect tile, a plant background, a fabric surface. Shoot your lifestyle content in these same locations every time. Consistency across your product range creates a cohesive look that makes your store feel like a real brand.

For clothing: a plain wall with consistent light is better than a cluttered background. A cheap mannequin from Pettah or photos on a real person in front of a neutral wall consistently outperform flat-lay clothing shots on conversion.

Consistency in your setup beats perfection on any single shot.

04
Edit your photos to platform standard — not to artistic preference

Every platform has an implicit standard for how good photos look: Daraz product pages, Instagram feeds, Facebook marketplace listings. The goal of editing is to match or exceed that standard — not to express creativity. Save creativity for lifestyle content; keep product shots clean and accurate.

Basic editing that improves almost every product photo: increase brightness slightly, increase contrast slightly, reduce highlights if the background is blowing out, sharpen slightly. Snapseed (free, Android and iOS) does all of this in 3 minutes per photo with presets you can save and reapply.

What not to do: do not over-filter to the point where the product colour is inaccurate. A buyer who receives a product that does not match the photo in colour or tone will return it — and the return reason 'not as described' goes against you on Daraz. Accurate is always better than beautiful when the two conflict.

For Daraz: the main image must be on a white background with the product filling at least 85% of the frame. This is a technical requirement, not a preference. Non-compliant main images get rejected or listed with lower visibility.

Good editing makes accurate photos look their best. It does not make inaccurate photos look accurate.

05
Photograph a complete set in one session — not one at a time

The cost of setting up your photography space — mentally, physically, and in time — is the same whether you photograph one product or twenty. Batch your photography into one weekly or fortnightly session. Set everything up, then work through your entire pending product list.

Prepare a shot list before you start. For each product: main shot, detail shot, lifestyle shot, size reference shot. Check them off as you go. A three-hour session produces content for 10 to 15 products when you are working from a list rather than deciding what to shoot as you go.

Name and organise your photo files by product SKU or name immediately after the session. Sellers who dump all their photos in one folder and sort them later lose hours to organisation that could have been done in minutes at the time.

After each session, review your shots critically before putting down the setup. If a photo is not good enough, reshoot it now — not in two weeks when you have to set everything up again. The review takes 15 minutes and saves significant rework.

Batch photography turns a recurring daily task into a scheduled weekly one.

06
Use buyer questions to identify photo gaps

Every question a buyer asks before purchasing is a gap in your photo or description. 'What are the dimensions?' — add a size reference photo. 'What material is it?' — add a texture close-up. 'Does it come in other colours?' — add a colour variant shot. 'How does it look when worn?' — add a lifestyle shot.

Keep a running list of pre-purchase questions you receive for each product. When you see the same question twice, that is your cue: the current photos are not answering it. Update the photo set before the next listing cycle.

Daraz allows up to 9 images per listing — most sellers use 2 or 3. Instagram carousels can go to 10. Every additional photo you add, when it addresses a real buyer question, increases conversion. The upper limit on photos is not a constraint — it is an opportunity most sellers are not using.

The sellers with the highest conversion rates on Daraz consistently have fuller photo sets with deliberate variety. This is not coincidence — it is the direct result of removing more buyer doubt before the purchase decision.

Every buyer question you receive is a photo you have not taken yet.

Your 4-week plan

Here's how to execute everything above, week by week.

week1
Set up your photography space
Identify your brightest, most consistent natural light window
Buy white poster board from a stationery shop (LKR 50-100)
Set up your tabletop or flat-lay area
Test shoot one product with your phone and review the light quality
2 hrs (including setup and test)
week2
Build your shot list for your current product range
List every active product you are selling
Note which products have fewer than 4 photos in their listings
Write the shot types needed: main, detail, lifestyle, size reference
Plan your first batch photography session date
1 hr
week3
Run your first batch photography session
Work through your shot list in one session
Review all shots before packing up the setup
Edit your photos in Snapseed using consistent adjustments
Organise files by product name immediately
3-4 hrs
week4
Update your listings with the new photos
Upload new photos to your Daraz listings (main image first)
Replace the weakest photos on your Instagram highlights or grid
Track click-through rate or engagement change over the next 2 weeks
Note any buyer questions that come in — update photos accordingly
2 hrs
After 4 weeks, you'll have:
✓
A reusable photography setup you can deploy in 10 minutes
✓
Complete photo sets (4-6 images) for your top 10 products
✓
A batch photography habit that runs every 2 to 4 weeks
✓
Daraz-compliant main images for all active listings
✓
A direct connection between buyer questions and photo improvements
Better photos are not a one-time project. They are a habit that compounds over time as your listings get progressively better with each batch session.

The buyer who clicks away because your photo did not answer their question is invisible to you — you never see them. The ones who convert because you did answer their question are visible in your orders. Better photos change that ratio.

This weekend: find your window, buy one sheet of white poster board, and photograph your best-selling product from five angles. Upload those five photos and watch what happens to the listing in the following two weeks.
