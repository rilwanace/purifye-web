“Posting a product is not selling a product. Selling is a conversation — posting is just showing up.”
“The algorithm does not suppress bad content. It amplifies content that earns attention. The question is whether your content earns it.”
“The sellers who get orders from social media are not posting more. They are posting differently.”

You post a photo of your product with a price and your contact number. Nothing. Maybe one like from someone you know. No messages, no orders. You try again. Same result. You assume the algorithm is working against you, or your product is wrong, or the market is saturated. In most cases the actual problem is simpler: your content is an announcement, and announcements do not convert. On Facebook, Instagram, and WhatsApp, content that earns engagement is content that creates a reaction — curiosity, recognition, desire, entertainment. Product photos with prices do none of these things reliably.

01
Stop posting product announcements — start posting buyer problems

The most common social content mistake: leading with the product. 'New stock arrived — LKR 2,500 — PM to order.' This tells the buyer everything about your product and nothing about why it matters to them. In a feed full of other sellers doing the same thing, it is invisible.

Start with the buyer's problem or desire instead. 'Finding a skincare routine that actually works for Sri Lanka's humidity is almost impossible — we tested 6 products on local skin conditions and this is the one that held up.' Now the buyer is reading. Now you have their attention. Now the product fits into a story about their life, not your inventory.

This is not manipulation. It is relevance. Facebook in Sri Lanka has 15 million users and Instagram has 8.5 million — the people you are trying to reach are scrolling through enormous amounts of content. Content that immediately addresses something they are thinking about stops the scroll. Content that announces your latest stock does not.

Rewrite your next 5 product posts as buyer-problem posts. Start with the problem or desire. Let the product be the solution that appears in the middle or at the end. Compare the engagement and message rate with your previous posts.

The post that starts with the buyer's world outperforms the post that starts with your product every time.

02
Use video and reels — they get significantly more reach than photos

On Instagram and Facebook, short video content consistently achieves more organic reach than static images. This is not a preference — it is how the platforms allocate distribution. Instagram Reels and Facebook short videos are pushed to non-followers as discovery content in a way static posts are not.

You do not need production skills. The most effective e-commerce videos on Sri Lankan social media are simple: an unboxing, a before and after, a product in use, a packaging process, a behind-the-scenes clip. Authenticity outperforms polish for small sellers.

TikTok has 10.5 million users in Sri Lanka and is the fastest-growing discovery platform for products. Short product demonstration videos — 15 to 30 seconds, showing the product doing what it does — routinely reach audiences orders of magnitude larger than your follower count if they earn attention in the first two seconds.

The first two seconds of a video determine whether the viewer stays. Start with the result, the surprise, or the most visually interesting moment. A video that opens with your logo and a slow pan of the product loses viewers before they see anything interesting.

Video is not a format upgrade from photos. It is a different distribution system — one that reaches non-followers.

03
Build a content mix that includes proof, education, and entertainment

A feed of only product posts trains followers to skip your content — they know what they will see. A mixed content approach keeps your feed interesting enough that followers actually look when you post, and share when something resonates.

Proof content: customer photos, messages from happy buyers, before-and-after results. Proof posts have extremely high shareability among Sri Lankan social users and build trust more effectively than any ad. Educational content: tips related to your product category — skincare routines, fashion styling, home organisation. This positions you as knowledgeable, not just a seller. Entertainment: humour, relatable situations, trending audio on Reels and TikTok.

A simple content calendar for a seller posting 4 times per week: Monday — product post (buyer-problem format), Wednesday — proof post (customer photo or message with permission), Friday — educational tip, Sunday — lifestyle or entertainment post that subtly features your product. Four posts a week with variety is more effective than daily product announcements.

Customer proof posts with permission from the buyer are the highest-converting content type for most Sri Lankan sellers. Ask every satisfied buyer for a photo and permission to post. One genuine buyer post per week, consistently, builds social proof that no ad can replicate.

Content that earns shares is free distribution to audiences you have not reached yet.

04
Make ordering as easy as one message — remove every friction point

Buyers who are interested but face friction in ordering do not order. They move on. If your Instagram posts require the buyer to follow a link in bio, go to an external site, create an account, and fill a form — most of them will not. If a message to your WhatsApp results in an order in 3 messages — most engaged buyers will.

Every extra step between interest and order loses a percentage of buyers. Map the journey: buyer sees post, feels interest, what do they do next? If the next step is unclear, complicated, or broken, fix it before your next post.

The most effective call to action for Sri Lankan WhatsApp-centric commerce: 'Comment SIZE below or DM me now and I will confirm availability and delivery time.' This is specific, requires minimal effort from the buyer, and routes them to a channel where you can close the sale conversationally.

Add your WhatsApp number as a clickable link in every Instagram and Facebook post caption. In Sri Lanka, WhatsApp is where transactions happen — a buyer who has to find your number is a buyer who may not bother.

The post that makes ordering easy gets more orders than the post that makes ordering possible.

05
Post consistently — not whenever you have something to sell

The algorithm on every major platform rewards consistency. Accounts that post at predictable intervals receive more favourable distribution than accounts that post in bursts and then disappear for a week. Your follower growth, your reach per post, and your WhatsApp message rate all improve with regular posting schedules.

Consistent posting also trains your audience. Followers who see you reliably in their feed on certain days start to look for you. Followers who see you sporadically eventually stop expecting you — and stop engaging.

The minimum viable posting schedule for a seller building an audience: 3 to 4 times per week, every week. This is more important than posting quality in the early stages. A good-enough post published consistently outperforms a perfect post published randomly.

Batch your content creation to make consistency possible. One dedicated two-hour session per week — photography, video clips, caption writing — produces 4 to 5 posts ready to schedule. Scheduled posts do not require you to be online at the right moment; they go out even when you are packing orders.

Consistency in posting is more valuable than quality in any single post.

06
Treat comments and DMs as the start of a sale, not an interruption

When someone comments on your post or sends a DM about a product, they are warm. They saw your content, they felt interest, and they acted. Most sellers respond to these messages slowly, generically, or not at all — and lose buyers who were already most of the way there.

Respond to every comment and DM within an hour during operating hours. Be specific in your response — if they asked about a colour, confirm which colours are available and mention which is most popular. If they asked about delivery, give them the timeline for their region. Specificity signals that a real person is on the other side.

The WhatsApp conversation that starts with a comment or DM has a higher close rate than any cold outreach you will ever do. The buyer already expressed interest. Your job is to confirm availability, answer the specific question, and make the next step easy. 'Yes we have that in your size — want me to hold one for you?' is a closer, not an answer.

Track your message-to-order conversion rate informally. If you get 20 DMs and convert 3 to orders, your close rate is 15%. What are the 17 who did not order saying? Their objections — price, delivery time, colour not available — are your next content topics.

Every unanswered DM is a sale you gave to a seller who replied faster.

Here's how to execute everything above, week by week.

week1

List each post and its engagement: likes, comments, shares, DMs
Identify which posts got the most engagement and what they had in common
Identify which posts got zero engagement and why they failed
Rewrite the worst-performing post in buyer-problem format

week2
Plan a 4-week content calendar

Map each week: product post, proof post, educational post, lifestyle post
Identify which posts need video vs photo

2 hrs
week3
Create a week of content in one session
Shoot photos and video clips for your first week of posts
Write captions in buyer-problem format with a clear call to action
Schedule or draft-save your posts for the week
Add your WhatsApp clickable link to every post caption
3 hrs
week4
Set up your DM response system
Write 3 quick-reply templates for the most common questions
Set a personal rule: respond to every DM within 1 hour during operating hours
Turn on post notifications so you see comments immediately
Track your DM-to-order rate for the next 2 weeks

Social media does not owe you reach. You earn it by posting content people actually want to see — consistently, over time.

The sellers getting orders from social media are not doing anything technically complicated. They are consistently making content that earns attention — and then making it easy for that attention to become an order.

Rewrite your next product post in buyer-problem format. Post it. Then reply to every comment within an hour. That is the experiment. Run it 4 times before you evaluate the result.
