Starting to sell online in Sri Lanka doesn't require a shop, a warehouse, or a big investment. It requires one product, one platform, and one sale. Here's how to get there without the common first-timer mistakes.

01
Choose a winning product — not just a popular one

Products that sell well for Sri Lankan online sellers share a pattern: priced under Rs.3,000–5,000 for impulse purchases, visually appealing on a phone screen, addresses a real everyday need, and isn't easily found in local retail shops or is significantly cheaper online.

Categories that consistently perform: beauty devices and skincare tools, phone accessories, kitchen gadgets, fashion accessories, imported sarees, and handmade goods. Products in the Rs.500–2,000 range convert best through social media because buyers don't need to think hard about the purchase.

Before you source anything, validate. Post on Facebook or Instagram first: 'Would you buy this for Rs.1,500?' Read the comments. Message people who react. One weekend of social validation is worth more than Rs.50,000 of guessed inventory.

What doesn't work: Generic products already sold by dozens of Daraz sellers at lower prices. Dropshipping from China (no reliable local supplier network, COD incompatible, AliExpress budget shipping suspended July 2025). Importing expensive stock before you've made a single sale.

02
Start with ONE platform — don't spread yourself thin

First-timers who try to run Daraz, Instagram, Facebook, and a website simultaneously fail on all of them. Pick one platform based on your product type and commit to it for 60 days.

Daraz: Best for products people search for. Huge reach, trust, and COD built in. Commission 0–25% by category + 3% payment handling. Free to list. High competition on popular items.

Instagram: Best for visual, lifestyle, and fashion products. The urban young SL consumer phrase says it all: 'We buy everything on IG now.' Discovery → DM → WhatsApp → bank transfer is the standard flow.

Facebook: Best for broad reach. 15 million SL users. Selling groups (like 'Best Buy & Sell Sri Lanka') drive real volume. Older and family buyer demographic.

WhatsApp is not a platform to choose — it's the transaction layer for all social commerce. Wherever a buyer discovers your product (Facebook, Instagram), the actual sale typically happens on WhatsApp.

The Sri Lankan social commerce cycle: Facebook/Instagram for discovery → WhatsApp for transaction → Courier for fulfillment.

Pick the platform where your product looks best. Master it. Then expand.

03
Source from Pettah first — importing comes later

Pettah in Colombo is the starting point for most Sri Lankan online resellers. Different streets specialise in different products: textiles, electronics accessories, household goods, fashion. Digital platforms like Pettahhub.lk now let you access Pettah suppliers without the physical visit.

Starting capital from Pettah reselling: as low as Rs.10,000–50,000. Primary costs are stock, packaging materials, and a float for COD returns.

Once you have a proven product, importing becomes viable. From Alibaba (China): minimum Rs.100,000–500,000 including customs duty (10–15%), VAT (18%), Port and Airport Development Levy (10%), freight, and agent fees. The landed cost of Chinese goods is typically 40–70% above the ex-factory price.

India via ISFTA (India-Sri Lanka Free Trade Agreement) offers a pricing advantage over Chinese goods for many categories — textiles, garments, herbal products. Worth exploring once you know what sells.

Dubai shopping trips (Dragon Mart, wholesale markets) are used by mid-to-large resellers for fashion, jewellery, and electronics — personal duty-free allowances reduce import costs.

04
Calculate your minimum capital before you spend anything

Different selling models have very different starting costs. Know yours before you buy anything:

Pettah reselling: Rs.10,000–50,000 (stock + packaging + COD float). Lowest risk.

Handmade/homemade: Rs.5,000–30,000 (raw materials + packaging + photography). Revenue constrained by production capacity.

Importing from Alibaba: Rs.100,000–500,000. Only viable once product is proven.

Daraz seller setup: Add Rs.70,000 for a thermal label printer — the biggest upfront cost cited by Daraz sellers. Basic models are cheaper.

Hidden costs most first-timers miss: COD return float (budget 15–20% of your order volume for rejections), packaging materials, photography equipment (ring light Rs.2,000–8,000, lightbox Rs.3,000–12,000), and at least 1 month of operating costs before revenue comes in.

The number that kills new sellers: unexpected COD rejection rates of 15–30%. For every 10 orders dispatched, 2–3 may come back. Budget for it before you start.

05
Understand why dropshipping doesn't work in Sri Lanka

Dropshipping sounds ideal: no inventory, no upfront cost, just list and sell. In Sri Lanka, it faces three structural problems that make the domestic version unviable:

No reliable local supplier ecosystem. Unlike India or the US, there are no established SL wholesalers offering dropship fulfillment.

COD incompatibility. Most SL buyers demand COD. Dropshipping requires prepayment before the order is placed with the supplier. The two models are incompatible.

AliExpress budget shipping suspended July 2025. International dropshipping was already slow (2–6 weeks). Now it's also expensive.

Where dropshipping CAN work in SL: selling to international customers (diaspora) via Shopify, or a hybrid model where you keep small safety stock for popular items.

The safest approach for beginners: start with physical stock you can inspect, photograph yourself, and ship reliably. Build a COD-compatible business first. Explore dropshipping for international sales once you have a working local operation.

The sellers who succeed in SL own their stock. The sellers who fail are waiting for someone else to ship it.

06
Learn the SL seller cycle — and plug into it properly

Sri Lankan social commerce runs on a specific cycle that every successful seller understands: Discovery → WhatsApp → Courier.

Discovery happens on Facebook, Instagram, or TikTok (10.5M SL users). A product post, a Reel, or a selling group listing catches a buyer's attention.

The transaction moves to WhatsApp. Price, availability, payment, delivery address — all confirmed on WhatsApp. Bank transfer screenshot sent. Seller confirms credit in bank app (not the screenshot — the actual bank app).

Fulfillment goes through courier (Pronto, Domex, SL Post). COD collected on delivery if buyer didn't prepay.

WhatsApp Business (free) gives you professional features: product catalog, auto-replies, labels for order status, and broadcast lists for repeat customers.

Set up your WhatsApp Business account before your first sale. Add a profile photo, business description, and your top products to the catalog. Every new buyer should be added to a broadcast list for future announcements.

This cycle isn't going away. Every tool and platform you add should plug into it — not replace it.

COPY-PASTE THIS — First message to a new buyer
Hi! Thank you for your interest in [product name]. The price is Rs.[X] and delivery islandwide is Rs.[Y]. Payment by bank transfer (Sampath/BOC/Commercial Bank) or COD available. Please share your delivery address and I'll confirm your order. 😊

The sellers who grow fastest in Sri Lanka are the ones who master the cycle, not the ones who fight it.
