“A return policy that protects you and feels fair to buyers is not a contradiction — it is what keeps both sides from feeling cheated.”
“Most return requests are not fraud. They are a product description problem you have not fixed yet.”

A buyer messages: 'I want to return this.' Your stomach drops. Will they abuse the policy? Will you lose the product and the money? Will this become a Daraz dispute you cannot win? Returns feel threatening when there is no system — every one is a negotiation you handle differently and dread. The reality: a clear, written returns policy, applied consistently, makes individual return requests mechanical rather than emotional. Most legitimate return requests cost you less than you think. And most return rate problems are a content problem, not a buyer problem.

01
Write a clear returns policy and state it before purchase

An unstated returns policy is not a no-returns policy. In the absence of clear terms, every dispute defaults to the platform's buyer protection rules — which typically favour the buyer. On Daraz, a seller without a clearly stated returns policy is always at a disadvantage in a dispute.

Your returns policy does not need to be long. It needs to be clear: what can be returned, in what condition, within how many days, and what happens — refund, exchange, or store credit. Write it once, put it in your Daraz product descriptions, your WhatsApp profile, your Instagram bio link, and your standard order confirmation message.

A reasonable returns policy for a Sri Lankan online seller: unused items in original packaging, within 7 days of delivery, for exchange or store credit. Items returned because of genuine description mismatch: full refund including courier. Items returned because the buyer changed their mind: exchange or store credit, buyer pays return courier.

Buyers who see a clear policy before they order are less likely to dispute it after. The policy is not just protection — it is a trust signal. A seller with a clear, fair policy looks more professional than one with no stated terms.

A policy stated upfront is a policy you can enforce. An unstated one is a policy you are at the mercy of.

02
Separate legitimate returns from fraudulent ones

The majority of return requests are legitimate. The buyer received something different from what was described, the item arrived damaged, or the size did not match the listing. These are your responsibility and handling them well protects your reputation.

A smaller number of returns are attempts to get free products or extract refunds without returning the item. These are distinguishable from legitimate returns by specific patterns: the buyer cannot provide photos of the issue, the return request comes after the item has been used, or the described fault is inconsistent with the product type.

Your response process for any return request: ask for a photo of the issue before agreeing to anything. This single step filters most fraudulent requests. A genuine buyer with a damaged item can send you a photo immediately. A buyer who is fabricating a fault will often drop the request when asked.

Do not be aggressive in asking for evidence. Frame it professionally: 'Can you send me a photo of the item so I can understand what happened?' This is standard practice, not an accusation. Most platforms including Daraz require evidence for dispute escalation anyway.

Photos before action is the standard — not because you distrust buyers, but because it is how professional returns work.

03
Use your return rate as a content quality signal

A return rate above 5% is telling you something about your product descriptions, photos, or sizing information — not (in most cases) about your buyers. When multiple buyers return the same product with the same reason, the description is the problem.

Categorise your returns by reason. 'Not as described' returns point to photo quality or written description gaps. 'Wrong size' returns for clothing point to a missing size chart or inaccurate measurements. 'Damaged in transit' returns point to a packaging problem. Each category has a different fix.

The most actionable metric: which product has the highest return rate? Fix that listing first. Improve the photos, add measurements, clarify materials, update the description to match exactly what the buyer receives. A listing change that reduces returns from 12% to 4% on your top-selling product pays back in reduced return costs and increased conversion.

Share return reason data with your supplier too. If a product consistently returns for 'poor quality,' your supplier needs to know — either the quality standard is wrong or you need a different source.

Every return is feedback you did not ask for — and it is usually about the listing, not the buyer.

04
Handle Daraz returns before they become disputes

On Daraz, when a buyer opens a return request, the clock starts. Responding slowly or not at all automatically tilts the resolution toward the buyer. Sellers who respond within 24 hours with evidence and a clear position win the majority of legitimate disputes. Sellers who wait or ignore the request lose even when they are right.

When a Daraz return request comes in: check the reason code, check your listing against what was delivered, gather your dispatch evidence (photo of packed order is gold), and respond with your position clearly stated. If the return reason is legitimate, accept it and arrange the return courier — fighting legitimate returns on Daraz is a losing strategy that damages your metrics.

Keep a dispatch photo for every Daraz order. A photo of the sealed, labelled package before handover to courier is strong evidence in any dispute. It shows the item was dispatched in good condition and the packaging was intact at collection. This costs 10 seconds per order and wins disputes you would otherwise lose.

Know your Daraz return metrics. A high return rate on Daraz affects your seller score and ultimately your listing visibility. Handling returns well — even at short-term cost — protects your longer-term platform standing.

Responding fast and honestly on Daraz costs less than every alternative.

05
Decide your direct-channel returns approach — and stick to it

For WhatsApp and Instagram sales, you are the dispute resolution authority. There is no platform to escalate to and no protection for the buyer beyond what you offer. This is both a risk and an opportunity: you can handle returns in a way that builds loyalty rather than just processing a transaction.

A dissatisfied buyer who gets a fast, generous response on WhatsApp often becomes a repeat buyer. A dissatisfied buyer who is argued with, delayed, or ignored posts in every Facebook group they are in. The audience for how you handle a complaint is not just the buyer in front of you.

Set your direct-channel refund rule clearly in advance, not case-by-case. 'If the product is genuinely not as described or arrives damaged, I will exchange it or refund. If the buyer simply changed their mind, I will exchange for same value.' Having this written down means you do not make different decisions under pressure from different buyers.

Exchange rather than refund where possible — it keeps the revenue in your business and gives the buyer a resolution. A buyer who asks for a refund often actually wants the right product, not the money back.

Consistency in returns builds trust — arbitrary case-by-case decisions erode it.

06
Track returns by product and use the data every month

Returns tracking is not just for large operations. A simple sheet with columns for date, product, return reason, and resolution takes one minute per return to fill in. After 8 to 12 weeks you will have data that pays back in reduced return rates, better listing content, and more informed buying decisions.

The monthly review question: which product had the most returns, and what was the most common reason? That combination tells you exactly where to focus first. Fix the listing. Improve the packaging. Update the size chart. Talk to the supplier.

Track the financial cost too: for each return, note the courier cost absorbed, the value of the returned item, and whether the item could be resold. Some returned items are in perfect resaleable condition and can be relisted. Others are not — that damage needs to be in your cost calculation.

A seller who reviews their return data monthly is constantly improving. A seller who handles returns reactively but never reviews them is paying the same costs month after month without understanding why.

Returns data is product improvement data — use it as such.
