“Stock is frozen cash. COD orders are loans you gave to strangers. Courier holds are your money in someone else's bank.”

You had a good month. Orders came in, you fulfilled them all, you reordered stock. And now you have no money to run ads, no cash to buy the new product you found, and you are waiting on a Daraz settlement that is 11 days away. This is the cash flow trap — specific to online selling in Sri Lanka because of COD dominance, courier settlement delays, and the pressure to tie up cash in inventory. It is not a sign that your business is failing. It is a sign that your business is growing faster than your cash cycle.

01
Map exactly where your money is right now

Before you can fix a cash flow problem you need to know where the money is. For most online sellers it is in at least three places simultaneously: stock you have purchased but not sold, orders you have fulfilled but the courier has not settled, and Daraz or platform settlements that are pending.

Write it down right now. Stock value at cost price: LKR X. Pending courier settlements: LKR Y. Pending platform payouts: LKR Z. That is your tied-up cash. The sum usually surprises sellers the first time they calculate it.

This exercise also reveals which part of your cycle is the biggest bottleneck. If most of your money is in stock, you are overbuying relative to your sales velocity. If most of it is in courier holds, your settlement terms with your courier are working against you. If most is in Daraz pending, your payment cycle is your constraint.

Each bottleneck has a different solution. Fixing the wrong one first wastes time and still leaves you stuck.

You cannot shorten a cash cycle you have not mapped.

02
Shorten your cash cycle with prepayment incentives

COD is the default in Sri Lanka because buyers do not trust sellers they have never met. But every COD order is cash you will not see for a minimum of 5 to 10 days after dispatch — and that is assuming no rejection. Prepaid orders convert faster and pay faster.

You cannot eliminate COD, but you can incentivise buyers to choose prepay. A LKR 100 discount for bank transfer or LANKAQR payment is often enough to shift a buyer's preference — and a LKR 100 discount on a LKR 2,500 order is 4% of sale value versus the 5 to 10 days of cash float COD costs you.

LANKAQR is accepted by over 400,000 users in Sri Lanka and growing. PayHere integrates with WhatsApp order links. Both give you instant settlement confirmation — you see the money before you dispatch. That is a structural improvement to your cash cycle for every prepaid order you convert.

Run the maths for your business. If 30% of your orders shift to prepaid at a LKR 100 discount, what is the cash cycle benefit versus the discount cost? For most sellers this calculation is strongly in favour of the incentive.

A LKR 100 discount that gets your cash three weeks faster is not a cost — it is a financial decision.

03
Stop overbuying stock — smaller batches, faster turns

The temptation to buy in bulk when a good product is working is completely understandable. Bulk buys get better prices. But bulk buys also tie up cash for weeks or months at a time — and if the product's demand softens while your stock is sitting, you have expensive inventory that is not converting to cash.

Calculate your stock turn rate: how many days does it take from when you receive stock to when it is all sold and the cash is in your account? For fast-moving fashion or beauty items this might be 21 days. For seasonal or trend-driven products it could be 90. The longer your turn, the less cash you have available for anything else.

The alternative to large bulk buys is smaller, more frequent orders at slightly higher per-unit cost but significantly lower cash commitment per cycle. The per-unit cost difference is almost always smaller than the opportunity cost of cash you could not deploy because it was locked in inventory.

Test this: next reorder, buy 40% of what you would normally buy. Reorder faster when you hit reorder point. After two cycles, compare your available cash position and your total profit. For most sellers, the smaller batch model frees up cash without reducing profitability.

The best inventory size is the smallest one that keeps you in stock.

04
Understand your Daraz payment cycle exactly

Daraz pays sellers on a cycle that varies by account tier and selling history. New sellers often have longer settlement cycles. Established sellers with good metrics can access faster payouts. Most sellers do not know exactly when they get paid — they just wait and hope.

Log into your Daraz Seller Centre and check your payment schedule. Find the exact settlement day — it is specific, not approximate. Map it against your typical order-to-delivery timeline. This tells you the maximum time from sale to cash receipt.

With this number you can plan your cash position: if Daraz settles on the 15th and 30th and today is the 5th, you know your next cash injection is 10 days away. Do not spend cash you do not have today expecting Daraz to cover it on the 15th — check the pending settlement amount first.

If your Daraz settlement cycle is creating persistent cash gaps, explore Daraz's early payout programmes if available, or use the period just after settlement to restock rather than the period just before — when you are most cash-constrained.

A payment cycle you know is a constraint you can plan around. One you guess at will surprise you every time.

05
Build a two-week cash buffer before spending on growth

A cash buffer is a minimum balance in your business account that you treat as untouchable for operating expenses. It covers your next two weeks of stock purchases, courier charges, and fixed costs regardless of what happens with settlements and incoming orders.

Without a buffer, any disruption — a slow week, a delayed settlement, an unexpected return wave — immediately hits your ability to operate. With a buffer, you absorb the disruption without changing your behaviour.

Calculate your typical two-week operating cost: stock reorders, courier charges, packaging supplies, platform fees, any ads. That is your buffer target. Build it gradually by holding back 10 to 15% of each settlement until you reach it. Once you have it, do not touch it except for genuine emergencies.

The buffer is also what allows you to act on good opportunities. A supplier offers a good deal but you need cash in 3 days. A seasonal peak is approaching and you need to restock ahead of it. A buffer means you can move without waiting for a settlement that is 9 days away.

A two-week cash buffer is the difference between a business that reacts and one that acts.

06
Invoice wholesale buyers on day one — not whenever you remember

If you supply to retailers or take wholesale orders, your invoicing discipline directly determines your cash cycle. Every day between delivery and invoice is a day of free credit you are extending to someone else. Every day between invoice and payment is additional credit.

Invoice the same day you deliver or dispatch. Set payment terms in writing before the first delivery: 7 days, 14 days, or 30 days. Follow up on day 8 if unpaid. Follow up again on day 12. Most late payments are not deliberate — they are responded to in the order they are chased.

For WhatsApp-based wholesale buyers, a simple voice note or text saying 'Payment due for last week's order — total is LKR X, please transfer to 07X XXXXXXX or PayHere link below' is sufficient. It is not aggressive. It is how businesses operate.

Track your wholesale receivables separately. If you have three wholesale accounts and two of them consistently pay 3 weeks late, that is a cash flow problem that will compound as your wholesale volume grows. Either tighten terms or stop extending credit to chronic late payers.

Chasing money you are already owed is not awkward — it is the minimum acceptable standard for running a business.

Your 4-week plan

Here's how to execute everything above, week by week.

week1
Map your cash position today
Calculate stock at cost: how much cash is sitting in inventory right now
Check pending courier settlements: what is owed and when it arrives
Check Daraz pending: total and expected settlement date
Total these three — this is your tied-up cash figure
2 hrs
week2
Set up a prepay incentive
Decide your discount or incentive for prepaid orders (suggest LKR 100-200)
Activate LANKAQR or PayHere if not already done
Add 'Pay now and save LKR X' to your standard order message
Test it on 10 orders and track how many convert
3 hrs
week3
Calculate your two-week buffer target
Add up your typical two-week operating costs
Check your current business account balance vs this target
If below, decide the percentage of each settlement to hold back
Set a target date to reach your buffer
1 hr
week4
Review your next stock reorder size
Calculate current stock turn rate for your top product
Determine if the next order can be 30-40% smaller without going out of stock
Model the cash freed up from the smaller order
Place the smaller order and track how it affects your cash position
2 hrs
After 4 weeks, you'll have:
✓
A clear picture of where your money is and when it is coming back
✓
At least one prepaid payment option active with a live incentive
✓
A two-week cash buffer goal with a plan to reach it
✓
A stock ordering discipline that frees up cash without running out
✓
A wholesale invoicing habit that does not leave money sitting unpaid
Cash flow is a system problem, not a money problem. The money is usually there — it is just in the wrong place at the wrong time.

Most sellers feel the cash flow squeeze and blame bad luck or a slow month. The reality is almost always structural — COD delays, courier holds, bulk buying habits, and no buffer. Every one of those is fixable.

Start by mapping where your money is. Right now. Before the next order, the next settlement, the next thing. The map is the beginning of the fix.
