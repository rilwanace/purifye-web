Sri Lankan online selling is seasonal by design — Avurudu, 11.11, Christmas, and Valentine's Day drive disproportionate volume. Sellers who plan for these peaks 3 weeks ahead, capture buyers' WhatsApp numbers during the rush, and build baseline habits between campaigns stop riding the rollercoaster.

01
Know the SL e-commerce calendar — it's more predictable than you think

November — 11.11 (Daraz's biggest event): 53% of Daraz 11.11 first-hour buyers come from outside Western Province. Home appliances saw 4x GMV uplift. Over 200 sellers used Daraz Marketing Solutions Ads. This is the single biggest online selling event in the SL calendar. Prepare 3 weeks ahead.

April — Avurudu: Fashion, gifts, and food categories dominate. Social media buying spikes as families shop for new clothes and traditional gifts. Start content and stock preparation in late February.

December — Christmas: Gift categories, electronics, and premium items. Kapruka dominates diaspora gifting but individual sellers can capture local demand through social commerce.

Secondary peaks: Valentine's Day (February), Mother's Day (May), Back to School (January), Black Friday (November, adjacent to 11.11).

For each peak: stock arrives 2 weeks before. Content starts 10 days before. Ad campaigns go live 7 days before. WhatsApp broadcasts every 2 days during peak week.

The sellers who capture Avurudu buyers in April and keep them active through May are the ones who show up in the January baseline statistics.

02
Prepare stock and content 3 weeks before each peak

Week 3 before peak: Stock ordered and en route. Product photos taken. Content calendar drafted for peak week (what to post each day).

Week 2 before peak: Stock received and quality-checked. First teaser posts go live ('Something exciting coming for Avurudu — stay tuned'). WhatsApp broadcast to existing customers announcing the upcoming promotion.

Week 1 before peak: Campaign content live — product showcases, price reveals, urgency posts ('Only 20 left!'). Daraz listings updated with sale pricing. Facebook selling group posts daily.

What most sellers do wrong: They think about Avurudu in the first week of April. By then, the sellers who prepared in February are already getting their first wave of orders.

Create a recurring calendar reminder: 3 weeks before each peak, run your preparation checklist. This one habit puts you 3 weeks ahead of the reactive sellers every single time.

The sellers who dominate Avurudu prepared for it when everyone else was thinking about January.

03
Capture WhatsApp numbers during peak — retain buyers after it

The most expensive mistake of seasonal selling: letting peak buyers walk away with no way to reach them again. Every sale during 11.11 or Avurudu is an opportunity to add a loyal customer to your broadcast list — or let them disappear forever.

Capture tactic: inside every package dispatched during peak season, include a note: 'Thank you for your order! Message us on WhatsApp for early access to our next sale and new arrivals.' Include your number prominently.

WhatsApp follow-up sequence after peak season:

Day 3 after delivery: 'How did your [product] arrive? Any questions?'

Day 14: 'We just got new stock of [related product]. Here's a photo — would you like details?'

Day 30: 'Our next big sale is coming in [month]. I'm reserving early access for existing customers. Reply to be on the list.'

This 3-message sequence turns a seasonal buyer into a repeat customer for the next peak and every sale in between.

04
Run Daraz Marketing Solutions during 11.11

Over 200 sellers used Daraz Marketing Solutions Ads during 11.11 2024. This is Daraz's pay-per-click system within its own search — products that are already converting organically show up higher in search results during campaign periods.

How it works: sellers bid on keywords relevant to their product category. Sponsored listings appear above organic results. You pay only when a buyer clicks. Effective budget: varies by category, but Rs.5,000–15,000 for a small campaign during 11.11 is a reasonable starting test.

Participation in Daraz campaigns (like 11.11, 7.7, and Avurudu sales) gives listings a promotional badge that increases visibility. To participate: enroll via Seller Center before the campaign enrollment deadline (typically 3–4 weeks before the event).

For the 11.11 specifically: enroll your best-performing products, set competitive prices (platform tracks price history — deep cuts get better placement), and ensure stock is available. Running out of stock mid-campaign is a ranking penalty.

05
Build baseline sales habits between peaks

Sellers who rely only on campaign peaks never escape the revenue rollercoaster. The goal is to build a floor of baseline sales — consistent enough that January doesn't feel catastrophic after a great November.

Baseline habits: Post consistently 3–5 times per week even when there's no campaign. Send a WhatsApp broadcast every 10–14 days to your list. List new products or restock popular ones every 2–3 weeks to signal freshness to algorithms.

Categories that have stable demand year-round in SL: phone accessories (phones break, accessories wear out), skincare basics (daily use), kitchen gadgets (household need), stationery (students buy year-round). If your product is purely gift-oriented, consider adding an everyday-use product to smooth the revenue curve.

Set a monthly revenue floor target. 'I will make at least Rs.50,000 per month from baseline sales' forces you to build habits that survive outside campaign periods.

Campaign revenue is exciting. Baseline revenue is what keeps your business alive in February.

06
Prepare for post-peak returns and complaints proactively

Every peak season brings a wave of returns and complaints 7–14 days later. Orders arrive late. Products don't match expectations set during the promotional rush. COD rejections spike as buyers who impulse-ordered change their minds.

This wave will happen. Prepare for it: increase stock reserves of packaging for replacements, draft your return/refund response templates in advance, and allocate 2 hours per day post-peak to customer service.

Post-peak customer service protocol: For delivery complaints, check the courier tracking before responding. For quality complaints, ask for a photo. For refund requests, confirm receipt of the product back before issuing refund. For COD rejections that return, check the item is resaleable before relisting.

Respond to every complaint within 24 hours — even if it's just 'I've received your message and I'm looking into this.' Radio silence after a problem is the fastest way to lose a potential repeat customer permanently.

COPY-PASTE THIS — Post-peak complaint acknowledgment
Hi [Name], I'm sorry to hear about this issue. I've received your message and I'm looking into it now. I'll come back to you with a solution by [specific time tomorrow]. Thank you for your patience.

How you handle the post-peak mess tells your buyers more about your business than the peak promotion ever did.
