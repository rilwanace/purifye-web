A website makes sense at a specific point in an online seller's journey — not at the beginning. Here's how to know if you're there, what it costs, and what to set up if you decide to proceed.

01
Validate on Daraz or social first — website comes later

The consensus among Sri Lankan e-commerce practitioners is clear: validate your product and pricing on Daraz or social media first, then build a website for brand-building at higher volumes.

A website without existing traffic is an expensive shop on a street that nobody walks down. Daraz has millions of monthly visitors. Instagram has 8.5 million SL users. Your website has zero unless you build traffic from scratch.

When a website starts to make sense: you're consistently doing 30+ orders/day across social channels, you have repeat customers who would bookmark a product page, you want to reduce platform dependency and commission costs, or you're building a recognisable brand (not just reselling generic products).

Before building: do you have a working Facebook/Instagram following? Do you get repeat buyers asking if you have a website? Have you proven your product sells without the platform's built-in trust and search?

A website is a destination. Build the audience first, then give them somewhere to go.

02
Shopify vs WooCommerce — the real SL comparison

Shopify: USD 29/month (approx Rs.8,700). No coding required. Shopify Payments is NOT available in Sri Lanka — you need a third-party gateway (PayHere, WebXPay, OnePay). Easiest to set up. Best for sellers who want to focus on selling, not tech.

WooCommerce (WordPress): Hosting costs Rs.1,500–5,000/month for basic shared hosting. More flexible but requires more technical setup. PayHere has a native WooCommerce plugin. More popular among tech-savvy sellers and those with existing WordPress sites.

Payment gateways for SL: PayHere is the most popular — no setup fee, 2.5–3.9% per transaction, supports Visa/MC/Amex/Genie/eZCash/FriMi/mCash. WebXPay and OnePay are alternatives.

Total monthly website cost for a small seller: Shopify Rs.8,700 + PayHere transaction fees (~3%) + your time. For 50 orders/month at Rs.2,000 AOV = Rs.100,000 revenue, Daraz commission at 15% = Rs.15,000. Website costs: Rs.8,700 + Rs.3,000 fees = Rs.11,700. You'd save ~Rs.3,000/month at this volume — marginal until orders scale significantly.

03
The traffic problem — websites don't bring their own visitors

This is the most underestimated challenge of building an online store in Sri Lanka. Daraz brings buyers to you. Instagram's algorithm shows your posts to people who might be interested. Facebook's reach gives your posts organic distribution. Your website does none of this.

To drive traffic to your website, you need: SEO (months of effort before results), paid ads (ongoing cost, needs working capital), your social media profile pointing to the website (requires an existing following), or your existing WhatsApp list (requires a warm audience).

Realistic website traffic at launch: Zero. The sellers who successfully run their own website already had a social media following or a Daraz review history that established trust before launching the website.

If you do build a website: run all social media traffic to it. Put your website URL in your Instagram bio, Facebook page, WhatsApp Business profile, and every delivery package. Build the habit of sending potential buyers to the website, not to a platform.

A website is only as good as the traffic you send to it. Build the traffic first — on platforms that already have people.

04
Website design — what you need and what you don't

What every SL online seller's website needs: clear product photos, price in LKR, PayHere payment button, delivery information, contact details (WhatsApp number), and basic product descriptions.

What you don't need on day one: custom animations, 10-page navigation, multiple currencies, complex discount logic, a blog, or integration with 15 shipping partners.

Minimum viable e-commerce store: 5–10 product listings, a payment gateway, a clear shipping policy, and a WhatsApp contact. A Shopify store set up in 1 weekend can handle 50 orders/month if the products and traffic are there.

Website development quotes in SL range from Rs.15,000 (basic Shopify setup) to Rs.500,000+ (custom-built). For most online sellers, a self-set-up Shopify store or a Rs.50,000–80,000 basic WooCommerce setup is the right starting point. Don't overbuild.

05
When a website unlocks real value

Three specific scenarios where a website dramatically changes the business:

1. Repeat customer subscription or loyalty program: A website lets you offer accounts, order history, and loyalty points — impossible on Daraz or social commerce.

2. International selling: Kapruka serves diaspora. Amazon Handmade (no monthly fee for approved sellers, 15% referral fee) serves international artisans. A website with international shipping options and Wise/Payoneer integration opens export channels that Daraz can't.

3. Wholesale and B2B: If you're getting bulk inquiries from other retailers, a website with a wholesale inquiry form or a B2B pricing page gives them a professional way to engage.

For branded handmade products, own-brand imports, or products that benefit from storytelling and content marketing, a website is genuinely worth the investment. For generic resellers competing on price, the commission savings rarely justify the traffic acquisition cost.

The question isn't 'should I build a website' — it's 'do I have a product and a story worth building a website for?'

A website is a brand asset. If you're not building a brand, you're building a shop that nobody walks into.

06
SEO — the long-term website strategy

Search engine optimisation means your website shows up when Sri Lankan buyers search Google for your product. It's free traffic, but it takes 3–6 months of consistent effort before results appear.

Basic SL SEO for product pages: Title tag with product name + 'Sri Lanka' + 'buy online'. Product description with keywords buyers actually use (check Daraz search terms for your category). Site speed matters — Shopify is generally faster than shared hosting WooCommerce.

If you sell handmade natural soaps, rank for 'natural handmade soap Sri Lanka' and 'organic soap Colombo'. If you sell imported sarees, rank for 'Indian sarees Sri Lanka' and 'sarees online Sri Lanka'.

Realistic expectation: 3–6 months to rank for medium-competition terms. Google Search Console (free) shows you which searches bring buyers to your site. Write one new product description or blog post per month at minimum.

SEO is the compound interest of website marketing. Zero results for months, then a growing stream of free visitors indefinitely.

Every month you delay starting SEO is another month before free traffic begins. Start today, even with one optimised product page.
