Printing is the most visible cost in a tuition teacher's budget — and the easiest to reduce significantly without changing how you teach.

01
Switch to WhatsApp PDF distribution — not gradually

Create a WhatsApp group for each batch. Every Sunday, send the week's materials as a single PDF. Students open the PDF on their phone during class, or screenshot the pages they need.

That's a 90% reduction in printing overnight — without removing any content.

Most O/L and A/L students have smartphones. For the 1–2 students in a batch who don't, print only their copies. The rest is digital.

02
MCQ practice tests: less printing and less marking

A 20-question MCQ practice test can be peer-corrected in class by exchanging papers and reading answers aloud. No collection, no marking pile to take home. 5 minutes.

You're not compromising quality. You're reducing printing and marking simultaneously while aligning to actual exam format.

Design your regular practice tests in MCQ format where the subject allows. O/L and A/L exam papers already include MCQ sections — your practice tests should match.

You're not compromising quality. You're reducing printing and marking simultaneously.

03
Negotiate bulk printing prices — most teachers don't bother

If you're spending LKR 5,000–8,000/month at the same printing shop, ask for a bulk rate. Shops that handle regular high-volume customers will negotiate.

The shop nearest your hall is probably not the cheapest. It's just the easiest habit.

Alternatively, find the cheapest per-page rate in your area — not just the most convenient shop. Two minutes of research could save LKR 1,500–2,000 per month.

04
Print only what students actually use

Track which materials your students reference versus which ones end up in the bottom of a bag after class. In most classes: the model paper gets used, the notes get lost, the summary sheet stays at home.

Targeted printing costs 30–40% of blanket printing — for materials that actually reach students.

Print model papers. Send notes digitally. Stop printing things students lose.

05
Create a reusable past paper pack — print once, reuse for years

The O/L mathematics syllabus doesn't change annually. Compile a comprehensive 5-year past paper booklet once. Print 30 copies. Collect them at the end of each term.

The student treats it differently too — a bound booklet with their name on it gets kept. A loose sheet gets lost.

A reusable pack printed at the start of a 2-year O/L preparation run has a per-student, per-year cost that's a fraction of printing new materials monthly.

Your 4-week plan

Here's how to execute everything above, week by week.

week1
Audit what you print
List every material you printed last month — Notes, papers, worksheets, receipts — rough page count and unit cost
Separate one-time prints from recurring ones — One-off exam papers vs notes reprinted every intake — different fixes
Calculate your monthly printing spend — At LKR 3–4 per page — the number usually surprises teachers
~1 hour
week2
Send the first PDFs
Convert 2 handouts to PDF and share via WhatsApp — Your most-printed item first — notes or a past paper
Track how many students acknowledge receipt — Most will — those who don't reveal who actually needs paper
Print only for students who explicitly ask — Data-gathering week — note what percentage need paper
~1 hour
week3
Build the reuse library
Compile your last 3 exam papers into a question bank — Sort by topic and difficulty — draw from this, don't create from scratch
Create a digital folder structure — One folder per class/subject/year — papers, notes, and keys together
Use the monthly printing cost tracker — Track this month's spend now as your baseline
~2 hours
week4
Measure the saving
Calculate this month's printing spend vs last month — Even 30% reduction in month 1 = LKR 1,800–2,700 back
Use the digital vs print decision matrix — Run each remaining printed item through it — notes, papers, worksheets
Set a monthly printing budget ceiling — A number you commit to not exceeding — track it monthly
~1 hour
After 4 weeks, you'll have:
✓
Materials sent as PDFs to at least one batch WhatsApp group, Sunday evening, single file
✓
A question bank compiled for 2+ topics organized by topic, reusable for future tests
✓
A bulk printing rate negotiated or the cheapest local shop identified
✓
A monthly printing budget set with a target to cut by 30% over the next term
You know exactly what you're spending on printing — and you've started cutting it.

Printing is one of those costs that grows quietly. Not because any single decision was wrong — a model paper here, a worksheet there — but because "I'll just print it this time" becomes the default for every material decision. The teachers who cut their printing costs didn't become less generous. They just stopped printing things that could be sent on a phone screen.

Look at what you printed last week. Pick one item that could have been a WhatsApp PDF instead. Switch it for one class this week.
