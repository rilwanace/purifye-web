“January was good. April feels empty. I don't know what changed — I'm teaching the same students.”
“I know money is coming in but I never seem to have any. Something is going wrong somewhere.”
“Someone asked me what my profit margin is. I had no answer. I don't even know what that means for my class.”

Independent tuition teachers should have some of the highest profit margins of any profession. Most don't know their number — and that's the only reason they don't.

01
Track every expense for one month — just one

Hall rent, printing, phone data, transport, stationery. Write down everything you spend related to your tuition business for exactly one month.

The month of tracking isn't about being perfect. It's about seeing reality.

Most teachers who do this find 2–3 expenses they'd forgotten existed and 1–2 that are significantly higher than they thought.

The month of tracking isn't about being perfect. It's about seeing reality.

02
Categorize your costs — four buckets is enough

Venue (rent, utilities). Materials (printing, stationery, data). Transport (travel to halls, petrol). Admin (phone costs, any software).

Most teachers who do this exercise find their costs are concentrated in one or two categories, not spread evenly.

Once you can name the four categories, you can see which one is the problem. Usually it's venue or materials — and both are reducible.

Once you can name which bucket is the problem, cutting is obvious.

03
Find your hidden waste: cash float losses and venue downtime

Cash you collected and can't account for. Hours you paid hall rent for and didn't teach.

Tracking makes these visible. What's visible can be fixed.

In a cash-only system, small discrepancies accumulate. A student you forgot to chase for 3 months is LKR 9,000 gone. A hall rented on a day you cancelled class is LKR 1,000 wasted.

Tracking makes the invisible visible. What's visible can be fixed.

04
Calculate your actual profit margin — most teachers have never done it

Monthly students × monthly fee = gross revenue. Subtract all expenses. What's left is your net income.

The teachers who grow fastest aren't the ones with the most students. They're the ones who know their numbers.

At 85%+ margins, independent tuition is an extremely profitable profession — if you track it.

05
Tax preparation is your forcing function for financial clarity

The annual income tax filing deadline is November 30. Declaring tuition income requires you to gather all income records and list deductible expenses. Even if you're below the LKR 1.2 million personal relief threshold, this process forces you to know your numbers.

Regulation is tightening. The teachers who already track their finances will barely notice when licensing and formal requirements arrive.

Teachers who do their taxes know their numbers. Teachers who don't, don't.

Your 4-week plan

Here's how to execute everything above, week by week.

week1
Build the income record
List every student and their monthly fee — Name, amount, class — and whether they've paid this month
Total your monthly income figure — The actual number — what actually came in last month
Note the gap between expected and received — Usually 2–3 students account for most of the collection problem
~1.5 hours
week2
List every cost
Write down every fixed cost — Hall rent, Zoom Pro, software, phone plan — recurring regardless of student count
Write down every variable cost — Printing, transport, teaching materials — costs that scale with activity
Separate personal spending from business costs — Even informally — teaching money vs personal crossover
~1 hour
week3
Calculate the real number
Calculate profit = income − costs — Write this number down — usually higher than expected when actually calculated
Calculate your margin percentage — Profit ÷ income × 100. Compare to 85% benchmark for solo tuition
Identify your single largest cost — Hall rent is almost always #1 — is it delivering proportional value?
~1 hour
week4
Run the monthly review
Block 15 min on the first Sunday of every month — The only financial habit that matters — everything else is one-time setup
Review the same 5 numbers each month — Students paying, income received, total costs, profit, one cost you're watching
Identify one cost to reduce or cut next month — LKR 1,000/month saved = LKR 12,000/year — small cuts compound
~30 min
After 4 weeks, you'll have:
✓
One month of expenses tracked in full every rupee, every category
✓
Costs sorted into 4 buckets venue, materials, transport, other
✓
Actual profit margin calculated gross revenue minus all costs
✓
The biggest cost category identified and at least one reduction action taken
You have a complete picture of monthly income, costs, and margin.

Most teachers who feel like their money is disappearing are right — not because they're spending badly, but because teaching income is irregular, costs are mixed with personal spending, and nobody ever sat down to look at the whole picture. Doing that calculation once, for real, is usually enough to change how you handle money for years.

This weekend, write down your income for last month. Just the number. Then write down every cost you can remember. You'll have a rough picture in under 30 minutes — and the picture alone changes how the rest of the month feels.
