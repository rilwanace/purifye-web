“I've been thinking about starting tuition classes for months. I just don't know where to begin.”
“I'm a good teacher — my students at school do well. But running my own class feels like a completely different thing.”
“What if nobody shows up? What if I set the wrong price? What if I can't handle the admin on top of everything else?”

Starting a tuition class is simpler than it feels. The teachers who succeed aren't the ones who planned everything perfectly — they're the ones who started with 5 students and figured the rest out as they went. Here's your day-one checklist.

01
Pick your subject, level, and batch size before anything else

Don't try to teach everything. Pick the one subject you're strongest in, at the level where demand is highest. O/L Mathematics, O/L Science, and A/L subjects have the most consistent demand in Sri Lanka. Start with one batch of 10–15 students, one day per week. That's manageable alongside a full-time job.

The teachers who try to teach 4 subjects from day one burn out in 3 months. The ones who start with one subject and do it well are still teaching 5 years later.

Write down: "I will teach [subject] for [level] students, [day] from [time] to [time], maximum [number] students."

The teachers who try to teach 4 subjects from day one burn out in 3 months. The ones who start with one subject and do it well are still teaching 5 years later.

02
Set your price by checking what teachers near you charge

Ask 3–4 parents what they currently pay for tuition in your subject and area. Check Iskolaya.lk and Panthi.lk listings for comparable teachers. Price yourself at or slightly below the area average to start — you can increase once you have results to show. Typical ranges: LKR 1,500–2,500 for O/L group classes outside Colombo, LKR 2,500–4,000 in Colombo suburbs, LKR 3,500–5,000 for A/L subjects.

Your starting price should be competitive enough that price isn't the reason a parent says no. Results will justify increases later.

The wrong price isn't the one that's too low — it's the one you never set because you kept overthinking it.

03
Find your first 5 students before you rent a hall

Start at home. Your dining table, a whiteboard, and 5 students is a tuition class. Tell family, neighbours, colleagues at school, and parents you know. Post on your personal Facebook — not a page, your personal profile. "I'm starting O/L maths tuition on Saturdays. Small group, maximum 10 students. First class is free — message me if interested." Your first students will come from people who already trust you.

Message 10 people today who might know parents looking for tuition. Not a broadcast — personal messages.

Every teacher with 200 students started with 5 who came because someone they trusted said this teacher is good.

04
Get your basics ready — it's less than you think

You need: a whiteboard (LKR 3,000–5,000), markers, a printed fee policy (use our template), an attendance register, and a WhatsApp group for the batch. That's it. You don't need a hall, you don't need software, you don't need a logo, you don't need business cards. Those come later when you have 20+ students and actual revenue.

Total startup cost: under LKR 10,000. If you already have a whiteboard, under LKR 2,000.

The biggest barrier to starting isn't money or equipment. It's the belief that you need more of both than you actually do.

05
Run your first class — then follow up

Your first class is your audition. Pick a topic you teach exceptionally well. Make it interactive — not a lecture. At the end, hand parents a one-page fee policy with your schedule, fee, and contact number. Collect WhatsApp numbers. Two days later, message every parent: "Thank you for bringing [child name] to the class. I have [X] spots left in the Saturday batch. Would you like to reserve one?"

The follow-up message within 2 days is what converts a trial into a student. Don't wait a week.

Your first class won't be perfect. It doesn't need to be. It needs to happen.

06
Set up the system from day one — not after it gets messy

From your very first student: track attendance, record test scores, track fee payments, send parents a monthly update. These habits take 30 minutes per week when you have 5 students. If you wait until you have 50 students to start tracking, you'll spend a full weekend trying to reconstruct 6 months of records. Start clean, stay clean.

Set up the fee tracker spreadsheet and attendance register before your first class, not after.

The teachers who drown in admin at 100 students are the ones who didn't build systems at 5. The ones who breeze through it started tracking from day one.

Your 4-week plan

Here's how to execute everything above, week by week.

week1
Make the decision
Write down your subject, level, batch day/time, and maximum class size — Commit it to paper — decision made, not still pending
Set your price — Check 3 comparable teachers in your area on Iskolaya or Panthi
Message 10 people who might know interested parents — Personal messages, not a broadcast — people who already trust you
~2 hours
week2
Get ready
Buy or set up a whiteboard — LKR 3,000-5,000 new, or borrow one — your only required equipment
Print your fee policy and set up a fee tracker — One page: due date, amount, late policy — use our template
Create a WhatsApp group for your first batch — Even before you have students — name it and set it up now
~1.5 hours
week3
Open the door
Announce on your personal Facebook — One honest post: subject, level, day, free first class — message me if interested
Run your first free trial class — Pick your best topic and make it interactive, not a lecture
Collect WhatsApp numbers from every parent who attends — Hand each parent your one-page fee policy at the end
~1 hour
week4
Convert and commit
Follow up with every trial parent within 2 days — Thank you for bringing [name]. I have [X] spots left. Would you like to reserve one?
Confirm your first paying students — Collect first month fee before or at the second class
Send your first monthly update to parents — Even if it is just: welcome, here is what we will cover this month
~1 hour
After 4 weeks, you'll have:
✓
A subject, level, and schedule locked in
✓
A price set based on real market data
✓
Your first batch of students confirmed and paying
✓
Fee tracking and attendance running from day one
✓
A WhatsApp group active with parents
You're no longer thinking about starting. You've started.

The difference between a teacher who runs tuition and one who has been thinking about it isn't talent, confidence, or a business plan. It's a Saturday afternoon and 5 students.

Message one person today. Not 10 — one. Ask if they know any parents looking for tuition. That single message is the start of everything on this page.
