Most parent messages are asking questions you haven't answered yet. Close the information gaps proactively and the volume drops — without ignoring anyone.

01
Broadcast lists, not group messages — the key difference

A WhatsApp broadcast sends the same message to many people but lands in each parent's personal chat as if you sent it directly to them. There's no group thread, no notifications from other parents, no side conversations you have to manage.

WhatsApp groups breed questions. Broadcast lists breed silence. Silence is what you want.

Set up one broadcast list per batch. For schedule changes, fee reminders, and announcements — one message, sent once, reaches everyone.

The difference: a group message starts a thread. A broadcast ends one. Switch your class communications to broadcast lists and the noise drops immediately.

WhatsApp groups breed questions. Broadcast lists breed silence. Silence is what you want.

02
Set response windows — and tell parents about them

"I reply to messages between 9–10am and 7–8pm." Write that in your enrollment information. Put it in your class rules document.

This isn't rude. It's professional. And once it's set as an expectation from day one, parents adapt to it instantly.

Most parents don't need immediate replies — they need to know you'll reply. A note that sets response windows is the same as a doctor's receptionist saying "the doctor will call you back this afternoon."

03
Create a parent FAQ that pre-answers 80% of questions

The 5 most common questions parents ask you are probably: what time is class, how do I pay, what happens if my child misses class, how will I know how they're doing, and what's your number.

These answers stop at least 60% of the messages before they ever start.

Write the answers once, put them on one page, and give it to every parent on the day they enroll. For current parents who've never had this — send it now.

These answers stop at least 60% of the messages before they ever start.

04
Build template replies for your 5 most-asked questions

Save the text for your 5 most common replies in your phone's saved messages. When the same question arrives, 2 taps — done.

The message for "what time is Saturday class?" already exists. Stop writing it from scratch.

This doesn't feel like a big deal until you realize you've been typing the same 30-word message 8 times a week for 3 years.

Copy-paste this

Hi! The Saturday batch is at 10am at [address, near landmark]. Fee for this month is LKR [amount], due by the 1st. You can pay by cash at class or scan the QR code on the whiteboard.

05
Proactive updates prevent most questions before they're asked

A parent who received a monthly progress update last week won't message asking "how is my child doing?" A parent who got a fee reminder on the 25th won't message on the 2nd to ask if they owe anything.

One proactive update per month eliminates the most common reactive questions.

Most parent messages are filling information vacuums you left. Close the vacuum, close the messages.

06
Put class info in your WhatsApp status — once, visible to all

A simple image saved to your WhatsApp status: class times, fee, your number, payment info. Update it once per term.

One image. Zero messages. Takes 10 minutes to create.

Parents who see it in a status update can screenshot and have everything they need — without messaging you.

Your 4-week plan

Here's how to execute everything above, week by week.

week1
Create the broadcast
Build your WhatsApp broadcast list — Add every parent — test message to confirm all receive it
Send the first batch announcement — Class schedule, fee due date, contact hours, upcoming topics
Send the response window announcement — "I respond 6–7pm and 9–10pm weekdays. Urgent: call directly"
~1 hour
week2
Set the boundaries
Create 5 saved reply shortcuts — Fee confirmation, schedule reminder, result notification, absence, general enquiry
Block your two response windows in calendar — Set as recurring — trains your behaviour as much as parents'
Batch all pending messages into the evening window — Don't reply during teaching hours — queue and batch-respond at 6pm
~1 hour
week3
Build the FAQ
Write the 5-question FAQ — Class times, fees, urgent contact — write all answers once
Send the FAQ to all parents via broadcast — "Here are answers to the questions I get asked most often"
Send the monthly parent update to all classes — Attendance summary, topic progress, what's coming next month
~1.5 hours
week4
Run a clean week
Note the reactive message count vs Week 1 — Messages outside your response windows — the number should be dropping
Add any new questions to the FAQ — Any question asked 3+ times this week gets added now
Review the broadcast for next month — What do parents need before next term? Draft it now
~30 min

Most parent communication problems are actually boundary problems. The messages feel unmanageable because there's no structure — they arrive at any hour, about anything, with the expectation of immediate response. The strategies above don't reduce communication. They redirect it into a shape you can actually manage.

Set two response windows tonight. Choose two 45-minute slots. Put them in your calendar as recurring events. That single change alone will make this week different from last week.
