Strategies›Education & Tuition›Automate My Operations›I spend too much time on admin, not teaching
“I spent Sunday evening updating attendance records. Sunday evening. That's not why I became a teacher.”
“I calculated it once — I spend almost as many hours on admin as I do in front of students.”
“I'm burning out. Not from teaching — from everything around teaching.”

Admin doesn't have to be your second job. Once you can see exactly where the hours go, most of it is fixable in under a week.

01
Track your time for one week before you change anything

Get a notebook and for one week, log every hour: teaching, marking, fee collection, printing, parent messages, scheduling, travel. Do it honestly.

You can't fix what you haven't measured. And the measurement itself shows you exactly where to attack first.

Most teachers who do this are shocked. Teaching is usually 12–20 hours. Admin is 8–12. Add marking and prep and you're at 28–40 hours a week — for what most people assume is a part-time job.

02
Apply the "what only I can do" filter

Teaching requires your expertise. Collecting cash, recording attendance, printing handouts, replying to "what time is class?" — these don't.

Most teachers find the second list is 50–60% of their working hours.

Make two lists: what only you can do vs. what could be done by a system, a template, or a part-time helper. Everything on the second list is a candidate for automation or delegation.

Most teachers find the second list is 50–60% of their working hours.

03
Batch your parent communications into two windows

Replying to parent messages as they arrive throughout the day is one of the biggest hidden productivity killers. Set two windows per day — 9am and 7pm — for all parent communications.

Outside those windows, WhatsApp stays closed during teaching and prep time.

Most parents don't expect an instant reply. They expect a reply. Those are very different things.

04
Systematize fee collection once and stop thinking about it

Set up the 3-message reminder sequence (pre-due, due, 7-day overdue) once. Every month, you run the sequence. The messages don't change — only the month name does.

One afternoon of setup recovers 2+ hours per week for the rest of the year.

This converts a 2–4 hour weekly process into a 10-minute monthly one.

One afternoon of setup recovers 2+ hours per week for the rest of the year.

05
Switch materials to digital distribution

If you're printing 4 pages per student per week for 60 students, that's 240 pages a week — roughly LKR 600–900 in printing and 2–3 hours in printing logistics.

Students screenshot what they need, or open the PDF on their phone during class. The transition takes one term to feel normal.

Create a WhatsApp group for each batch. Send the week's materials as a single PDF every Sunday evening. 15 minutes instead of hours of printing and collating.

Printing for 60 students is 5 hours of work per month. Sending a PDF takes 2 minutes.

06
Hire admin help when you cross 100 students

Below 100 students, automation handles most of the admin overhead without the cost of a person. Above 100, the volume of fee tracking, parent communications, and scheduling genuinely requires dedicated time.

The question isn't whether you can afford help. It's whether you can afford to keep doing everything yourself.

A part-time admin assistant (3–4 hours/day, LKR 20,000–25,000/month) is less than 7% of a 100-student monthly revenue at average rates.

The question isn't whether you can afford help. It's whether you can afford to keep doing it yourself.

Your 4-week plan

Here's how to execute everything above, week by week.

week1
Name the hours
Track every non-teaching task this week — Log as you go — task name and rough time estimate
Do the two-list exercise — "Only I can teach this" vs "Anyone organised could do this"
Identify your 3 biggest time sinks — Fee chasing, parent messages, attendance — confirm yours
~2 hours
week2
Remove what you shouldn't do
Set up fee tracker and 3-message reminders — One hour now saves 3–4 hours every month — do this first
Set up WhatsApp broadcast list — One message to all parents instead of 20 individual ones
Draft a policy message for your most common question — "Fees due by the 5th" answers 40% of messages — send it proactively
~1.5 hours
week3
Automate the rest
Set up the attendance log — One sheet per class, tick or cross per session — 90 seconds/class
Connect attendance to parent updates — Absent twice this term = personal check-in — system flags it
Write your hiring threshold in writing — "I will hire admin help when I reach [X] students." Put it somewhere visible
~1.5 hours
week4
Measure the difference
Compare this week's non-teaching hours to Week 1 — The difference is what automation bought back — note it
Identify one more thing to remove next month — Marking is next for most teachers — what's yours?
Block protected teaching-prep time — Two 90-min blocks per week, calendar locked, phone on DND
~1 hour

The admin problem isn't about being organised. Every teacher who ends up drowning in admin was organised once. It's a structural problem — the tasks multiply as you grow, but the system never changes.
