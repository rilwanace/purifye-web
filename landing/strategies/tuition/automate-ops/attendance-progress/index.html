Tracking attendance and progress isn't paperwork — it's your retention system. The data you don't have is the data parents ask for right before they leave.

01
Start with a class list and a pencil — that's enough

A5 notebook. Student names on the left. Dates across the top. A tick means present. A blank means absent. That's the whole system.

Any system you'll actually use beats the perfect system you won't.

Nothing more complex than this is needed to start. The goal is a record that exists and gets used every class — not a perfect system.

Any system you'll actually use beats the perfect system you won't.

02
Flag 2 absences in a row — every time, without fail

Two consecutive absences without explanation is the most reliable early signal of a student about to drop. The ones who leave quietly have almost always missed 2+ classes in a row first.

That message costs 30 seconds. It shows the parent you notice, you care, and the student matters to you by name.

Set one rule: after 2 consecutive absences with no message, send a check-in. Not a complaint — a genuine check-in.

Copy-paste this

Hi Mrs. De Silva, just checking in — Tharushi missed the last two classes. Is everything okay? We've been doing exam-style past papers and I'd hate for her to miss the pattern.

That message costs 30 seconds. It shows the parent you notice, you care, and the student matters.

03
Track test scores as trends, not snapshots

A single score means nothing without context. A score that's been going up for 3 months means everything to a parent.

A parent who sees 52 → 61 → 68 over three months won't question whether tuition is working.

Keep a monthly score column in your register. Note whether it's up or down from last time. That's all you need.

04
Send monthly parent updates — short, specific, proactive

A monthly WhatsApp with 3 sentences: attendance this month, test score trend, one thing you're working on together next month.

Teachers who send proactive updates get fewer incoming messages, fewer worried calls, and retain students longer.

That's every question a parent was going to ask — answered before they ask it.

Copy-paste this

Hi Mrs. Wickramasinghe, Kavinda attended 8/9 sessions in October. His test score was 74, up from 68 last month. He's solid on algebra — we're focusing on graph problems this term. Good progress.

Teachers who send proactive updates get fewer incoming messages, fewer worried calls, and longer retention.

05
Use term-end reports to justify your fee and build loyalty

At the end of each school term, generate a one-page progress summary for every student: attendance rate, test score trend, areas of strength, areas to work on, plan for next term.

Teachers who send term reports retain students at a significantly higher rate than those who don't — because the report makes the progress tangible.

These reports do three things: show parents the value of what they're paying for, give students a sense of progress, and set expectations for the next term.

Teachers who send term reports retain students at a significantly higher rate than those who don't.

06
Identify struggling students early — before parents do

If you're tracking attendance and scores, you'll see a student starting to fall behind before their parents notice. Reach out first.

The moment a parent has to come to you with a concern, you're already in a defensive position. But if you message them first, you're a proactive teacher who caught it early.

That framing changes everything about how the conversation goes.

Your 4-week plan

Here's how to execute everything above, week by week.

week1
Build the log
Set up the attendance log for each class — Names down the left, session dates across — tick or cross per session
Set up the score-tracking sheet — Same structure: names, one column per test, running average
Define the 2-absence flag rule — Absent twice in a row or 3× this term = check-in within 24 hours
~2 hours
week2
Run first reports
Generate updates for 3 parents — Students with absent pattern or score drop — write the update
Send the parent update messages — Attendance, latest score, one observation, what to focus on next
Flag any student already at 2 absences — Review the log now — anyone at risk gets a message today
~1 hour
week3
Catch at-risk students
Send personal check-ins to all flagged students — "I noticed [name] hasn't been in for a couple of sessions"
Send monthly updates to all other parents — A short message for students doing fine — proactive contact prevents questions
Log this term's test scores into the tracker — If testing without tracking, enter last 2 tests as baseline
~1 hour
week4
Make it weekly
Set a 15-minute Sunday review block — Scan log, flag anything new, send pending check-ins — recurring
Draft your term-end report template — Attendance %, test average, one strength, one focus, next steps
Set the term-end report send date — Pick a date 2 weeks before term ends — add it now
~1 hour
After 4 weeks, you'll have:

✓

✓

✓
A term-end report template ready so next term takes under 30 minutes to send
