Fee collection is the biggest time drain in a tuition teacher's week — and the most avoidable. Here's how to get from 2–4 hours of chasing per week down to 10 minutes.

01
Start with the most basic tracking system that will actually stick

Start with the simplest system you'll actually use. Even a notebook with student names down the left and months across the top works as a first step — the point isn't the tool, it's the habit of recording every payment the day it happens. Once you're tracking consistently, you'll quickly see why automating this matters: at 30+ students, even a well-kept notebook becomes a job in itself.

Most fee problems aren't about the money — they're about not knowing who owes what. A paper register solves that immediately.

The system doesn't need to be fancy. It needs to exist and be used every single month.

02
Send fee reminders on a schedule, not when you remember

Send 3 messages per month: 5 days before the due date, on the due date, and 7 days after if still unpaid. Write these messages once. Send them every month.

The 3-message sequence recovers most outstanding fees before they become awkward conversations.

Most parents aren't trying to avoid paying — they just forget. A reminder is not rude. Silence followed by awkward in-class pressure is rude.

Copy-paste this

Hi Mrs. Perera, just a reminder that fees for May are due on the 1st. You can pay by cash at class or transfer to [account]. Thank you!

The 3-message sequence recovers most outstanding fees before they become awkward conversations.

03
Add a digital payment option — even one

LANKAQR is free to set up and lets parents scan and pay by bank transfer instantly. Print the QR code, stick it on your whiteboard.

Teachers who have a QR code up collect fees faster because parents pay the moment they think of it, not when they happen to have cash at the next class.

Adding digital payment doesn't replace cash — but for the parents who find it easier, you'll get paid faster and have an automatic record.

04
Set your fee policy on day one — and write it down

At enrollment, give every parent a one-page document: monthly fee, due date, what happens after 2 months of non-payment, and your payment methods.

Parents who signed a written policy rarely argue about it. Parents who never saw a policy argue about everything.

Most fee awkwardness comes from unwritten expectations. When the policy is clear from the start, the conversation is about following what was agreed — not confronting someone.

Parents who signed a written policy rarely argue about it. Parents who never saw a policy argue every month.

05
Handle the "I'll pay next week" problem directly

After two "next weeks", have a direct conversation: offer a payment plan. Half now, half on the 15th. That's professional, not aggressive.

If the answer is "no" to a payment plan, you have clarity — and can make a decision. Endless deferral is worse than a clear answer.

The worst outcome is continuing to teach for free while resenting the student.

Copy-paste this

Mrs. Kumari, I want Sachini to keep attending — she's making real progress. I can hold her spot if we agree on a payment plan. Can we do half now and half on the 15th?

A payment plan gives you an answer. Endless 'next weeks' give you neither the money nor the clarity.

06
Keep a monthly income record — even if it's just totals

At the end of each month, write down: total students, total collected, total outstanding. Three numbers. Three minutes.

This isn't about audits today — it's about not being unprepared when the rules change.

Regulation is moving toward licensing and tax enforcement. Teachers who have 2 years of income records when that happens will have a much easier time than teachers who have nothing.

Your 4-week plan

Here's how to execute everything above, week by week.

week1
Build the register
Set up the fee register — Names, monthly fee amount, paid/unpaid column — all classes on one sheet
Write your payment policy — Due by 5th, late by 10th — one sentence per rule, send to all parents
Print the policy and send via WhatsApp — Physical copy for new enrollments, broadcast for existing parents
~2 hours
week2
Send first reminders
Send the Day 0 reminder to all classes — "Fees for [month] due by [date]. Cash or LANKAQR accepted."
Check the register on Day 7 — Note who hasn't paid — send the Day 7 follow-up to those only
Prepare the Day 14 message — Have it drafted — you'll need it for 2–3 students at most
~1 hour
week3
Add digital payment
Set up LANKAQR — Download the app, register, generate your QR — about 15 minutes
Share the QR with all parents via broadcast — "You can now pay digitally — screenshot and save this QR"
Print a payment card for each student — Name, monthly amount, payment methods — in their folder each session
~1.5 hours
week4
Close the loop
Send Day 14 messages to anyone still outstanding — "I haven't received [name]'s fees for [month] — can we sort this out?"
Review which parents paid fastest — Digital vs cash — push the faster method next month
Have one hardship conversation if needed — One student who genuinely can't pay deserves a real conversation
~1 hour
