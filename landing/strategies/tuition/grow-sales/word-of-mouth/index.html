Word of mouth is a gift. But when it's your only channel, one bad term can unravel years of work. Here's how to build a second engine — one that keeps working even when referrals dry up.

01
Directory presence is your foundation, not Facebook

60% of teachers already promote on at least one directory. If your profile on Iskolaya, Panthi, Mytutor, and Advice isn't current, you're invisible to parents who are actively searching right now.

Word of mouth brings people who already know your name. Directories bring the ones who don't know you exist yet.

Directories don't require ongoing posting. Set them up once, update after each result season.

02
Turn Facebook from a thing you mean to do into a thing that works

Most teachers post when they remember and wonder why nothing happens. The answer isn't more inspiration — it's a formula.

After 90 days of this, parents in your area recognize your name. After 6 months, they recommend you to other parents unprompted — and those recommendations don't require a personal connection to you.

3 posts per week: a study tip in Sinhala/Tamil on Monday, a results or student story on Wednesday, class logistics or an exam date reminder on Friday. Post at 7pm when parents are scrolling after work.

After 90 days of consistent posting, parents in your area recognize your name. After 6 months, they recommend you.

03
Set up a WhatsApp broadcast list — this week

A broadcast list sends a message to many parents at once, but it lands in their personal chat as if you sent it directly. It's not a group — there are no notifications from other parents, no thread to manage.

This costs nothing, takes 5 minutes to set up, and keeps your name in front of every parent in your existing network.

Export every current parent's number into a broadcast list. Send one update per week: exam dates, study tips, schedule changes.

WhatsApp groups create noise. Broadcast lists create presence. When parents see your name in their personal chat every week, you stay top of mind — without them having to follow you anywhere.

An algorithm doesn't decide who gets your message. You do.

04
Plant a YouTube channel as a long-term lead generator

You don't need to be a YouTube star. 10-minute subject explainers in Sinhala or Tamil — one per week — build a searchable library that works while you sleep.

This is a 12-month game. In month 1 you have 4 videos. In month 12 you have 52, and new students find you through search. That's not word of mouth — that's a second engine.

A parent Googling "A/L chemistry diffusion Sri Lanka" and finding your video is a warm lead who already knows how you teach before they message you.

Month 1 you have 4 videos. Month 12 you have 52 — and new students find you every week without a single extra post.

05
Partner with schools where teachers are missing

Sri Lanka's national teacher absenteeism rate is 15.9%. Parents near government schools with chronically absent teachers are actively searching for supplementary teaching — not by choice, but because there's no alternative.

These parents are your most motivated prospects. They're not comparison shopping — they need you.

Identify the 2–3 schools nearest to you with a known reputation for absent subject teachers. Put your flyers near those schools specifically.

06
Engage where parents are already talking

Every area has Facebook community groups and school parent groups where parents ask each other for recommendations. Join 5 of them. Answer questions about O/L preparation, exam dates, study strategies — not as advertising, as a teacher being helpful.

You're not selling. You're building familiarity. Familiarity converts when the moment arrives.

When someone eventually asks "does anyone know a good chemistry teacher near Kandana?" — your name will already be familiar.
