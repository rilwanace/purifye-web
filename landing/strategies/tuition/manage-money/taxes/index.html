Most tuition teachers operate in a grey area — and the lines are getting clearer. Understanding your actual obligations right now is the only way to avoid a problem later.

01
Understand what you're actually legally required to do

Self-employed tuition teachers must declare income under Individual Income Tax. The personal relief is LKR 1,200,000 per year — if your annual tuition income is below that, you owe zero income tax. You still need to file.

Most teachers who do this calculation discover they're either below the threshold or just slightly above it. The tax is real but manageable — and knowing is better than not knowing.

Annual filing deadline: November 30. Personal relief: LKR 1,200,000/year.

02
If you work through an institution, understand WHT

If a tuition center pays you more than LKR 50,000/month, they are legally required to deduct 5% Withholding Tax under Section 85 of the Inland Revenue Act 2017.

If you're on a center's payroll, ask whether they're deducting WHT. Non-compliance penalties fall on the institution — but your tax record is still affected.

This applies to teachers paid by the center, not to teachers who collect fees directly from students.

Non-compliance penalties fall on the center, but you're the one without a clear record.

03
Licensing is coming — prepare now rather than be caught later

In 2025, the Parliamentary Sectoral Oversight Committee recommended a formal licensing system for private tutors, citing international precedents. No implementation date has been set — but the direction is clear.

Start a monthly income record now — even a simple notebook. By the time licensing arrives, you'll have 2+ years of records.

Teachers who have clean income records when licensing is implemented will face no problems. Teachers who have never declared income may face back-tax assessments.

The teachers who started tracking in 2025 will barely notice when licensing arrives. The ones who didn't will reconstruct a year of income from memory.

04
What the Western Province ban means — and doesn't mean

In December 2024, Western Province issued a circular banning government school teachers from teaching their own school's students privately. It was suspended within days after teacher objections. Central and Sabaragamuwa Provinces implemented similar bans earlier.

If you are a full-time private tuition teacher (not a government school teacher), this regulation does not apply to you.

This does not ban private tuition in general. It targets the specific arrangement where a school teacher coerces their own students to attend their private classes.

If you are a full-time private tuition teacher, this regulation does not apply to you.

05
What to track right now — simple, not perfect

Monthly income total. Monthly expense total. Keep receipts for major expenses (rent, printing). That's all you need for basic filing.

If you're already above LKR 1.2 million annually: consult a tax advisor before your first filing. The cost (LKR 3,000–8,000 for one consultation) is far less than filing incorrectly.

Start this month, not next term. A notebook with 12 monthly entries is more useful than perfect records you never started.

Simple records started now are worth more than perfect records started too late.

Your 4-week plan

Here's how to execute everything above, week by week.

week1
Know where you stand
Calculate your approximate annual tuition income — Monthly fees × 12 × rough attendance rate — your starting figure
Compare to the LKR 1.2M zero-rate threshold — Below LKR 1.2M after personal relief? Tax liability may be zero
Check your WHT exposure on hall rental — Paying a landlord over LKR 100,000/month? Withholding tax may apply
~1 hour
week2
Get the records in order
Collect expense receipts from the past 12 months — Rent agreements, printing invoices, phone bills, teaching materials
Calculate your deductible expenses total — Rent, printing, phone (partial), internet (partial), professional development
Calculate taxable income = tuition income minus deductibles — This is the number that matters — not your gross income
~1.5 hours
week3
Claim your deductions
Identify deductible expenses you haven't been claiming — Most teachers under-claim — development, books, equipment all qualify
Note your expected tax bracket — 1.2M–3M at 6%, 3M–6M at 12% — knowing your bracket means planning, not reacting
Set up a simple expense log going forward — Date, amount, category — one line per expense, 2 min/week to maintain
~1 hour
week4
File or plan to
Mark the 30 November filing deadline in your calendar — Know the date whether or not you're filing — missing it has consequences
Note when you'll cross the registration threshold — Growing? Calculate at what point your income requires IRD registration
Read the latest on the licensing framework — Check Ada Derana or Daily Mirror — the SOC recommendation has no confirmed implementation date yet
~30 min
After 4 weeks, you'll have:
✓
IRD filing obligation confirmed whether you are above or below the LKR 1.2M threshold
✓
Approximate tax liability calculated after personal relief and claimable expenses
✓
Filing deadline noted November 30 — in the calendar now
✓
A monthly income record started notebook or spreadsheet, this month
When licensing arrives, your records are already in order.

The fact that you're even reading this puts you ahead. Most teachers won't think about this until a letter arrives. You're thinking about it now, which means you have time to prepare properly.

One notebook. Monthly income totals. Start this month. That's all it takes to be ready for whatever comes next.
