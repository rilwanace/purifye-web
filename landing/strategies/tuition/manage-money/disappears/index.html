“January was my best month ever. By April I was borrowing from savings. How does that happen?”
“I put my tuition earnings into the same account as my personal money. I genuinely can't tell which is which.”
“I know the O/L batch is finishing in March. I have no plan for what happens to my income after that.”

Money disappearing without explanation has two causes: mixed personal and business accounts, and a cash flow cycle you haven't planned around. Both are fixable.

01
Separate business and personal money — open a second account

The easiest way to fix "money disappears" is to know whose money is whose. A separate bank account for tuition income takes 30 minutes to open.

Once accounts are separate, every financial question about your tuition business becomes answerable.

Every tuition fee collected goes into the business account. Every business expense comes out of it. Your personal account gets a fixed monthly transfer.

Once accounts are separate, every financial question about your business becomes answerable.

02
Build a cash flow calendar aligned to the exam cycle

January: peak income, maximum enrollment. February–March: O/L exam period, structural drop-off begins. April: school vacation, lowest income. August–September: crash course income spike. This pattern repeats every year.

The cycle is predictable. Plan for it.

Plan your major expenses for January. Build your cash reserve during August. Don't make financial commitments in October that assume income that hasn't arrived yet.

03
Build a 2-month cash reserve — methodically

Two months of operating expenses is enough buffer to survive any post-exam drop-off without stress. Take 10% of each month's net income and put it in a separate savings account. Don't touch it.

This is the single most stabilizing financial move a tuition teacher can make. April stops being stressful.

At LKR 400,000/month net income, 10% is LKR 40,000. In 5 months you have your 2-month reserve.

April stops being a crisis and starts being a month. That's the only difference a 2-month reserve makes.

04
Add digital payment for automatic income records

Every LANKAQR transfer and bank transfer creates an automatic record. Cash doesn't.

Bank statements become a partial income ledger for free, without any extra record-keeping effort.

Even moving 30–40% of fee collection to digital immediately gives you much better visibility into your monthly income.

Every digital payment creates a record automatically. Cash doesn't. The difference accumulates over a year.

05
15-minute financial review on the last Sunday of each month

Total income received. Total expenses paid. Net income. Outstanding fees. Cash reserve balance. Five numbers. 15 minutes.

The teachers who manage money well don't have special knowledge. They have a consistent habit.

This habit, done consistently, means you never have another April where you're surprised by how little you have.

The teachers who manage money well don't have special knowledge. They have a consistent habit.

Your 4-week plan

Here's how to execute everything above, week by week.

week1
Open the account
Open a separate account for tuition income — ComBank, NDB, any bank — even a separate mobile wallet works
Redirect all fee payments to this account — "Please send fees to [new account]" — no explanation needed
Stop mixing personal and teaching money today — Teaching income in, teaching costs out — personal money stays separate
~1.5 hours
week2
Map the exam calendar
Draw your 12-month cash flow — Mark high months (Oct–Jan) and low months (Mar–Apr, Jul–Aug) — paper first
Calculate your average monthly income — Total annual income ÷ 12 — high months are surplus, low months need covering
Identify the biggest single drop — Which month falls sharpest? That's your buffer target month
~1 hour
week3
Build the buffer
Set the buffer rule — "Keep one month's costs in the teaching account at all times" — write it where you'll see it
In your next high-income month, hold back the buffer first — Not "save what's left" — transfer buffer first, then use what remains
Set up LANKAQR for digital payment — Faster collection = less cash floating outside your account
~30 min
week4
Run the 5-number review
Review 5 numbers — Income, expenses, profit, buffer balance, exam cycle position — 15 min max
Check where you are in the exam cycle — Heading into a high month or a low one? Spending should reflect that
Adjust the buffer if costs have changed — Rent or printing increased? Recalculate your 1-month buffer target
~30 min
After 4 weeks, you'll have:
✓
A separate account for tuition income every fee in, every expense out — tracked
✓
A 12-month cash flow map built showing peak months, lean months, and the April dip
✓
A 2-month reserve rule started 10% of net income set aside each month
✓
At least 30% of fee collection moved to digital automatic income records, no effort
Tuition income now has its own account, its own calendar, and its own buffer.

The money problem for most teachers isn't that they're spending too much. It's that October and November feel like you're finally making good money, and then April and May feel like you're back to nothing. Both feelings are wrong — the income is predictable once you draw the full-year picture. The buffer isn't a sacrifice. It's just moving October's surplus to April's shortfall.

Draw a 12-month grid on paper. Write your income estimate for each month — even a rough guess is fine. Look at where the valleys are. That picture took 10 minutes and will change every money decision you make for the rest of the year.
