Posting without a system is how most people get stuck. A small change in what you post, when you post it, and where you post it can change everything — without posting more often.

01
The 3-post-per-week formula that actually works

Post when parents are scrolling — after work, after dinner. Not during school hours, not at 11am.

This isn't creative. It's consistent. And consistency at this scale beats quality when you're starting out.

Monday 7pm: a study tip or exam technique in Sinhala or Tamil. Wednesday 7pm: a student result or progress story. Friday 7pm: class schedule, upcoming exam dates, or a practical reminder.

Consistency at this scale beats quality. Three posts a week, every week, wins over the best post once a month.

02
Make content parents share, not content you want to post

Parents share two kinds of content: useful information (exam dates, study guides, preparation tips) and proof (results, student improvements, testimonials).

Every post should answer one question: would a parent share this with another parent? If not, rewrite it.

A post that says "Working hard builds character!" gets ignored. A post that says "3 students scored distinctions in last week's O/L practice paper — proud of this batch" gets shared by parents to other parents.

Every post should answer one question: would a parent share this with another parent? If not, don't post it.

03
Community groups are where discovery actually happens

Every area has WhatsApp groups and Facebook groups for parents: "Rajagiriya Parents", school community groups, "Anuradhapura Mothers Network." Join them. Answer questions about exams, study strategies, school calendars.

Familiarity built over time converts when the moment arrives.

Don't advertise. Help. When someone eventually asks "does anyone know a good chemistry teacher near Nugegoda?" — your name will already be familiar from weeks of helpful answers.

04
Run one small Facebook ad during enrollment season

LKR 2,000–5,000 for a 7-day boosted post is enough to reach 3,000–5,000 parents in your area. Choose January (peak enrollment) or August (crash course season).

This is not a big investment. It's enough to test whether paid reach works for you before committing more.

Boost a post with your best recent results — pass rates, grade counts, a student quote. Not a class schedule. Results.

05
Measure inquiries, not likes

Likes don't pay fees. Track how many new parent inquiries you get each month — and note which posts they mentioned seeing.

A results post that gets 8 likes and 3 inquiries beats an inspirational post that gets 40 likes and zero inquiries every time.

After 3 months of consistent posting, you'll know exactly which type of content brings inquiries. Double down on that. Stop making the rest.

Your 4-week plan

Here's how to execute everything above, week by week.

week1
Audit what's working
Review your last 10 posts — Which got most engagement? Tips, results, or personal stories?
Identify your two best posting times — For Sri Lanka parent audiences: 7pm and Saturday morning
Decide on your posting format — Results posts and teaching moments beat generic tips — stop posting tips
~1 hour
week2
Switch the formula
Post one student result update this week — "[Name] came to me averaging 40%. Last week: 67%. Here's what changed." — post at 7pm
Post one real teaching moment — A situation from your class this week and what it taught you
Join 2 local parent community groups — Search "[area] parents" — comment helpfully before posting
~1 hour
week3
Get into the rooms
Post your results update in both groups — Same post, new audience — max once per week per group
Comment genuinely on 5 posts in those groups — Helpful teacher, not marketer — exam prep, study habits, your subjects
Ask one parent to share your page — "If you know anyone looking for a tutor, I'd be grateful if you'd share"
~1 hour
week4
Build the calendar
Write a 4-week content calendar — Two posts/week: one result, one teaching insight — write all 8 headlines now
Schedule posting times as calendar reminders — 7pm Sunday and 7pm Wednesday — not ad hoc when you remember
Review this month's reach — Any new parents from a post? Note which type — do more of it
~1 hour
After 4 weeks, you'll have:
