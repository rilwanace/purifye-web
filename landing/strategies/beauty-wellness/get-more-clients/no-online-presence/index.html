“I have been open for two years and I still have no Facebook page, no Instagram, nothing. I know I need it but I do not know where to start.”
“My competitor across the road gets new clients every week just from Instagram. I get nothing because nobody can find me unless someone tells them.”
“I tried to make an Instagram once but I did not know what to post so I left it. I have 11 posts from 2022 and I have not touched it since.”

In Sri Lanka today, a new client looking for a salon near them will check Google, Instagram, and Facebook — in that order — before they walk in anywhere. If your salon does not appear, you do not exist for that client. The good news: the foundation can be built in one week with a phone, a free Google account, and two hours.

01
Google Business Profile is the highest-return thing you can do — and it is free

When someone in Nugegoda searches 'salon near me' or 'keratin treatment Nugegoda', Google returns a local map pack — three businesses with reviews, photos, hours, and a call button. A verified Google Business Profile is what gets you into that map pack. It costs nothing. It takes 30 minutes to set up. A completed profile with photos, services listed, and regular reviews consistently outperforms paid advertising for generating new walk-in clients.

Go to business.google.com and create a profile for your salon. Fill in: business name, category (Beauty Salon), address, phone number, website or Instagram link, opening hours. Upload at least 5 photos — your exterior (so clients recognise it), your interior, and 2–3 examples of your best work. Google will send a postcard to your address with a verification code. Enter it when it arrives. Once verified, your salon appears in local searches. Then immediately send this message to your next 5 happy clients.

COPY-PASTE THIS
Hi [name], thank you so much for coming in today! If you have a moment, could you leave us a quick Google review? It helps new clients find us. Here is the link: [your Google review link]. It only takes 30 seconds and it means a lot to us — Salon Mihiri

A complete Google Business Profile with 10–15 genuine reviews is worth more than Rs. 50,000 in advertising.

02
Instagram: consistency matters more than quality

The most common Instagram mistake Sri Lankan salon owners make: trying to post perfect content and giving up when it feels too hard. The second most common: posting only during busy months and going silent during slow periods. Instagram rewards consistency over perfection. A salon that posts 3 times per week — even simple before/after phone photos with basic captions — builds a visible presence faster than one that posts professional content once a month.

Three content types that work with a phone camera and no editing skills: before/after photos (client's hair before and after a keratin, colour, or cut — always ask permission), process videos (a 20-second Reel of you applying colour, blowing out hair, doing a gel manicure — no commentary needed, just the work), and results (a finished bridal look, a gel nail set, a straightening result). Post with your location tagged (Nugegoda, Colombo 5, Kandy — wherever you are). Add 5–8 relevant hashtags in Sri Lanka Sinhalese or English.

Three real posts per week beats one perfect post per month, every time.

03
Your bio and profile must answer: what do you do, where are you, how do I book?

Most salon Instagram profiles fail the three-second test: a new visitor cannot immediately tell what the salon does, where it is, or how to book. If your bio says 'beauty is our passion ❤️✨💋', you are losing clients who arrived curious. If your bio has no address or contact method, you are losing clients who were ready to book.

Your Instagram bio should include: your salon name, your city and area ('Nugegoda • Colombo'), your top 2–3 services ('Keratin • Bridal • Colour'), and a contact link (your WhatsApp number as a wa.me link, or your booking link). Pin three posts to your profile: your best bridal work, your best colour transformation, and your best everyday service. These are what every new visitor sees first.

A bio that answers what, where, and how to book converts visitors to enquiries. Passion quotes do not.

04
Facebook: set it up but do not let it distract you from Google and Instagram

Facebook remains relevant in Sri Lanka for older clients (35+) and for community groups where salon recommendations circulate. A Facebook Business Page is worth having — it takes 20 minutes to create and it appears in Facebook searches and Google searches. But Facebook organic reach for business pages has declined significantly. Unless you are running paid Facebook ads (a separate decision), Facebook should not consume your content creation time.

Create a Facebook Business Page that mirrors your Google and Instagram presence: same name, same photos, same contact information, same services listed. Link your Instagram so that posts automatically cross-post. Then prioritise your energy on Google reviews and Instagram content. Facebook will exist as a passive presence that works without maintenance — and it will satisfy clients who specifically look there.

Facebook is a presence to maintain, not a platform to build your business on in 2025.

05
WhatsApp Business: your most direct client communication channel

WhatsApp Business (the free app available from the Play Store or App Store) turns your existing WhatsApp number into a business profile with: a business name, address, website link, description, catalogue of services, and quick-reply templates. For a Sri Lankan salon where most bookings arrive via WhatsApp message, this is the most immediately impactful upgrade you can make.

Download WhatsApp Business and set up your profile. Add your salon name, address, a brief description, your Instagram link, and your opening hours. Create 3–5 quick replies for your most common messages: your price list, your booking availability response, your location, your services list. Label the app icon on your phone so clients see 'Salon Mihiri' when they contact you rather than a personal name. Your existing WhatsApp conversations transfer across automatically.

WhatsApp Business makes your most-used booking channel look and work like a professional business.

06
The local community group strategy: where Sri Lankan salons still find clients

Facebook community groups — neighbourhood groups, expat groups, women's groups, school parent groups — remain active in Sri Lanka and are still a source of genuine client referrals. Members ask 'can anyone recommend a good salon in Nugegoda?' regularly. If your salon has a Google review link and an Instagram profile, a single recommendation in a group of 5,000 members can generate 3–8 new bookings in a week.

Join the 2–3 most active community groups for your area. Do not spam — this destroys trust and gets you removed. Instead: be present, answer questions when you can help, and when someone asks for a salon recommendation, ask a happy client to post your name (more credible than self-promotion). Create a referral incentive: any client who refers a new client gets their next treatment at 15% off. The incentive is cheaper than the cost of finding new clients through advertising.

One genuine recommendation in a local Facebook group can bring in more new clients than a week of posting.
