96.71% of Sri Lankan salons are single-owner operations — you are not attempting something rare. Here is the realistic path, with the actual numbers.

01
Choose your growth stage — don't skip straight to a full salon

The typical path in Sri Lanka: home-based or freelance (zero overheads, personal kit) → small rented space or shared chair → own small salon with 3–6 chairs. Most owners who skip straight to a full salon fail not because of skill but because of cash flow pressure before the client base is big enough to sustain rent and staff.

Start freelance if: you have under 20 committed clients or no savings buffer. Rent a chair if: you have 20–40 clients and want a fixed address. Open your own salon if: you have 40+ loyal clients, 3–6 months of rent saved, and a clear niche.

The salons that survive year one are the ones that started at the right stage — not the most ambitious stage.

02
Pick your niche before you pick your location

Hair-only, full beauty, nails-only, barbershop, bridal specialist — these are different businesses with different client frequencies, pricing, and equipment. A nail studio client comes monthly. A bridal MUA client comes once but pays Rs. 40,000–90,000. A haircut client comes every 6–8 weeks. Trying to do everything from day one spreads your investment too thin.

Keratin is the most popular premium service in Sri Lanka and the key revenue driver for mid-to-upper salons — if you have this skill, build around it. Men's grooming is a growing segment. Provincial markets are underpenetrated at the quality end — being the premium salon in Kandy or Galle beats fighting for the middle of crowded Colombo.

A clear niche makes your marketing simpler, your equipment list shorter, and your reputation faster to build.

03
Understand what you are actually committing to with rent

Rent is your largest fixed cost and the number one reason salons close. Colombo city centre (Colombo 1–7): Rs. 150,000–300,000/month. Colombo suburbs (Nugegoda, Battaramulla, Mount Lavinia): Rs. 60,000–150,000/month. Provincial cities (Kandy, Galle, Negombo): Rs. 30,000–80,000/month.

The minimum viable rent rule: your monthly rent must not exceed 25% of your projected monthly revenue. At Rs. 95,000 rent (Salon Mihiri, Nugegoda), you need at least Rs. 380,000/month in revenue to be sustainable. Calculate this number before you sign anything.

Sign only after your revenue projection covers rent twice over. Month one is never at full capacity.

04
Register your business — it takes one afternoon

There is no mandatory practitioner licence to operate a beauty salon in Sri Lanka — no hygiene inspection, no NVQ requirement for operation. What you need: business registration under the Business Registration Act (for a sole trader) or with the Registrar of Companies. For most independent salon owners, sole trader registration is sufficient. VAT registration is only mandatory above Rs. 60 million annual turnover.

NVQ certification through TVEC is voluntary but builds credibility. Display it if you have it. Income tax applies to all business income at personal brackets (6–40%). Keep basic records from day one — even a simple notebook — so tax time is never a reconstruction exercise.

Registration takes one afternoon. Operating without it costs you credibility every time a vendor or landlord asks for your business details.

05
Set up Google Business Profile and WhatsApp Business on day one

Google Maps is the primary discovery channel for salons in Colombo. Noeline's has 4,000+ Google reviews — a moat built over years. Salons with under 50 reviews are significantly disadvantaged in local search. Create your Google Business Profile at business.google.com before you open. Add photos of your space and services. Ask every satisfied client for a review.

WhatsApp Business is your booking system, CRM, and broadcast tool. Set up your business profile with services, hours, and location. Create a broadcast list from day one — every client who visits gets added. This is how Mihiri at Salon Mihiri keeps Chamari, Mrs. Rajapaksha, Fathima, and Anusha coming back without any paid software.

06
Separate your business and personal finances from the first transaction

Most salon owners mix business and personal money from day one and never fully untangle it. This makes it impossible to know if you are profitable. Open a separate bank account for the salon before your first client — any major bank will do. Every client payment goes in, every business expense comes out, and your own pay is a transfer.

Cash is still dominant in Sri Lankan salons, but the Central Bank removed LankaQR transaction fees up to Rs. 5,000 from April 2026. Set up LankaQR — it is free, every digital payment is auto-recorded, and it eliminates the cash-handling errors that silently eat revenue in a busy week.

The salon owners who cannot tell you if they made money last month are the ones who never separated their accounts. Five-minute fix, ten-year payoff.

Your 4-week plan

How to execute this before and during your first month open.

week1
Make the decisions
Write your niche (hair-only, bridal, full beauty, nails) and your target client in one sentence each — commit it to paper
Calculate minimum viable revenue: rent + 1 staff salary + monthly products = your floor number
List 10 clients who would follow you today — if you cannot name 10, start freelance first
~3 hours
week2
Visit locations and run the numbers
View 3 spaces — note rent, size, foot traffic, AC, parking, and lease terms
Check the rent-to-revenue ratio: rent must be under 25% of your realistic monthly revenue
Open a separate business bank account before you commit to a space
~4 hours
week3
Register and set up digital presence
Register as sole trader under the Business Registration Act — one visit to your local authority
Set up WhatsApp Business with your salon name, services, and hours
Create your Google Business Profile — add photos and set up your review link
~3 hours
week4
Open with a system in place
Message every past client personally to announce your opening date — not a broadcast, individual messages
Add every first-week client to your WhatsApp broadcast list and send a review request after their visit
Record every transaction in your daily sales log — a notebook works fine to start
~2 hours

✓

✓
