Strategies›Beauty & Wellness›Manage My Appointments & Operations›My product costs keep going up and I can't raise prices enough
“I'm paying Rs. 12,000 for a litre of professional colour that used to cost Rs. 6,500 three years ago. I can't charge double for a colour service.”
“The products come from Nalaka in Colombo. Every time I order, the price has gone up again. I have no idea what my actual cost per service is.”
“I absorbed the price increases during the crisis to keep clients. Now I'm running on margins I don't understand and can't afford to calculate.”

Professional beauty products in Sri Lanka are 100% imported. The LKR is now at Rs. 310–320 per USD — compared to Rs. 200 before the 2022 crisis. Product costs are structurally 50%+ higher than four years ago. This is not going away. Here is how to protect your margins within that reality.

01
Know your actual product cost per service — most owners are guessing

Most Sri Lankan salon owners estimate product cost as a feeling — 'colour is expensive, haircuts are cheap.' The reality is more precise: professional product cost as a percentage of service revenue is typically 10–25% depending on treatment type. A keratin treatment uses Rs. 1,500–3,000 in product on a Rs. 25,000 service — that is 6–12%, manageable. A colour service uses Rs. 2,000–5,000 in product on a Rs. 10,000–15,000 service — that is 13–50%, potentially dangerous.

Calculate the product cost for your top 5 services. For each service: (1) list every product used, (2) note the pack size and price, (3) calculate how much of each pack one service uses, (4) add it up. Do this once for each service. Mihiri does it for the keratin (product: Rs. 2,200, service price: Rs. 25,000, cost ratio: 8.8% — healthy). Then for colour (product: Rs. 3,800, service price: Rs. 12,000, cost ratio: 31.7% — needs attention).

You cannot manage product costs you have not measured. Calculate once. Then manage.

02
Reduce product waste — it is a larger cost than most owners realise

Product waste in Sri Lankan salons takes three forms: wrong mixing ratios (mixing too much colour and discarding the excess), over-ordering (buying more than can be used before expiry, especially for skincare), and inconsistent application (using different amounts for the same service depending on who is doing it). Each of these is a direct margin leak.

The fix for colour waste: mix to a written formula based on hair length and thickness, not by eye. Kaveesha mixes one amount because she was trained that way. Thanuja mixes 30% more because she is less confident. Standardising the mixing ratio for each service type reduces colour product waste by 15–25% in most salons. For skincare and facial products: measure by service rather than by feel. A standardised pump count per facial costs the same every time.

Inconsistent application is inconsistent cost. Written ratios for every colour service pay back their 10 minutes of work every week.

03
Negotiate with Nalaka — bulk orders and payment terms

Most Sri Lankan salons order from distributors reactively — when stock runs out, they call Nalaka. This is the most expensive way to buy. Reactive orders are small orders. Small orders get the standard retail price. Consolidated monthly orders get bulk pricing. Nalaka needs Mihiri's volume as much as Mihiri needs his supply.

Approach your distributor with a consolidated order proposal: instead of three small orders per month, one large order per month at a 10–15% negotiated discount. Ask for net-30 payment terms if your cash flow is tight around product ordering. If your current distributor will not negotiate, get a price comparison from one alternative — this is often enough to get a discount from your existing supplier without actually switching.

04
Explore K-Beauty products as an alternative for some treatments

Korean beauty (K-Beauty) products have entered the Sri Lankan market through authorised retailers including ORION XO, RunRabbit, Blush Me, and 4her.lk. K-Beauty skincare and treatment products are often priced more competitively than European professional brands (L'Oreal Professional, Wella, Sothys) because they do not carry the same distribution mark-up in Asia. The K-Beauty Culture 2025 initiative formally positioned Sri Lanka as a K-beauty education and retail market.

K-Beauty is not appropriate for every treatment — professional colour still requires European brands for reliability. But for facials, scalp treatments, skin brightening, and moisturising treatments, Korean skincare products can deliver comparable or superior results at 20–40% lower product cost. Mihiri could offer a 'Korean glass skin facial' using K-Beauty products at a price point that is both affordable to clients and high-margin for the salon.

K-Beauty products in facials and skin treatments can cut product costs 20–40% while actually making the service more marketable.

05
Raise prices on the high-product-cost services — incrementally and with framing

Many Sri Lankan salons absorbed cost increases during the 2022–2023 crisis rather than passing them to clients. Operating on compressed margins years later. The LKR is structurally 50%+ weaker than pre-crisis — your product costs are permanently higher. Pricing based on what you charged in 2021 is not sustainable.

Raise prices on your highest-product-cost services first — colour, bleach, chemical treatments. Frame increases as a product quality note: 'We have upgraded to the latest professional formula — our colour services are now Rs. X.' A Rs. 1,500–2,000 increase on a Rs. 12,000 colour service is 12–17% — most clients who value quality will accept this. Clients who leave over Rs. 1,500 were price-sensitive regardless. Communicate to your regulars via WhatsApp before updating your price list publicly.

A Rs. 2,000 price increase on your colour service, done across 15 colour clients per month, is Rs. 30,000 extra revenue with zero additional work.

06
Track product stock and set reorder points — stop buying in panic

Salon Mihiri orders from Nalaka when the colour shelf looks low. Sometimes this means ordering urgently, paying delivery, and accepting whatever price is current. Sometimes it means running out mid-week and cancelling appointments. Both are avoidable. A basic product stock tracker — even a whiteboard on the stockroom wall — with reorder points ('order when colour stock drops below 3 packs') eliminates panic buying.

Once a week, Niroshi checks stock against the reorder point list. Products at or below reorder point go on the next consolidated order to Nalaka. This keeps stock levels consistent, prevents over-ordering of slow-moving products (which expire and get discarded), and ensures every monthly order is deliberate rather than reactive.

Panic buying costs more per unit and often leads to over-ordering of the wrong things. A reorder point list costs nothing and saves it.
