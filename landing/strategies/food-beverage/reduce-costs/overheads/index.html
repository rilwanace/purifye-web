“The electricity bill alone last month was more than my staff wages. I don’t know how long I can sustain this.”
“The delivery platforms take 25% and then there’s rent on top. By the time I pay everything, I’m working for free.”

Sri Lanka has the highest electricity bills in South Asia as of 2024. Delivery platform commissions run at 25–30%. Colombo rents in prime locations can exceed Rs.1 million per month. Every one of these overheads is compressible — but only if you know which lever to pull and when.

01
Audit every overhead line before cutting anything

The correct sequence: measure first, cut second. List every fixed and semi-fixed overhead: rent, electricity, gas, staff wages, delivery commissions, packaging, licenses, marketing.

Express each as a percentage of your monthly revenue. Industry benchmarks (SL F&B, estimated): food cost 30–40%, labour 15–25%, rent 10–20%, utilities 5–12%, delivery commissions 8–15% of total revenue (25–30% of delivery channel only). Which of your lines are above benchmark? Those are your targets.

Most owners carry a vague sense that ‘costs are high’ but don’t know which specific cost is the primary problem. An overhead audit takes 2 hours and tells you exactly where to focus your energy.

You can’t cut what you haven’t measured. The audit is not optional.

02
Electricity: the single most actionable overhead for most SL operators

Sri Lanka implemented a 66% electricity price increase in February 2023, following a 75% increase in August 2022. The cumulative increase since 2014: 705% in fixed charges, 264% in variable charges. Sri Lanka’s electricity bills are the highest in South Asia (PublicFinance.lk, 2024).

High-impact actions: (1) Switch to LED lighting throughout (Rs.50,000–100,000 one-time; payback in 4–6 months). (2) Turn off all non-essential refrigeration between 11pm–5am. (3) Assess whether your AC is essential for the full operating period. (4) Service your kitchen equipment annually — inefficient compressors and burners waste energy.

Several bakeries cited energy cost as the primary reason for closure during the 2022–2023 crisis. Those that survived: reduced operating hours, cut AC in non-essential areas, and switched to more efficient equipment. You don’t need to replicate the crisis to take the same precautions now.

Your electricity bill is the overhead most within your control. Most restaurants are wasting 20–30% of what they pay.

03
Delivery commissions: build the direct channel as your long-term defence

A 25% delivery platform commission is the cost of customer discovery. It is unavoidable if you rely on the platform to bring you new customers. But once a customer has ordered from you and been satisfied, every subsequent order they place directly via WhatsApp costs you 0% commission.

Every delivery order package should include: your WhatsApp number + a message: “Skip the app next time — order directly and save the platform fee.” If 30% of your repeat customers shift to direct ordering, your effective commission rate on total revenue drops significantly.

The structural constraint: you need the platform for discovery. The structural opportunity: you only need to pay for discovery once per customer. Build your direct channel aggressively for repeat orders while using the platform exclusively for new customer acquisition.

COPY-PASTE THIS — Package insert for direct ordering
Hi! Loved your order? Next time, order directly from us on WhatsApp: [number]. Same food, no platform fee. Save our number now!

Use the platform for new customers. Keep the margin on repeat customers.

04
Rent: negotiate from data, not desperation

Sri Lankan commercial landlords for restaurant premises are mostly individuals, not corporations. They respond to data-backed, respectful conversations more than most owners expect.

Your negotiating tools: (1) Your monthly revenue data showing your seasonal low periods. (2) The cost comparison of your business vs. an empty premises generating zero rent. (3) A proposal: seasonal rent adjustment, or a fixed monthly reduction in exchange for a longer lease commitment.

Colombo prime location rents can reach Rs.1M–1.5M+/month. Even a Rs.30,000–50,000/month reduction adds Rs.360,000–600,000 to your annual profit.

Timing: negotiate at lease renewal, or if you have evidence of a sustained revenue decline. Never negotiate from a position of crisis (late payments, visible distress) — negotiate from a position of a good tenant presenting data.

Your landlord’s alternative to keeping you is an empty premises. That is your leverage.

05
Gas: switch to LPG bulk where possible, and time usage

Gas prices in Sri Lanka have been volatile since 2022, driven by Rupee depreciation and global commodity prices. Litro Gas pricing was revised in July 2024.

Practical reductions: (1) Audit your kitchen burner usage: are burners on when nothing is cooking? This is extremely common and extremely wasteful. (2) Cover pots: a covered pot boils in half the time of an uncovered one. (3) Batch-cook: concentrating cooking into fewer, longer sessions uses less gas than repeated short heating cycles.

Cloud kitchens and delivery-only businesses typically have lower energy footprints than dine-in restaurants with full AC, ambient lighting, and long operating hours. If your dine-in revenue doesn’t justify your full dine-in operating cost, consider hybrid hours: dine-in lunch-only, delivery for dinner.

The burner that’s on without a pot is the most expensive item on your menu.

06
Packaging costs: reduce without reducing customer experience

Delivery packaging (containers, bags, sealing tape, cutlery sets) represents 3–8% of delivery revenue for most operators. Post-crisis, packaging costs increased with oil prices and import costs.

Reductions that don’t hurt the customer: (1) Remove single-use cutlery sets from delivery bags except on explicit request (most customers have cutlery at home). (2) Switch from branded packaging to plain (the cost difference is often Rs.5–15 per order). (3) Bulk purchase packaging quarterly instead of weekly to get volume discounts.

The packaging cost that does matter to the customer: leak-proof containers for gravies and curries. A spillage complaint from a delivery customer costs you their repeat business — far more than the Rs.20 saving on a cheaper container. Cut packaging cost where the customer doesn’t notice. Never where they do.

Cut packaging where the customer doesn’t see it. Never where they do.

Your 4-week plan

Here's how to execute everything above, week by week.

week1
The overhead audit
List every monthly overhead. Express each as % of monthly revenue.
Identify which 2 overheads are above industry benchmark.
Measure your electricity bill vs. the same period last year.
Calculate your effective delivery commission % of total revenue (not just delivery revenue).
2–3 hrs
week2
Quick wins
Switch all fluorescent lighting to LED this week.
Brief kitchen staff: every burner off when no pot is on it.
Remove single-use cutlery from delivery bags unless explicitly requested.
Add a ‘order direct’ insert to every delivery package today.
1–2 hrs
week3
Delivery and rent strategy
Calculate: if 30% of your repeat delivery customers switched to WhatsApp direct, what would your commission saving be monthly?
Build your direct WhatsApp order channel (auto-reply, menu, order format).
Review your lease: when does it expire? Prepare your rental negotiation data.
Schedule a meeting with your landlord to discuss the next lease period.
3–4 hrs
week4
Ongoing monitoring
Add electricity and gas to your weekly 5-cost tracker.
Compare this month’s utility bill to last month. Is the trend improving?
Audit packaging costs quarterly: are you buying the most cost-effective option?
Set a target: reduce total overhead % of revenue by 3 percentage points in 6 months.
30 min/week
