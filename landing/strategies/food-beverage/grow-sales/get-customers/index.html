“I’ve been open three months and I’m barely filling 20 tables a day. I don’t know what else to do.”
“I posted on Facebook twice but nothing happened. I feel like nobody can even find me.”

New customers in Sri Lanka’s F&B market come from four channels: foot traffic, delivery platforms, social media discovery, and word of mouth. Most owners are only working one of them. Here’s how to activate all four without spending much.

01
Claim your Google Business Profile — it’s free and powerful

Google Maps is the primary local discovery tool for both Sri Lankan locals and tourists in Colombo. A restaurant without a verified, photo-rich Google Business Profile is effectively invisible to anyone searching “restaurants near me.”

Claim your listing at business.google.com. Add 10+ photos of your food. Set correct opening hours. Add your WhatsApp number. This takes 2 hours and costs nothing.

Actively ask every happy customer to leave a Google review. A restaurant with 50 genuine reviews outranks a competitor with 5, even if that competitor spends on ads. Reply to every review — positive and negative — within 24 hours.

Your Google Business Profile is the most cost-effective marketing investment you will ever make.

02
Post short-form video — TikTok and Instagram Reels

Sri Lanka has 5.2 million TikTok users as of 2024–2025, with food content among the most-watched categories. Short-form video generates 2.3x higher engagement than static posts. You don’t need a professional camera — a clean counter, decent lighting, and a steady hand is enough.

What works: cooking in progress, plating a signature dish, showing the busiest moment of your lunch rush, behind-the-scenes kitchen prep, and food arriving at the table. Tower Burger (Wellawatta/Negombo) actively uses TikTok for menu launches and customer acquisition. Campaigns tied to local culture (Avurudu, Ramadan specials, Vesak menus) see ~40% higher engagement.

Post one 30-second video per week for 8 weeks. Your reach will surprise you.

03
Optimise your delivery app listing

Being on PickMe Food or Uber Eats doesn’t automatically bring orders. Your listing competes with hundreds of others. The algorithm favours: high-quality food photos (bright, appetising, professionally lit), fast acceptance times, high ratings, and promotional participation.

Items on delivery platforms should be priced 15–20% higher than your dine-in prices to offset commissions of 20–30%. Customers on delivery apps expect this.

Add a “Best Seller” or “Staff Pick” badge to your top 3 items. Keep your menu focused: 8–12 items outperform 40-item menus on delivery platforms because choice paralysis reduces orders.

A delivery listing is a shop window. Nobody stops at a window with no display.

04
Build a WhatsApp broadcast list from every sale

Your happiest customers are walking out the door and you have no way to reach them again. Fix this immediately: ask every customer who pays — or every customer who orders delivery — for their WhatsApp number and add them to your broadcast list.

Send your daily menu every morning at 9am. Send a special offer once a week. This is free, direct, and has dramatically higher open rates than any social media platform.

WhatsApp Business (free app) lets you create broadcast lists, set a business profile with your menu, and manage orders. Over 70% of Sri Lankan small F&B businesses have no systematic way to reach past customers. A broadcast list of 300 regulars changes that completely.

COPY-PASTE THIS — Add to WhatsApp broadcast
Hi! If you enjoyed your meal today, I’d love to send you our daily menu and weekly specials on WhatsApp. Just reply YES and I’ll add you. No spam — just food updates from us.

You already have customers who love your food. Make sure they know when you’re open and what’s on.

05
Partner with one local food influencer (offer a free meal)

Sri Lanka has 97+ tracked food influencers with 1K–10M followers. Nano-influencers (1,000–10,000 followers) with hyper-local, engaged Colombo audiences are reportedly the highest-ROI option for neighbourhood restaurants — at zero cash cost.

The industry standard in Sri Lanka: invite the influencer for a complimentary meal in exchange for an honest Instagram or Facebook post. Key accounts: @rasaimachang, @the_urbane_grub, @food_in_squares, @_food_diaries_, @raha.deen (Halal focus).

Don’t chase the biggest accounts. Chase the accounts whose followers look like your target customer. A 3,000-follower food account in your neighbourhood is more valuable than a 100,000-follower account based in another city.

One honest influencer review from a trusted local voice can fill your tables for a week.

06
Show up at a food market or festival

The Colombo Food Festival (every Sunday, Viharamahadevi Open Air Theatre, 10am–10pm) and Good Market (every Saturday, Colombo Racecourse, 9am–5pm) attract thousands of food-curious Colombo residents each week.

Participating as a vendor gives you: dense discovery in one day, face-to-face feedback, WhatsApp numbers, Instagram followers, and a story (“find us every Sunday at Viharamahadevi”) that drives repeat visits.

Galle Face Green hosts nightly food vendors (Isso Vade, corn, juices) with extremely high foot traffic. The Fairway Colombo Street Food Festival runs the last weekend of every month on Hospital Street, Colombo 01.

Discovery density beats advertising spend. Be where the food lovers already are.

Here's how to execute everything above, week by week.

Ask 5 regular customers to leave a Google review today.

week2

Add a WhatsApp number request to every customer receipt or order bag.

3–5 hrs
week3
Build consistency
Post one short video per week. Don’t overthink it — consistency beats perfection.

Add a ‘How did you find us?’ question to every new customer conversation.
