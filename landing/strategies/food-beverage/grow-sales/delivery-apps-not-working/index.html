“I’m on PickMe Food and Uber Eats but by the time they take their commission, I’m barely making anything on each order.”
“My listing just sits there. Other restaurants show up first and I don’t even know why.”

Being listed on PickMe Food or Uber Eats is not a strategy — it’s a starting point. Most restaurants fail on delivery apps for two reasons: a weak listing that doesn’t convert, and delivery economics they’ve never actually calculated.

01
Fix your listing — photos and menu structure first

The delivery app algorithm surfaces restaurants with higher click-through and acceptance rates. The most powerful single variable: food photography. Bright, appetising photos taken in natural daylight outperform dark, unclear ones in every click-through study. If your photos look like they were taken at night with a phone on low battery, redo them this weekend.

Menu structure matters too: 8–12 items outperform 40-item menus on delivery platforms. Too many choices reduce orders. Keep your delivery menu to your best sellers only.

Add descriptive, appetising names and short descriptions to each item. Instead of “Kottu Roti — Rs.500,” write: “Chicken Kottu — freshly chopped on the tawa, egg, veg, chef’s gravy — Rs.540.” Tag your best sellers with the “Popular” or “Best Seller” badge.

Your listing is your only storefront on the app. Treat it like a display window, not a spreadsheet.

02
Price your delivery items correctly — 15–20% higher than dine-in

PickMe Food and Uber Eats commissions run at 20–30% of each order. If your kottu is Rs.500 and the app takes 25%, you net Rs.375 before ingredient and labour costs. That is not a viable margin.

The solution is standard industry practice: price delivery-only items 15–20% higher than your dine-in menu. Customers ordering on delivery apps expect this — they know there’s packaging, delivery, and platform overhead.

You can also create delivery-exclusive bundle deals (e.g., meal + drink + dessert at a fixed price) that appear to offer value while protecting your margin on each order. Track your net margin per order weekly — most restaurant owners have never calculated this number.

Pricing for delivery is a separate calculation from pricing for dine-in. Treat it that way.

03
Track your net delivery margin weekly

Most operators know their gross delivery revenue. Almost none know their net margin after commission. The calculation: (order value × 0.75) − ingredient cost − packaging cost = net profit per order.

For a Rs.600 order: Rs.450 after 25% commission − Rs.200 ingredient cost − Rs.30 packaging = Rs.220 net. Is that enough? Only you can decide. But you need to know the number to make the decision.

Sunday Times Sri Lanka (2020) documented restaurant owners saying “It’s like we come to work for Uber Eats.” The platform relationship is structural: they have leverage and you have none. The only intelligent response is to know your numbers and push customers toward direct ordering wherever possible.

If you don’t know your net per delivery order, you’re flying blind in a low-margin business.

04
Build a direct WhatsApp ordering channel alongside the apps

Every delivery customer who orders via WhatsApp directly costs you 0% commission instead of 25%. The challenge: you still need the app for discovery. But once a customer has ordered once and been satisfied, you can invite them to order directly.

Add a sticker or note to every delivery package: “Skip the app next time — WhatsApp us directly at [number] for the same food, faster, with no extra fees.”

WhatsApp direct ordering is how home chefs, cloud kitchen operators, and small caterers across Sri Lanka retain margins while still using platforms for discovery. The app gets you found. WhatsApp keeps your margin.

COPY-PASTE THIS — Package insert for direct orders
Loved your order? Order directly from us next time on WhatsApp: [number]. Same food, faster response, no platform fee. Save our number now!

Use the app for discovery. Use WhatsApp for retention. That’s the correct relationship.

05
Create one delivery-exclusive item to make your profile memorable

On a crowded delivery app, every restaurant looks similar. A delivery-exclusive item — something only available through the app, not at your dine-in — gives you a competitive story. “The Only Place for [X] on PickMe Food in Colombo 05.”

It also gives you a reason to run in-app promotions on that specific item, which improves your algorithmic placement without discounting your core menu.

K24 Cloud Kitchen built their identity around “authentic biryani” on Uber Eats and TikTok. That specificity — not being everything to everyone — is what makes a delivery profile stick in a customer’s mind.

A restaurant that’s known for one brilliant thing on delivery is more powerful than one that does everything passably.

06
Participate in platform promotions to improve your ranking

Both PickMe Food and Uber Eats offer sponsored placements, discount schemes, and bundle promotions. Participating in these costs you margin in the short term but significantly improves your visibility in the algorithm. A 10% discount campaign that doubles your order volume may net more absolute profit than no discount and 50% of the volume.

Also: your acceptance rate and average delivery time affect your ranking. Accept orders quickly and communicate delays proactively — a 4.7-star average outranks a 4.0 regardless of other factors.

Review your platform analytics monthly (both apps provide merchant dashboards). Look for: peak order times, your most-ordered items, and your average order value. Use that data to run promotions at exactly the times your competitors are quiet.

On delivery platforms, visibility is a competitive advantage you can earn — not just buy.

Your 4-week plan

Here's how to execute everything above, week by week.

week1
Fix listing and pricing
Retake photos of your top 8 items in natural light. Upload them this week.
Reduce your delivery menu to your best 10 items maximum.
Recalculate your delivery prices: add 15–20% to your dine-in prices.
Calculate your net per order for your top 3 items (order × 0.75 − cost − packaging).
4–6 hrs
week2
Launch direct ordering channel
Set up WhatsApp Business with an auto-reply: “Thanks for your message! Here’s today’s menu…”
Print package inserts: “Order directly next time — save the platform fee.”
Put your WhatsApp number on every delivery bag, box, and sticker.
Add direct-order customers to your broadcast list immediately.
2–3 hrs
week3
Create your delivery identity
Identify your one strongest, most unique item. Build your delivery profile around it.
Write a compelling restaurant description on both platforms — 2 sentences, what makes you different.
Apply to participate in the next platform promotion or discount campaign.
Ask happy delivery customers (via WhatsApp) to leave a 5-star platform rating.
2–3 hrs
week4
Review numbers
Pull your delivery platform analytics: volume, top items, peak times, average order value.
Recalculate your net margin per order after your price changes.
How many direct WhatsApp orders did you get this month vs. last?
Decide: is delivery a core revenue channel or a discovery channel for you?
2 hrs

✓
A WhatsApp direct-order channel actively converting platform customers
✓
Your net margin per order calculated and tracked weekly
✓
One delivery-exclusive item that makes your profile memorable
✓
Participation in at least one platform promotion
Delivery apps will always take their commission. Your job is to make sure what’s left is still worth your time — and to convert platform customers into direct customers wherever possible.

The platform takes 25%. That’s the deal. The question is whether you’ve built your pricing, your listing, and your direct-order channel to survive that deal — and thrive despite it.

Start today: calculate your actual net profit on your top 3 delivery items. If the number surprises you, adjust your prices before you take another order.
