“Everyone tells me to “just start” — but start what, exactly, and how, without losing everything I have?”
“I don’t want to sign a lease and find out six months later that nobody wants what I’m selling.”

Starting a food business in Sri Lanka doesn’t have to mean a shophouse and a bank loan on day one. The smartest operators start cheap, test fast, and only commit to fixed costs once real customers have already proved they’ll pay.

01
Validate before you invest

The most common mistake: rent a space, fit it out, then discover the menu doesn’t sell. Good Market (every Saturday, Colombo Racecourse, 70–80 curated vendors) and the Colombo Food Festival (every Sunday, Viharamahadevi Open Air Theatre) exist precisely for this. A market stall costs a few thousand rupees. A failed shophouse costs lakhs.

Run your first 4 weekends as a stall vendor. Talk to every customer. Track what sells, what gets left on the plate, and what people ask for that you don’t have. That data is worth more than any business plan you could write.

Your first 100 customers are your best consultants. Pay them in food — not in research fees.

02
Use a shared kitchen — don’t build one

WORX Kitchen in Colombo rents certified commercial kitchen space by the shift (4hr, 8hr, or 24hr). Bring your own utensils, use their compliant equipment, and get on PickMe Food and Uber Eats from day one — without a Rs.500,000–1 million fit-out cost.

This is exactly how cloud kitchens like Foodprints by Koluu and K24 Cloud Kitchen started.

For caterers and home bakers, a registered shared kitchen also solves your PHI problem: your food is produced in a compliant, licensed space even if your home kitchen isn’t registered.

Rent the kitchen by the day until you’re profitable enough to justify building your own.

03
Build your customer list on WhatsApp before you have a premises

WhatsApp Broadcast Lists are how the best small food operators in Sri Lanka retain customers without spending a rupee. Every person who buys from you at a market stall gets added to your broadcast list. Every Monday morning you send them the week’s menu. Every Thursday, a special offer.

Home-based bakers, cloud kitchens, and caterers across Colombo use this as their primary marketing channel — more consistently than Facebook or Instagram. Yamu.lk’s catering directory lists WhatsApp as the primary contact for most small caterers.

COPY-PASTE THIS — WhatsApp first message after every sale
Hi! Thank you for trying [dish name] today. I’d love to keep you updated on new items and daily specials. Reply YES and I’ll add you to our WhatsApp updates list — no spam, just our weekly menu. You can stop anytime.

A broadcast list of 200 paying regulars is worth more than 2,000 Instagram followers.

04
Register properly from day one — don’t wait

You need two things minimum: (1) Business registration with the Registrar of Companies or your local Municipal/UC authority. (2) Food Premises Registration under the Food Act No. 26 of 1980 — a PHI inspects your premises before you get the certificate. Annual renewal required. The certificate must be displayed visibly on your premises.

If you operate from a shared kitchen like WORX, ask about their facility’s existing registration status.

Do NOT plan to serve alcohol without researching the excise license first. As of January 2024, a retail liquor license in a Municipal area costs Rs.15 million per year. That is not a typo. This is why most small independent restaurants don’t serve alcohol.

A PHI closure order costs more — in money and reputation — than registration ever will.

05
Know your food cost % before you set a single price

Food cost % = ingredient cost of the dish ÷ price you charge × 100. Target: 28–35% for most dishes. If your kottu costs Rs.150 in ingredients, you should charge Rs.430–540.

Post-crisis ingredient prices have been volatile: chicken swung from Rs.400/kg to Rs.2,100/kg in 2022–2023. Build a 10–15% price buffer into every item so ingredient swings don’t immediately destroy your margins.

Most small operators in Sri Lanka price by feel, or by copying the competitor across the road. If that competitor is losing money on every plate, copying their price means you will too.

Price for the business you need to run, not for the business your competitor appears to be running.

06
Choose your format deliberately

Four viable entry formats in Sri Lanka, ranked by startup risk: (1) Home-based — zero overhead, maximum flexibility, legally grey. (2) Market stall or pop-up — low cost, excellent validation, no long-term commitment. (3) Cloud kitchen via shared facility — delivery platforms from day one, no dine-in overhead. (4) Physical outlet — highest cost, highest risk, most walk-in visibility.

Cloud kitchens are growing but showing saturation signals in Colombo by 2024. Differentiation through a specific cuisine niche — like K24 Cloud Kitchen’s authentic biryani positioning — is essential. Being “just another cloud kitchen” on Uber Eats is no longer a viable strategy.

Start with the format that lets you lose the least if the market says no.

Your 4-week plan

Here's how to execute everything above, week by week.

week1
Concept and validation
Cook your top 3 dishes for 10 people outside your family. Write down every comment honestly.
Visit Good Market (Saturday) and Colombo Food Festival (Sunday) as a customer — study what sells.
Calculate food cost % for each of your dishes. Set your target prices.
Register your business (sole proprietorship or private company — choose based on tax situation).
10–15 hrs
week2
First public test
Apply for a vendor slot at Good Market (Colombo Racecourse) or Colombo Food Festival.
Set up your WhatsApp Business account with profile photo and your top 3 menu items.
Prepare 50 portions. Sell out. Collect every buyer’s WhatsApp number.
Note what sold fastest, what didn’t move, and what customers asked for.
1 full day + prep
week3
Build your customer base
Add all buyers from Week 2 to your WhatsApp broadcast list. Send a follow-up menu.
If cloud kitchen: register on PickMe Food and Uber Eats Merchant portals.
Post your first Instagram Reel or TikTok: cooking in progress or plating your signature dish.
Do your second market test with the updated menu based on Week 2 feedback.
8–12 hrs
week4
Decide: scale or pivot
Review Weeks 2–3 data: sales, margins, and direct customer feedback.
If validated: plan next step (more market days, WORX Kitchen, or small outlet search).
If not: adjust the concept before committing any more capital.
If going physical: contact your local PHI about Food Premises Registration requirements and timeline.
4–6 hrs
After 4 weeks, you'll have:
✓
A validated menu that real customers have paid for
✓
A WhatsApp broadcast list of at least 50 paying customers
✓
Your food cost % calculated for every item on your menu
✓
Business registered and PHI pathway mapped out
✓
A clear decision on format — and the reasoning to back it
