Strategies›Food & Beverage›Improve My Productivity›Morning market run eats my whole morning
“I leave at 4:30am for Manning Market, I’m back by 8, but by then I’m exhausted before the kitchen has even started.”
“The whole morning market thing — prices change every day, quality changes every day, and I can’t send someone else because they’ll get cheated.”

The morning market run is the defining time constraint of running a food business in Sri Lanka. Most small restaurant owners spend 3–4 hours every morning on it — arriving exhausted at the most important operational moment of their day. There are ways to compress this significantly without sacrificing quality or cost control.

01
Build relationships with 2–3 trusted market suppliers

The reason most owners go to Manning Market themselves: they don’t trust anyone else to get the right quality at the right price. This is rational. It is also the constraint.

The solution is not to stop going — it’s to build supplier relationships deep enough that you can delegate reliably. Pick 2–3 specific vendors who consistently give you good quality and fair prices. Go to them first, every day. Over time, they reserve the best produce for you. Over more time, they know your standards well enough that you can call or WhatsApp the order the night before and pick up in 20 minutes.

Manning Market vendor relationships are personal and long-term. A vendor who knows you by name, knows your restaurant, and knows you’ll be there tomorrow is a vendor who won’t sell you last week’s produce. That relationship takes 3–6 months to build and then saves you 90 minutes a day forever.

Trusted vendor relationships are the only sustainable solution to the morning market problem.

02
Move to WhatsApp pre-orders for most produce

Once a vendor relationship is established: WhatsApp your order the night before. 9pm message: “[Vendor name] tomorrow’s order: [item] [quantity] × [item] [quantity]. What’s the price for [main ingredient] tomorrow?”

The vendor confirms overnight or early morning. You arrive at the market, go directly to your vendor, collect the pre-packed order, pay, and leave. This can reduce your market visit from 90 minutes to 30 minutes. 3 days per week of this change is 3 hours of your morning back.

This only works with vendors you trust and who trust you. The first 3 months of the relationship require you to be present to check quality, calibrate the vendor to your standards, and build the trust that makes pre-ordering safe.

3 months of daily visits to build the relationship. Then 90 minutes a day back forever. The maths work.

03
Separate fresh produce visits from dry goods purchasing

The morning market trip is unavoidable for fresh produce (vegetables, fish, meat): quality varies daily and pre-purchasing doesn’t work for highly perishable items.

But dry goods (rice, flour, lentils, spices, oil) do not need to come from Manning Market. Dry goods can be delivered to your premises weekly from a wholesale supplier. Combining dry goods buying with the daily fresh produce trip doubles your market time needlessly.

A single weekly scheduled dry goods delivery from a trusted wholesaler eliminates 5 dry-goods-adjacent market trips per week. That is 60–90 minutes per week of saved market time, minimum.

Build your list of which items belong in each category: ‘Daily fresh’ (go to market): fish, fresh vegetables, meat, herbs. ‘Weekly scheduled delivery’: rice, flour, oil, dry spices, canned goods, packaging supplies.

Daily market trip for fresh items only. Everything else arrives on a schedule.

04
Train and trust a reliable buyer to handle 2–3 days per week

The goal is not to eliminate your market presence — it is to reduce your personal presence to the days when it matters most. Weekdays when your menu is standard can be handled by a trusted senior staff member or a family member who you have trained to your quality standards.

Training a buyer: take them to the market with you for 2 weeks, every day. Explain your standards explicitly: what does ‘fresh fish’ look like? How do you test a vegetable for freshness? What price range is acceptable for each item this season? After 2 weeks, let them handle Wednesday and Thursday while you handle Monday, Tuesday, and Friday.

The risk: they will occasionally make a sub-standard purchase. This is part of the training cost. It is far cheaper than 4 hours of your time every single day. A staff member who knows your market standards is one of the most valuable assets in your kitchen.

Your morning time is too valuable to spend entirely at the market. Train the handoff.

05
Use the time you save for the work only you can do

The morning market trip is consuming time that should be spent on: planning today’s specials based on what’s fresh and cheapest, briefing your kitchen staff on the day, reviewing yesterday’s revenue and costs, and managing the operational decisions that require your judgment.

When the owner is at the market until 8am and then immediately in the kitchen, there is no time for the strategic thinking that actually grows the business. Recovering even 90 minutes of morning time creates space for the work that compounds.

The most common complaint from successful Sri Lankan restaurant owners who have scaled: ‘I wish I had built systems earlier so I wasn’t doing everything myself.’ The morning market handoff is the first step in building those systems.

The work only you can do is worth more than the work anyone could do. Protect your morning for the former.

06
Build a ‘what’s cheap today’ decision system

One of the legitimate reasons the owner must be at the market: daily price and availability knowledge drives the day’s menu. If you can’t go yourself, you need a system for getting that information.

WhatsApp your trusted vendor at 5am: “What’s good today and at what price?” They respond by 5:30am. You’ve decided today’s special by 6am, broadcast it via WhatsApp at 9am, and your buyer has specific instructions for what to prioritise when they arrive.

Over time: track your ingredient prices in a log (see the supplier chaos strategy page). Your buyer can make the ‘is this price fair?’ decision independently once they have 4 weeks of historical price data to compare against.

The market information is the critical input. The physical presence is what you’re trying to reduce.

Your 4-week plan

Here's how to execute everything above, week by week.

week1
Build the vendor relationships
Identify your 2 most reliable current vendors. Start going to them first, every morning.
Introduce yourself properly. Learn their names. Tell them about your restaurant.
After 2 weeks: try your first WhatsApp pre-order with your most trusted vendor.
Track: did the quality and price match your expectation? Refine and repeat.
Ongoing for 4 weeks
week2
Separate your purchasing streams
List every item you currently buy at the market. Categorise as ‘daily fresh’ or ‘weekly scheduled.’
Identify a wholesale dry goods supplier who delivers to your area.
Place your first weekly scheduled dry goods order. Remove those items from your market trip.
Track: how much time did you save this week?
3–4 hrs
week3
Train your buyer
Identify your most reliable staff member or family member as your buyer trainee.
Take them to the market for 5 consecutive days. Explain your standards explicitly.
Give them responsibility for Wednesday’s market run in Week 3.
Debrief afterwards: what was right, what needs adjustment?
5 mornings + 30 min debrief each
week4
Use the saved time
For every morning you don’t go to market: write down what you did with the time instead.
Did you do things that only you can do? Revenue tracking, staff management, menu planning?
After 4 weeks: how has the saved time changed your stress level and the quality of your decisions?
What is the next task you can delegate after the market run?
Reflection, 30 min/week
After 4 weeks, you'll have:
✓
WhatsApp pre-order system working with at least 1 trusted vendor
✓
Dry goods purchasing separated to weekly scheduled delivery
✓
A trained buyer handling at least 2 market runs per week
✓
30+ minutes of morning time recovered and used for owner-only work
✓
A ‘what’s fresh today’ communication system running via WhatsApp
You didn’t start a food business to spend half your life at Manning Market. The system you’ve built gives you back your mornings.

The morning market run is not optional — but your personal presence at it every single day is. The relationships, the systems, and the trained buyer are what make the difference between an owner who’s exhausted by 8am and one who’s ready to run the day.

Start tomorrow: identify your 2 most trusted market vendors. Go to them first. Learn their names. Tell them about your restaurant. That 5-minute conversation is the first step.
