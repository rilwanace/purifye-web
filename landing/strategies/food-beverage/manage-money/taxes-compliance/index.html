“The PHI came once but didn’t find me. I’m not registered and I don’t really know what I need to do.”
“I heard about the excise license costing Rs.15 million. I don’t serve alcohol. But now I’m worried what other licenses I’m missing.”

Sri Lankan F&B compliance is not as complicated as it feels from the outside. Most small operators need fewer licenses than they fear — but the ones they need are mandatory, and operating without them is a real risk. Here is what you actually need.

01
Food Premises Registration — the one you cannot skip

Under the Food Act No. 26 of 1980 and the Food (Registration of Premises) Regulations 2019, every food business operating from physical premises must register with the local food authority: your Municipal Council (MC areas) or Medical Officer of Health (UC/PS areas).

A Public Health Inspector (PHI) inspects your premises before you receive the certificate. You must display the certificate visibly on your premises. Annual renewal required. PHI inspections can occur without notice at any time after registration.

The December 2024 Pettah raid found rat droppings and animals in operating restaurant kitchens — these restaurants faced immediate closure orders. PHI raids also spread on social media rapidly, destroying reputation overnight.

Cloud kitchens operating from shared kitchens like WORX Kitchen: confirm the shared kitchen’s registration status covers your operations. If you operate from your home kitchen and deliver commercially: you are operating in a legal grey area. Registration of your home premises as a food business is technically required.

Register first. Operate after. A PHI closure order is more expensive than registration ever will be.

02
Business Registration — straightforward and required

All businesses operating commercially must be registered. Options: (1) Sole proprietorship — registered with the Registrar of Companies or your local Municipal/Urban Council. (2) Partnership — same registration authority. (3) Private Limited Company — Registrar of Companies; more complex but provides liability protection.

For most small F&B operators, sole proprietorship registration is sufficient and inexpensive. Annual renewal required.

Business registration gives you: the ability to open a business bank account, legal protection in supplier and customer disputes, access to formal SME loans, and legitimacy with delivery platforms (some require business registration for merchant accounts).

Registered businesses have more options. Unregistered businesses have more risk.

03
VAT — only applies above Rs.60 million turnover

VAT in Sri Lanka is 18% (maintained from January 2024). VAT registration is mandatory only for businesses with annual turnover exceeding Rs.60 million (from 2024). Most small Sri Lankan F&B operators are well below this threshold.

If you are below Rs.60 million: you do not need to register for VAT, charge VAT, or file VAT returns. If you are approaching Rs.60 million: consult an accountant immediately, as the transition has significant administrative implications.

Quarterly VAT returns are required for registered businesses. VAT is charged on the sale and claimed back on business inputs. For a delivery-heavy restaurant, this means VAT on your platform commissions is claimable — a not-insignificant recovery if you’re above the threshold.

VAT registration is not your problem unless your annual revenue exceeds Rs.60 million. If it does, get an accountant.

04
SLTDA Registration — only if you want tourist-facing classification

SLTDA (Sri Lanka Tourism Development Authority) registration is required for establishments seeking ‘tourist restaurant’ classification under the Tourism Development Act No. 14 of 1968.

It is NOT required for general local restaurants. If you register with SLTDA, you become subject to the Tourism Development Levy (TDL): 1% of your turnover (excluding service charges and VAT), paid quarterly. A TDL arrears moratorium ended July 2024.

Benefits of SLTDA registration: eligibility to appear on official tourist maps and guides, access to SLTDA-affiliated training programmes, and potential access to tourist-facing platforms. Consider it if: you are in a tourist area (Colombo, Galle, Kandy, Ella), you regularly serve international guests, or you want to be listed in tourism directories.

SLTDA registration is an option, not an obligation — unless you want official tourist classification.

05
The excise license — you probably don’t need it

The retail liquor license in a Municipal area now costs Rs.15 million per year (post January 2024 revision). Application fee: Rs.10,000. Annual hotel licenses (SLTDA-registered, based on room count): Rs.250,000–Rs.1 million.

This is a prohibitive barrier for small independent restaurants. Most don’t serve alcohol — and this is why. If you don’t serve alcohol, you don’t need an excise license. If you want to serve wine or beer: the economics only work if your alcohol revenue justifies Rs.15 million per year.

Poya day alcohol ban: approximately 12 days per year (every full moon), alcohol sales are prohibited by government order. Vesak (May full moon) also bans fresh meat sales and slaughterhouse operations. These are legally enforced, not optional.

The excise license is designed for large operators. For most small restaurants, it’s simply not worth it.

06
EPF/ETF and income tax — the ongoing obligations

If you have employees: EPF (employer 12% of wages) and ETF (employer 3%) are mandatory monthly contributions to the Department of Labour. Non-compliance is a legal risk — back-payments, penalties, and disputes when employees leave are disproportionately costly.

Income tax: individual businesses are taxed at applicable personal income tax rates. Companies at 30% corporate tax rate. Sole proprietors must file an income tax return annually. Most small F&B operators underreport income — this creates risk if the IRD begins more aggressive enforcement (which has been discussed in 2023–2024 as part of Sri Lanka’s IMF-conditioned revenue targets).

Practical advice: hire an accountant (a CIMA/ACCA-qualified accountant costs Rs.5,000–15,000/month for basic F&B bookkeeping). The cost is recovered many times over through correct tax filing, EPF compliance, and avoiding the penalties that come from non-compliance caught years later.

Pay an accountant Rs.10,000/month. The alternative — discovering years of non-compliance — costs far more.

Your 4-week plan

Here's how to execute everything above, week by week.

week1
Audit your compliance
Do you have Food Premises Registration? If not, contact your local MC/MOH today to start the process.
Is your business registered (sole proprietorship or company)? If not, visit the Registrar of Companies.
Is your annual revenue above or below Rs.60 million? If below, VAT does not apply to you.
Do you have employees? If yes, are you paying EPF/ETF? Check your status with the Department of Labour.
2–3 hrs
week2
Get the licenses you’re missing
Food Premises Registration: book a PHI inspection. Ensure your kitchen meets basic hygiene standards.
Business registration: complete the application for sole proprietorship if not done.
If near SLTDA-tourist area: request an information package from SLTDA about registration.
Hire an accountant for a one-time compliance review if you’re unsure of any obligation.
4–8 hrs (spread over 2 weeks)
week3
Build your compliance calendar
Set annual reminders for: Food Premises Registration renewal (date?), Business registration renewal, EPF/ETF monthly submission deadline.
Add Poya days to your business calendar (12 per year). Note which ones affect your alcohol or meat service.
Add Vesak (May) as a full-impact planning day: no alcohol, no fresh meat, plan a special veg menu.
Keep a compliance folder (physical or Google Drive) with all license copies.
2 hrs
week4
Ongoing maintenance
Pay EPF/ETF on the 15th of each month. Set a monthly reminder.
File annual income tax return before the IRD deadline.
Renew Food Premises Registration before expiry (note: display it prominently).
Review compliance status annually with your accountant.
1–2 hrs/month
After 4 weeks, you'll have:
✓
Food Premises Registration confirmed or application started
✓
Business registration confirmed
✓
VAT threshold confirmed (above or below Rs.60M — you now know which)
✓
EPF/ETF compliance status confirmed
✓
A compliance calendar with all renewal dates set
