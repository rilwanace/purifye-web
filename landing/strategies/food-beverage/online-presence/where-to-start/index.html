“I’ve been running this restaurant for 4 years and I still don’t have a Facebook page. I don’t even know where to start.”
“My competitor down the road has 3,000 Facebook followers and I’m sending people looking on WhatsApp. Something has to change.”

Over 70% of small Sri Lankan businesses have no social media presence. That is your competitive advantage — if you act. You don’t need a website, a designer, or a marketing budget to build an online presence that drives customers. You need 4 hours this weekend and a consistent habit after that.

01
Google Business Profile — set this up before anything else

Google Maps is the primary local discovery tool for Sri Lankan consumers and tourists. When someone searches ‘restaurants near me’ or ‘Sri Lankan food Colombo,’ your Google Business Profile is what determines whether you appear.

Go to business.google.com. Create a free profile for your business. Verify it (Google sends a postcard or code). Add: your correct address, opening hours, phone number, WhatsApp link, and at least 10 photos of your food.

This is free. It takes 2 hours. It is the highest-ROI marketing action you can take today.

Restaurants with complete Google Business Profiles with 50+ photos appear significantly more often in search results than those with sparse listings. Hotels and guest houses with tourist guests: also add your Google Business Profile to TripAdvisor (8,257+ Sri Lanka restaurants listed). For tourist-facing establishments, TripAdvisor is critical.

Before any social media: be on Google Maps. That is where customers search first.

02
Facebook Business Page — the broadest reach in Sri Lanka

Facebook has the largest active user base of any social media platform in Sri Lanka, and is the dominant channel outside Colombo. A Facebook Business Page is free, indexed by Google, and allows customers to find you, check your menu, and message you directly.

How to set it up: go to facebook.com/pages/create. Business name = your restaurant name. Category = Restaurant. Add your address, phone, opening hours. Post your first 3 photos of food. That’s your launch.

Facebook is especially important for: suburban and rural customers (Gampaha, Kandy, Negombo, Kurunegala — Facebook more influential than Instagram outside Colombo), families and customers 30+, event promotions, and community groups. Your Facebook page is also where local community groups will share your posts when people ask ‘good restaurants in [area]?’

Facebook is where most of Sri Lanka still finds local businesses. Be there.

03
Instagram — the food photo platform

Instagram is the primary visual food discovery platform in Colombo. For cafes, specialty restaurants, cloud kitchens, brunch spots, and premium casual dining: Instagram is non-negotiable.

Create a free Instagram Business account. Username: your restaurant name (no spaces). Bio: 3 lines maximum. What you serve. Where you are. How to order (WhatsApp number or delivery app link). Post 9 photos in your first week: your 3 best dishes from 3 different angles. That’s your Instagram foundation.

Food photography tips for non-photographers: natural light (near a window) is everything. Simple backgrounds (a clean table, a wooden board, a plain plate). Fill the frame with the food. A Rs.0 setup with your existing phone and a window produces photos good enough for Instagram.

Your food looks better than you think. You just need natural light and a clean background.

04
WhatsApp Business — your ordering and communication hub

WhatsApp Business (free app) turns your WhatsApp into a business tool: a business profile with your name, address, opening hours, and menu. An auto-reply for new messages. A catalogue of your menu items with photos and prices. Broadcast lists for sending your daily menu to regulars.

This is how most successful small food operators in Sri Lanka — home chefs, caterers, cloud kitchen operators — manage customer communication. It requires no website, no app, and no subscription.

Setup time: 1 hour. 1. Download WhatsApp Business. 2. Set up your business profile. 3. Write your auto-reply: ‘Hi! Thanks for messaging [name]. Here’s our menu…’ 4. Create your catalogue with your top 5 items. 5. Import your existing customer contacts and create your first broadcast list.

WhatsApp Business is the most widely-used small business tool in Sri Lanka. Start using it like one.

05
TikTok — optional but powerful for rapid growth

Sri Lanka has 5.2 million TikTok users (2024–2025) with food content among the highest-engagement categories. Short-form video (under 60 seconds) generates 2.3x higher engagement than static posts. Campaigns tied to local culture see ~40% higher engagement.

TikTok for beginners: download the app. Create a business account. Your first video: 30 seconds of cooking something in your kitchen. No script. No editing. Just show the food being made. That’s it. Post it.

Tower Burger (Wellawatta/Negombo) uses TikTok actively for menu launches. K24 Cloud Kitchen uses TikTok to build identity around their biryani. The common thread: authentic, unpolished content often outperforms produced content. Showing your real kitchen, your real food, and your real personality is the strategy.

Your most viewed video will be the one you almost didn’t post. Post it anyway.

06
Yamu.lk — get listed on Sri Lanka’s food review portal

Yamu.lk is Sri Lanka’s primary local restaurant review portal — the first destination many Colombo residents check before choosing a restaurant. Listing on Yamu is free. Getting featured by their editorial team drives meaningful discovery traffic.

To get listed: visit yamu.lk and submit your restaurant details. To get featured: email their editorial team with your best food photos, a compelling story about your restaurant, and an invitation for a visit. Yamu’s editorial features (‘Best Kottu in Colombo,’ ‘Hidden Gems,’ seasonal guides) drive substantial foot traffic to the restaurants they cover.

For tourist-area restaurants: TripAdvisor is equally important. TripAdvisor lists 8,257+ Sri Lanka restaurants. Claim your listing at tripadvisor.com/owners. Add photos, respond to reviews. A restaurant ranking in the top 10% on TripAdvisor for its area sees a measurable tourist traffic lift.

Yamu and TripAdvisor are already ranking restaurants in your area. Make sure yours is on the list.

week3

Post 3 times per week on Instagram/Facebook. Minimum: 1 photo per post.
Send WhatsApp broadcast daily at 9am with the day’s menu.
Respond to every comment and DM within 24 hours.
Ask every happy customer to follow you on Instagram or leave a Google review.
30–45 min/day

Month 2: assess and optimise
Check Google Business Profile insights: how many searches and calls this month?

Invite one local nano-influencer for a complimentary meal.
1 hr
