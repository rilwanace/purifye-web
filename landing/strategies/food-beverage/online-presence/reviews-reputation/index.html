Strategies›Food & Beverage›Improve Online Presence›Bad reviews are hurting my reputation
“Someone left a 1-star review saying the food was cold and I don’t even know if it was our fault or the delivery driver’s. Now it’s just sitting there.”
“I have 4 bad reviews from last year that drag my rating down. I don’t know how to fight back against them.”

A bad review that goes unanswered is a bad review that speaks for your business. A bad review that gets a gracious, professional response often makes your business look more trustworthy, not less — because people see how you handle problems, not just whether problems happen.

01
Respond to every review — positive and negative — within 24 hours

Customers who leave reviews are 3x more likely to return if their review receives a personalised response. A response to a positive review: “Thank you [Name]! That made our whole team’s day. We’ll have your favourite [dish] ready whenever you come back.” This costs you 30 seconds and creates a customer who feels seen.

A response to a negative review requires more care — but is even more important. Future customers read your responses more carefully than they read the reviews themselves. They want to know: does this business care? Does it take responsibility?

Most small Sri Lankan restaurants don’t respond to reviews at all. Responding puts you in the top 20% of responsive businesses on Google and Facebook in your area. It signals professionalism and accountability — things chains genuinely can’t fake.

Your response to a bad review is more powerful than the bad review itself. Use that power.

02
The four-step bad review response

Step 1: Thank the reviewer. Never start defensive. “Thank you for taking the time to share this.” Step 2: Acknowledge specifically what they experienced. Not a generic apology — the specific issue they raised. “I’m sorry your kottu arrived cold.” Not: “Sorry to hear you didn’t enjoy your experience.”

Step 3: Explain briefly (if there’s a genuine reason) or commit to fixing it. “We use insulated bags for all deliveries but we’ll review this order with our kitchen team.” Step 4: Move offline. “Please contact us directly at [WhatsApp/phone] so we can make this right.”

Never: argue, deny, shift blame to the customer, or attack the review as fake. Even if the review is wrong, arguing in public makes you look petty and drives other customers away. If the review is genuinely fake or defamatory: report it to Google/Facebook via the platform (flag for review). This is the only legitimate public channel.

Thank. Acknowledge. Explain or commit. Move offline. Four steps. Every negative review, every time.

03
Build a systematic positive review campaign

The most effective response to bad reviews is more good reviews. A 1-star review in a sea of 100 four-and five-star reviews is marginal. A 1-star review among 8 total reviews is catastrophic.

Build the habit: ask every happy customer for a review. The best time: at the moment of peak satisfaction — when their food arrives and they say “this is amazing.” “That means so much! If you have 30 seconds, a Google review really helps us — you can find us by searching [Restaurant Name].”

Add a QR code on your tables and receipt that links directly to your Google review page. WhatsApp broadcast to your regulars once every 3 months: “If you’ve enjoyed your meals with us, a Google review takes 30 seconds and means the world to us. Here’s the link: [link]”

Target: add 5 genuine reviews per month. At that rate, a cluster of old bad reviews becomes less visible within 90 days.

Drown the bad reviews in good ones. This is the only permanent solution.

04
Deal with delivery review complaints differently

One of the most common sources of negative reviews for Sri Lankan restaurants: food that was cold or damaged on delivery — a problem that is often the delivery driver’s fault, not the restaurant’s.

Your response: still take responsibility publicly. “We’re sorry your order didn’t arrive as expected. Our food leaves the kitchen hot and properly packaged, but delivery quality is sometimes outside our direct control. Please contact us directly — we’d like to make it right.”

Your internal action: if a specific driver regularly delivers your orders late or damaged, flag this with the platform’s merchant support. Both PickMe Food and Uber Eats have escalation channels for repeated delivery issues.

Packaging matters here: invest in leak-proof, insulated containers for your delivery items. A container that keeps food hot for 30 minutes and doesn’t spill removes 60% of delivery complaint causes. The extra Rs.15–25 per delivery container is recouped immediately in reduced complaints.

You can’t control every delivery. You can control your packaging and your response.

05
Monitor what people are saying about you

Most small restaurant owners only find out about a bad review when someone tells them or when their rating drops. Set up a monitoring system that takes 2 minutes per day.

On Google: the Google Business Profile app sends you a notification when a new review is posted. Turn on these notifications and respond the same day. On Facebook: go to your Page, click Reviews. On TripAdvisor: set up email alerts for new reviews.

A December 2024 PHI raid in Pettah spread on social media within hours and destroyed multiple restaurant reputations before the owners were even aware of the coverage. Monitoring your online reputation daily is the only way to catch and respond to crises early — before the negative narrative becomes the only narrative.

You cannot respond to what you don’t know exists. Monitor every day.

06
Use negative reviews as a quality improvement tool

A cluster of similar complaints in your reviews is market research. Three reviews in a month mentioning ‘slow service’ tells you something your staff might not be saying. Multiple ‘cold food on delivery’ complaints tell you your packaging needs upgrading. ‘Inconsistent quality’ means your recipes need standardisation.

Monthly review audit: read all reviews from the past month. Categorise them: food quality / speed / service / delivery / pricing / atmosphere. Identify the most common complaint category. Fix that one thing before next month.

The feedback you get for free in reviews is worth more than any survey you could run. Treat every negative review as a direct message from the customer who almost stopped coming back. They cared enough to write. That means they might also care enough to return if you fix the problem.

A negative review is a customer telling you how to keep them. Listen to it.

Your 4-week plan

Here's how to execute everything above, week by week.

week1
Immediate response
Right now: open your Google Business Profile, Facebook Page, and TripAdvisor (if applicable).
Read every unanswered review. Draft a response to each using the 4-step formula.
Post your responses. All of them. Today.
Turn on review notifications on all platforms.
2–3 hrs
week2
Build the review volume
Create a QR code linking to your Google review page (qr-code-generator.com, free).
Print it on a small card. Place on every table and include in delivery bags.
Send a WhatsApp broadcast to your regulars asking for a Google review (include the link).
Ask 3 customers in person today. This week: aim for 10 new reviews.
2 hrs
week3
Monthly review audit
On the 1st of every month: read all new reviews. Categorise by complaint type.
Identify the most common complaint. Assign it to someone to fix.
Respond to every new review within 24 hours (daily check, not monthly).
Track your rating: is it trending up, flat, or down?
30 min/month
week4
Prevention
Brief your kitchen and front-of-house staff: what are the top 3 complaints in your reviews?
Fix the top complaint this month. Brief staff on the new standard.
Upgrade delivery packaging if cold/spill complaints are common.
Set a target: a 4.5+ Google rating by end of the next 3 months.
2–3 hrs
