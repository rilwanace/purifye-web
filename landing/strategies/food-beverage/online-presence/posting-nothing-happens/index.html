“I post a photo of today’s lunch special and maybe 3 people react. I have 800 followers but it feels like nobody sees it.”
“My competitor has half my followers but every post gets 50 comments. I don’t understand what they’re doing differently.”

Posting on social media and getting no engagement is usually not a content problem — it’s a format problem. Sri Lankan food audiences respond to specific types of content. Understanding what works changes the game completely.

01
The algorithm rewards video — stop posting only static photos

Short-form video (Instagram Reels, TikTok) generates 2.3x higher engagement than static photo posts. Instagram’s algorithm actively promotes Reels over static posts — a Reel from a 500-follower account can reach 5,000 people. A static photo from a 5,000-follower account might reach 300.

Your first Reel: 30 seconds. Your kitchen in action. Chopping, stirring, plating. Add a trending audio track (use whatever is popular in Instagram’s audio library this week). No script, no editing required. Post it.

The most-shared food content in Sri Lanka: (1) Cooking in action: behind-the-scenes kitchen footage. (2) The reveal: food going from raw ingredients to finished dish. (3) ‘First bite’ reactions: a customer or staff member eating something and reacting authentically. All three are free to produce with any smartphone.

One Reel per week outperforms seven static photos. Start shifting your posting format.

02
Use cultural and seasonal hooks — they get 40% more engagement

Research on Sri Lankan social media confirms: campaigns tied to local culture see ~40% higher engagement. Sinhala/Tamil New Year food posts. Ramadan Iftar specials. Vesak vegetarian menus. Christmas set menus. Avurudu kiribath and kavum recipes. These posts connect with something your audience already cares about.

Schedule these cultural content posts 1–2 weeks before the event. A post about your Avurudu special on April 10th gets 40% more reach than the same post on April 13th. Timing is everything.

Build a cultural content calendar: January: Duruthu Poya, Thai Pongal. February: Valentine’s Day. March: Madin Poya, Holi. April: Sinhala/Tamil New Year, Ramadan. May: Vesak (vegetarian content). December: Christmas. Each of these is a 40%-higher-engagement content opportunity.

Your food is already part of Sri Lankan culture. Post it that way.

03
Ask questions — engagement comes from conversation

Posts that ask questions get dramatically more comments than posts that just show food. Instead of: [photo of kottu]. Post: [photo of kottu] + caption: ‘Chicken or egg kottu? Which team are you on? Let us know below! 👇’

The question doesn’t need to be profound. It needs to be answerable in one word. ‘Rice or string hoppers for breakfast?’ ‘Spicy or mild — how do you take your devilled chicken?’ ‘What’s your favourite thing on our menu? We’re updating it!’

Comments signal to the algorithm that your post is engaging, which causes it to show the post to more people. The more comments you get, the wider your reach becomes — at no cost. Every comment you reply to creates a further signal. Respond to every comment within 2 hours of posting.

COPY-PASTE THIS — Caption for any food photo
We’re trying something new — [dish name], available this week only. Would you try this? 🍫 Drop a YES or NO below! Order via WhatsApp: [number] or find us at [location].

The algorithm is listening. Give it comments to reward.

04
Post at the right times

Sri Lankan social media peak usage times (estimated based on regional patterns): 7–9am (morning commute / breakfast social media scroll), 12–1pm (lunch break), 5–7pm (after work), 9–10pm (evening relaxation).

For F&B: the most effective post time is 11am–12pm, just before the lunch decision window. A photo of today’s special posted at 11:30am reaches people exactly when they are deciding where to eat.

Use Instagram Insights (free, available for Business accounts) to see when your specific followers are most active. Check it monthly. The answer varies by audience. Your 10am-posting competitor might not be wrong — but you can find the time that works best for your specific audience.

Your daily menu post at 11:30am is the most effective single marketing action you can take each day.

05
Repost customer content — it performs better than your own

User-generated content (photos and videos customers post of your food) is the highest-performing content category for most restaurant accounts. It is free. It is credible. And it comes with a built-in story: a real customer loved your food enough to post about it.

Create the habit: at the end of every day, search for posts that tag your restaurant or use your location. Repost the best ones to your Stories (with permission/credit). Every month, take your best customer photo and repost it to your feed with a caption: ‘[Customer name]’s photo made our week! Thank you for sharing.’

Encouraging customers to tag you: add a small sign at your table or counter: ‘Tag us on Instagram @[handle] and we’ll feature you on our page!’ This turns your happiest customers into your most effective marketers.

Your best content is already being created by your customers. You just need to find it and share it.

06
Accept that organic reach is declining — and adapt

Instagram and Facebook organic reach has been declining globally since 2019. Sri Lankan restaurant owners who launched pages in 2018–2020 report seeing 50% less reach from the same follower base. The platform wants you to pay for advertising.

The two practical responses: (1) Shift effort to formats the algorithm currently favours: Reels, Stories, TikTok. (2) Build channels that don’t depend on the algorithm: WhatsApp broadcast list (100% delivery rate), direct Google Business Profile visibility (search-driven, not algorithm-driven).

Your WhatsApp broadcast list is the most algorithm-proof marketing channel you have. Every person on that list gets your message. No algorithm decides who sees it. A broadcast list of 200 customers is more valuable than 2,000 Instagram followers whose feed you are no longer guaranteed to appear in.

Don’t build your business on rented land. WhatsApp broadcast list, Google Business Profile — these are yours.
