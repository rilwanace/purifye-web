Strategies›Retail Shop›Improve My Online Presence›I have no online presence and don't know where to start
My competitor three streets over has a Facebook page with 2,000 followers and I have nothing. I don't even have a smartphone good enough for proper photos.
I know I need to be online but every time I think about it I don't know which platform, what to post, or whether it will even bring customers.
My niece keeps telling me to get on TikTok. I'm 47. I don't know if that's my world.

You don't need to be everywhere. You need to be on one platform, doing it well, consistently. Here's a practical starting sequence for a small Sri Lankan retailer with no online presence.

01
Google Business Profile first — it's free, permanent, and works while you sleep

Before any social media, set up Google Business Profile. When someone searches for your shop name, or searches 'clothing shop Maharagama', a Google Business listing makes you appear on the right side of Google results and on Google Maps. This is the highest-value, lowest-effort online presence a small retailer can create.

Go to business.google.com and click 'Manage now.' Enter your shop name, address, phone number, and category (retail shop, clothing store, grocery, etc.). Add 5–10 photos of your shopfront, interior, and products. Add your opening hours. This takes one hour and is completely free.

Ask 5 regular customers to leave a Google review. Even 3–5 genuine reviews from real customers significantly increases trust for new searchers. Most customers are happy to do this when asked personally — they never think to do it unprompted.

Google Business Profile takes one hour to set up and starts appearing in 'near me' searches within a week.

02
WhatsApp Business is the easiest first step — you already use WhatsApp

If you use WhatsApp personally, migrating to WhatsApp Business is a free upgrade that takes 10 minutes. It adds a business profile, product catalog, away messages, and broadcast list functionality to the messaging app you already know.

Download WhatsApp Business and set up your profile: Shop name, address, business category, opening hours, website or Facebook link if you have one. Then add your 20 most popular items as a catalog — name, photo, price. This catalog shows when customers click your profile.

Start a broadcast list with your existing contacts. Add every customer whose number you have. This becomes your most direct marketing channel — messages arrive in the customer's personal WhatsApp inbox, not a social media feed they may or may not scroll.

WhatsApp Business reach is fundamentally different from social media. A Facebook post reaches 5–10% of your followers. A WhatsApp broadcast reaches 95%+ of your contact list. Start with WhatsApp because the conversion rate from broadcast to in-store visit is meaningfully higher.

A WhatsApp broadcast message reaches 95%+ of your contact list. A Facebook post reaches 2–5% of followers.

03
Set up a Facebook Page — this is your second step, not your first

8.2 million Sri Lankans use Facebook — the dominant social platform for small retail discovery. A business page allows you to post products, run ads, and be found by people who search for shops in your category in your area.

Create a page at facebook.com/pages/create. Name it exactly as your shop is known: 'Nilu's Fashion House, Maharagama.' Add a clear shopfront photo as the cover. Add your phone number, address, and a short business description. Post one photo immediately — new stock with a price.

Start posting 3 times per week: Monday new arrivals, Wednesday product highlight, Friday weekend promotion. Consistency for 4 weeks builds a modest following from your local area. Don't spend money on ads until you have at least 10 posts on the page.

Your phone already has a better camera than most small retailers use — the gap is lighting and background, not equipment.

04
Add Instagram only if your category is visual — fashion, food, cosmetics, gifts

Instagram has 1.95 million Sri Lankan users, heavily weighted toward urban women aged 18–35. For fashion, cosmetics, food, and gifts — where the product is inherently visual and aspirational — Instagram reaches an audience that Facebook misses.

Create an Instagram Business account and link it to your Facebook page. This lets you post to both platforms simultaneously. Your Facebook followers and Instagram followers can be different people — this is cross-platform reach for zero extra effort.

Your first 12 Instagram posts should show your product in use. Not stock on a shelf — a styled outfit, a finished makeup look, a plated food item. Aspirational content on Instagram, practical content on Facebook. The same product can serve both.

Post consistency beats post quality every time in the algorithm.

05
Good photos are your most important online asset — how to take them with your phone

Poor photography is the #1 reason small retailers don't get results from social media. Blurry, dark, cluttered photos communicate the opposite of quality even when the product is excellent. You don't need a camera or a photographer — you need two things: natural light and a clean background.

Set up a simple photo corner in your shop. A plain white or cream wall, good natural light from a window, and a table or hanger at the right height. This takes 30 minutes to set up and produces dramatically better photos than anything taken in the cluttered main shop area.

For clothing: photograph on a person where possible. A garment on a body converts 3–5x better than a flat lay on a table. If you don't have a model, ask a staff member or family member. The person doesn't need to be a model — they need to wear the item well and not look uncomfortable.

Batch your weekly content on Sunday evening. 10 photos taken in 20 minutes powers 7 days of content.

06
Create a repeatable posting schedule — consistency beats quality every time

The biggest failure mode for small retailers on social media is starting with 10 posts, then going silent for 3 weeks, then posting 5 more, then silence again. An inconsistent presence is worse than no presence for algorithm reach and customer trust.

Commit to one post per day on WhatsApp Story and three posts per week on Facebook or Instagram. Not more. This is the minimum sustainable for a solo retailer. Build the habit before expanding the volume.

Batch your content on Sunday evenings. Take 5–10 photos of the week's new stock on Sunday. That content lasts the whole week. Scheduling takes 20 minutes. You then post every other day without having to think about it each morning.

New at Glow Beauty Corner this week — Korean BB cream SPF 50, LKR 2,400. Rice water shampoo set, LKR 1,850. Vitamin C serum, LKR 3,200. WhatsApp to hold or come in — open 10am–8pm Mon–Sat.

Instagram is for aspirational product discovery. Facebook is for practical promotions. WhatsApp is for purchase conversion.

Start with what's free and permanent: Google Business Profile and WhatsApp Business. These two tools together cover search discovery and direct customer communication — the two most valuable online functions for small retail. Add Facebook when those are running. Add Instagram if your category is visual. Add TikTok if you want new-customer discovery at scale.

You don't need to be on every platform. You need to be findable on Google, responsive on WhatsApp, and visible on Facebook — consistently, for 90 days. By then you'll know which channel sends you real customers and which one to ignore.
