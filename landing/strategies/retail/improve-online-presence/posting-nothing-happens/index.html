Strategies›Retail Shop›Improve My Online Presence›I post on social media but nothing happens
I posted a photo of my new saree collection on Tuesday. 9 likes. 8 of them are my family.
I don't know what I'm doing wrong. The photos are clear, I write a description, I post at what I think is the right time. Zero new customers.
My competitor posts the same kind of photos and gets 200 likes and comments asking for prices. I don't understand why theirs works and mine doesn't.

Getting likes from family is not the same as getting customers from social media. Here's why most small retailer posts don't convert and what to change.

01
Your current followers are mostly people who already know you — that's the problem

A small Facebook page with 50–200 followers is almost entirely made up of existing contacts — friends, family, existing customers. Posting to them generates likes from them. It doesn't generate new customers because it's reaching the people who already know you.

The solution is not better posts — it's reaching people who don't follow you. This requires either paid distribution (ads), algorithmic distribution (high engagement that pushes posts to non-followers), or network distribution (a follower shares your post with their contacts).

Your current page growth strategy should be: invite every customer to follow the page or join the WhatsApp broadcast, and run one small ad per month to reach a local audience. The broadcast reaches existing customers. The ad reaches new ones.

Price hidden = purchase friction. Price visible in caption = zero friction.

02
Show the price in every single post — this is the #1 missing element

The most common reason Sri Lankan retail social media posts don't convert: the price is not visible. A customer sees a beautiful saree photo, wants to know the price, has to ask in comments or WhatsApp, waits for a response, and by that time has scrolled past. The purchase didn't happen.

Write the price in the post caption — always. 'Cotton saree, LKR 3,500. Available in blue, cream, and maroon. WhatsApp to hold.' This is the complete message. Price-visible posts on Sri Lankan retail generate 3–5x more purchase inquiries than price-hidden posts.

For fashion: include size availability. 'Sizes 8–16 in stock' tells a customer whether to bother contacting you. This single piece of information reduces irrelevant inquiries and increases relevant ones.

2–5% organic reach on Facebook means 200 followers → 4–10 post views. That's why nothing happens.

03
Every post needs a specific call to action — not 'for more info'

'For more info, WhatsApp us' is the weakest call to action in retail. More info about what? WhatsApp for what purpose? Vague calls to action produce vague responses (or none).

Replace 'for more info' with one of these specific calls to action: 'WhatsApp [number] to hold this item.' 'Come in today, we're open till 8pm.' 'Reply to this message with your size.' 'Limited stock — comment below to reserve.'

The best-performing Sri Lankan retail posts end with exactly one action the customer should take, and friction is zero. 'WhatsApp Rashmi on 077-XXXXXXX to hold' is zero friction. The customer can click the number, open WhatsApp, and send a message in 20 seconds.

A Rs.500 local Facebook ad reaches 1,000–3,000 people in your area who don't know you exist.

04
A Rs.500 local Facebook ad reaches 1,000–3,000 people in your area — do it once

Organic reach (posting without paying) on Facebook currently reaches 2–5% of your followers. A page with 200 followers gets 4–10 views per post. That's not enough to generate new customers. Paid reach solves this problem directly and cheaply.

Create one boosted post per month targeting a 3km radius around your shop. Choose your best product photo, write a clear caption with price and call to action. Click 'Boost Post', set LKR 500–1,000 budget, target women/men aged 18–45 within 3km. This reaches 1,000–3,000 people who live near you and don't know you exist.

Measure: how many people WhatsApp you after the ad? Even 2–3 new customer inquiries from a Rs.500 ad is a positive return if they convert to a sale. Track this each month to understand what post type and what audience generates the best return.

Post at 7pm, not 9am. Evening Sri Lankan social media usage is 3x higher than morning.

05
Engagement signals tell the algorithm to show your posts more — how to get them

Facebook and Instagram algorithms show posts to more people when those posts get early engagement — comments, shares, saves, and clicks in the first 30–60 minutes after posting. A post that gets 5 comments in the first hour gets shown to 5–10x more people than a post that gets none.

Post at peak engagement times: 7–9pm in Sri Lanka. Most people check social media in the evening after work. A post at 7pm collects engagement from 7–10pm — a 3-hour window. A post at 9am collects engagement during a lower-attention period.

Ask your 5 most engaged followers to comment on your posts. Even 'Love this!' from two people in the first hour signals the algorithm to expand reach. A WhatsApp message to 5 regulars: 'I just posted our new collection on Facebook — would you mind clicking the link and letting me know what you think?' is worth 3x the organic reach of your post alone.

Early engagement in the first hour signals the algorithm to expand reach — ask 5 regulars to comment.

06
TikTok and Instagram Reels are the only platforms with organic discovery still working

TikTok and Instagram Reels are the only major platforms where a new account with zero followers can reach thousands of people organically. The algorithm serves short video content to users based on interest signals, not follower count. A 30-second product video from a new account can get 5,000–50,000 views if the content resonates.

Post one product video per week on TikTok or Instagram Reels. Hold up the product, show the price, describe it briefly in Sinhala or Tamil for local reach, and end with your WhatsApp number. 60 seconds or less. Natural lighting, no music needed, direct and honest.

For fashion: show the item on a person walking, turning, with the garment in motion. Video showing how a saree falls or how a dress fits generates direct purchase inquiries at 5–10x the rate of a static photo. This is the most underused format in Sri Lankan small retail.

TikTok and Reels reward new accounts. A 30-second product video can outperform a year of photo posts.

The gap between posting and converting is almost always one of three things: price not visible, weak call to action, or wrong audience. Fix price visibility and call to action in every post this week. Fix audience reach with a Rs.500 Facebook ad. Measure what changes. Iterate from there.
