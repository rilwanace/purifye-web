The Sri Lankan retail calendar is one of the most predictable in the world. Once you know it, you can plan inventory, cash, and promotions around it — instead of reacting to it each time.

01
Map the eight demand events that drive Sri Lankan retail

Sinhala and Tamil New Year (April) is the single biggest retail event — food, clothing, gifts, household goods all spike. Christmas and year-end (December) is the second peak. School opening (January) drives stationery, bags, uniforms. Wedding season (June–August) lifts clothing, cosmetics, gifts. Vesak (May) drives vegetarian food and white clothing. Ramadan/Eid spikes for areas with Muslim communities. Diwali (October/November) for Tamil and Indian-origin communities. And the salary week pattern (25th–1st of each month) creates a mini-peak every month.

Map which of these events affect your specific category. Not every event hits every shop. Grocery benefits from almost all of them. Clothing has peak at Avurudu, Christmas, and wedding season. Stationery is almost entirely January.

Write this calendar on paper with stock order dates 4 weeks ahead of each event. Buy Avurudu stock in March. Buy school supplies in December. This simple discipline prevents the most common seasonal error — ordering too late.

02
Avurudu is your biggest event — prepare 4 weeks early

Consumer spending surges across food, clothing, gifts, and household goods in April. Banks and retailers run card discount schemes for 2–4 weeks around Avurudu. Government distributes festival advances to public sector employees. For most small retailers, April will be your best month if you're stocked for it.

Build your Avurudu stock by 1 March at the latest. Avurudu gift packs, festival clothing, kiribath ingredients, sweets — all of this needs to be on shelf when customers start buying in the last week of March.

Create a WhatsApp Avurudu promotion broadcast. Send it on 1 April and again on 10 April. Show gift pack photos with prices. Offer to set aside specific items. This single broadcast can generate 30–50% of your Avurudu revenue from regulars alone.

Avurudu special at Nilu's Fashion House — new saree collection from LKR 3,500, gift sets available for LKR 2,800–6,500. We're putting aside requests — WhatsApp us before 15 April to hold your pick. Wishing you and your family a blessed New Year.

The salary week pattern is free intelligence — schedule new stock arrivals for the 23rd–25th of every month.

03
Use the salary week pattern every single month

Private sector salaries mostly land 25th–1st of each month. Government salaries around 25th–26th. Grocery and FMCG shops see a notable spending spike in the last few days of the month and first few days of the next. Mid-month is your lull.

Schedule your new stock arrivals for 23rd–25th of each month. Fresh arrivals visible on shelves when customers have just been paid drives impulse purchases that mid-month arrivals miss.

Send your WhatsApp promotion broadcast on the 24th or 25th. 'New stock in for the month — come see us this weekend.' This timing converts at noticeably higher rates than broadcasts sent in the middle of the month.

May–June is the worst retail month. Use it to clear dead stock and fix your shop.

04
Use May–June and August–September strategically — don't just survive them

May–June is the post-Avurudu lull — the slowest retail period of the year. Consumers have spent on the April festival and are recovering. August–September is the secondary slow period before the year-end buildup. These are predictable. You can plan for them.

Clear dead stock during slow periods. Discounts of 20–50% move items that are tying up capital. The cash from clearance pays for the next peak's fresh inventory. Better to sell at cost than to hold dead stock through another quarter.

Use slow periods for shop improvements. Reorganise the shelves. Deep clean. Update your WhatsApp catalog. Fix the sign that's been crooked for three months. These are things impossible to do during April — do them in May.

December and January are back-to-back peaks. Stock both simultaneously from 1 December.

05
December and January are back-to-back peaks — don't recover between them

Christmas and year-end is your second biggest peak. Then January school opening immediately follows — stationery, bags, uniforms, shoes. If your shop covers any of these categories, December–January is a 6-week continuous peak period.

Stock both events simultaneously from 1 December. Christmas gift items and school supplies can coexist on the shelf — they serve different purposes and often different customers.

Note what school items parents asked for that you didn't have in January. Write it down. Order it in December next year. School stationery has extremely predictable demand — the Ministry of Education's uniform and stationery requirements barely change year to year.

A festival bundle increases basket size without requiring new customers.

06
Create festival bundles to increase basket size at peak moments

Bundling is one of the most effective tools in small retail because it increases average transaction value without requiring new customers. An Avurudu hamper of 8 grocery items at a slight discount feels like good value to the customer and moves more stock for you.

Create 2–3 pre-packed gift bundles for each major festival. Avurudu grocery hamper (LKR 2,500), Eid sweet pack (LKR 1,800), Christmas gift set (LKR 3,200). Pre-pack them so customers can grab and go. Display them prominently from 2 weeks before the festival.

Photograph your bundles and post on WhatsApp and Facebook. 'Limited Avurudu hampers — only 25 made. WhatsApp to reserve yours.' Artificial scarcity on a genuinely limited batch is honest and effective.

Glow Beauty Corner Avurudu special — skincare gift set: face serum + Korean sunscreen + lip balm, LKR 4,800 (save LKR 650). Only 20 sets made. WhatsApp Rashmi to hold yours before they go.
