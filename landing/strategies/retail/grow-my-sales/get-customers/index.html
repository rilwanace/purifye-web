Location is already set — so what else drives foot traffic? Here are 6 channels that are actually working for small Sri Lankan retailers right now.

01
Set up Google Business Profile — it's free and almost nobody has done it

Low adoption among traditional small retailers means this is a genuine competitive gap. A free Google Business Profile makes your shop appear in Google Search and Maps when someone nearby searches for what you sell. Most of your competitors don't have one.

Go to business.google.com and claim your listing. Add your shop name, address, phone number, opening hours, and at least 5 photos. This takes 30 minutes and starts showing your shop in 'near me' searches within a week.

Ask your first 5 satisfied customers to leave a Google review. Even 3–4 genuine reviews make you look more established than 95% of small shops in your area.

Google Business Profile is free and most of your competitors don't have one. That's a gap.

02
Use Facebook to reach the 2–5km around your shop

8.2 million Sri Lankans use Facebook — 48% of adults 18+. For small retail, it's the dominant social platform. The most effective use is not posting to your existing followers but running localized ads targeting your specific catchment area.

Create a Facebook Business page for the shop. Name it clearly (Nilu's Fashion House, Maharagama), add cover photo, opening hours, and your WhatsApp number. Post new stock arrivals with photos and prices — not just promotions.

Run one LKR 500–1,000 ad targeting a 3km radius around your shop. Target the post that shows your best product photos. Even a small budget can reach 1,000–3,000 people in your immediate area who don't know you exist yet.

03
Build a WhatsApp broadcast list and use it weekly

WhatsApp is the primary direct communication tool in Sri Lankan retail. Not for new customer acquisition — but for keeping existing customers coming back. A shop with 200–500 contacts sending weekly updates has a reliable repeat business engine that costs nothing.

Add every customer to your WhatsApp Business broadcast list. When someone buys from you, ask 'Can I send you our new arrivals?' Most say yes. This list becomes your most valuable marketing asset.

Send one broadcast per week — new stock, a promotion, or a festival reminder. Not daily. Weekly is enough. The customers who stop buying are usually the ones you stopped communicating with.

New arrivals at Nilu's Fashion House — cotton sarees LKR 3,500, party dresses from LKR 4,200. We also have the new silk blouse collection in. WhatsApp us or come in — open till 8pm today.

04
Use Instagram if you're in fashion, cosmetics, or gifts

Instagram has 1.95 million Sri Lankan users — smaller than Facebook but skewed toward the younger, urban, image-conscious demographic that buys fashion and cosmetics. If your category is visual, this is the platform where customers discover products they then come in to try.

Post high-quality product photos 3–4 times per week. Natural light, clean background, visible price. For fashion: show the full outfit on a person. For cosmetics: before/after or product-in-use. No graphics with text over blurry photos.

Use Instagram Stories for festival promotions and new arrivals. Stories get more reach than feed posts for small accounts. Post every new stock arrival as a Story with the price visible.

8.2 million Sri Lankans are on Facebook. Your local 3km is a tiny ad budget away.

05
Add bill payment services to generate daily foot traffic

Dialog recharge, Mobitel top-up, CEB bill payments, water board — these services generate daily visits and earn 1–3% commission on each transaction. The commission is modest but the foot traffic is real: people who come to pay a bill often buy something else.

Contact Dialog and Mobitel directly to become an authorized recharge point. Setup is minimal. You need a basic smartphone, mobile data, and a printed sign. Customers who top up regularly will start thinking of your shop as a regular stop.

Add the service to your WhatsApp broadcast. 'We now handle Dialog/Mobitel recharge and CEB bill payments — no need to go to the bank.' This one message alone drives new first visits.

Bill payments and mobile recharge have 1–3% commission margins — thin. But the foot traffic they generate is the point. A customer who visits to top up every week is exposed to your stock every week. This is how you convert utility transactions into product sales.

06
The successful retailer checklist — what the busy shops do differently

Research across small Sri Lankan retail consistently identifies the same 7 characteristics in shops that stay full. Not all of them are about marketing.

Check how many of these you're doing: Specialisation in something the area lacks. Proprietor presence during peak hours (customers want to deal with the owner). Extended hours, especially evenings. Selective credit to trusted regulars. A WhatsApp group for announcements. Stock that's unique or locally sourced. Staff who know customers by name.

If you're doing fewer than 4, that's your answer. The shops that struggle aren't in worse locations or selling worse products — they're missing the relationship layer that makes small retail irreplaceable.

A customer who tops up Dialog at your shop every week sees your products every week.

You don't need to be on every platform. You need to be findable on Google, responsive on WhatsApp, and visible on Facebook — consistently, for 90 days. By then you'll know which channel sends you real customers. Double down there.
