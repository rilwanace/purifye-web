Strategies›Retail Shop›Manage My Stock & Suppliers›I bought too much of the wrong thing and now it's sitting there
50 pieces of that pink floral dress that Sanduni swore would sell. Three months later, they're still on the rack taking up prime space.
I bought a full case of that chili sauce because the price was good. I've sold four bottles in six weeks.
Every time I need cash I stare at the dead stock and calculate how much money is just sitting there doing nothing.

Dead stock is trapped capital. The longer it sits, the worse the outcome. Here's how to move it and how to stop it from accumulating in the first place.

01
Track what sells and what doesn't — even a simple notebook changes everything

Most dead stock is created by a buying decision made without sales data. An item looked right, the price was good, the rep was persuasive. Without knowing what your specific shop sells at what velocity, every buying decision is a guess.

Keep a sales velocity notebook for your top 20 items. Date, item, quantity sold. After 4 weeks, you know which items move in 3 days and which sit for 3 weeks. This knowledge is the prevention for dead stock.

For fashion specifically, track margin per garment. Which styles from which suppliers sell at full price? Which require discounts to move? After one season, your buying becomes dramatically more targeted.

Dead stock is capital waiting to be freed — the longer it waits, the less it returns.

02
Clear dead stock with targeted promotions — don't just mark it down and wait

A 30% markdown on a shelf does nothing if customers don't notice it or don't understand it's discounted. Clearance has to be actively communicated.

Send a WhatsApp broadcast specifically for clearance items. 'Making room for new stock — selected items 30–50% off this week only.' Name the items, show the photos, list the discounted prices. Create genuine urgency.

Create bundle deals around dead stock. A dress that isn't selling alone may sell as part of 'buy any two pieces, get 20% off both.' The bundle justifies the discount without signalling that one item is unwanted.

End of season clearance at Nilu's Fashion House — selected pieces 30–40% off. Cotton sarees now LKR 2,200 (was LKR 3,500). Making room for new collection coming next week. Come in this weekend or WhatsApp Sanduni to hold your size.

A clearance broadcast with photos, prices, and urgency moves stock that a discount sticker on a shelf doesn't.

03
The MRP trap — why some dead stock can't be saved with a price increase

For packaged goods, the Maximum Retail Price printed on the packaging is the legal ceiling. The Consumer Affairs Authority actively enforces this — a shop in May 2026 was fined LKR 100,000 for selling Keeri Samba above MRP. You cannot price above the marked price regardless of what you paid.

This creates a specific dead stock problem: goods bought at high import cost when the LKR was weak, now sitting at an MRP that leaves no margin. You can't raise the price, and you can't return them.

For MRP-controlled goods, the only exit is selling at low margin or zero margin to free up capital. The calculation is: hold (capital tied up, opportunity cost, possible expiry) vs sell now at cost (capital freed, shelf space freed). In most cases, selling now at cost wins.

The MRP trap: for packaged goods, you can't raise the price above what's printed, regardless of your cost.

04
Facebook Marketplace and Daraz move non-perishable dead stock to a wider audience

Items that aren't selling in your physical shop may sell to someone 20km away. Facebook Marketplace and Daraz both allow you to list excess inventory at discounted prices to a broader geographic audience.

List your top 5–10 dead stock items on Facebook Marketplace this week. Clear photos, honest description, discounted price. Even one sale per week adds up. The listing takes 10 minutes.

For fashion specifically, bulk listings on Daraz or ikman.lk move excess stock faster than retail. Price to clear, not to profit. Rs.500 net from a garment that cost you Rs.800 is better than Rs.0 when it doesn't sell in 6 more months.

For new styles: buy 3–5 pieces, not 20. Validate the sell-through before scaling the order.

05
Buy smaller quantities more frequently to prevent future dead stock

Dead stock is almost always a buying problem, not a selling problem. The item could sell — just not in the quantities you bought. The fix is to buy less per order and reorder based on actual selling.

For new items or uncertain sellers, buy half of what you think you need for the first order. If it sells fast, reorder. If it doesn't, you've limited your exposure. The cost of a slightly faster reorder trip is always less than the cost of clearing dead stock.

For fashion: buy 3–5 pieces of a new style, not 20. Your margin on 5 pieces sold is real money. Your loss on 15 pieces discounted erases that margin and then some.

Facebook Marketplace and ikman.lk reach customers 20km away who will buy what your local area won't.

06
Use festival seasons to clear old stock through bundling and hampers

Dead grocery stock clears fastest during festivals when customers are in a buying mood. A slow-moving item included in an Avurudu hamper sells when it wouldn't sell off the shelf on a normal day.

Build your festival hampers around a core fast-mover plus 2–3 dead-stock items. The core item makes the hamper attractive. The dead stock gets moved. Price the bundle at a modest discount to the full retail price of all items.

For fashion dead stock, create a 'mystery bundle' — 3 pieces for LKR X — priced attractively. Customers enjoy the discovery element and you move items that individually aren't selling.

Festival season clears slow stock through bundles — the core fast-mover justifies the bundle; the dead stock gets moved.

Dead stock is capital waiting to be freed. Clear it now at a loss before it becomes worthless. Then fix the buying decision that created it. Buy half your estimate for new items. Track velocity before reordering. The shops that don't create dead stock are the ones that buy based on what they actually sell — not what they hope to sell.
