Strategies›Retail Shop›Manage My Stock & Suppliers›I keep finding expired products on my shelves
I found three boxes of biscuits expired two months ago, shoved behind the shelf. I didn't even know they were there.
If a customer finds an expired product I sold them, that reputation is gone. I don't get a second chance with that person.
I know first-in-first-out. I just never actually do it because the shelf is already full when the new stock arrives.

Expired stock is pure loss — you paid for it, you can't sell it, you have to throw it away. Here's how to stop it from accumulating without adding hours to your day.

01
Calculate what expired stock is actually costing you

Most shop owners underestimate expired stock losses because they happen in small amounts throughout the year. Rs.200 here, Rs.400 there. But if you're discarding Rs.300 of stock per week that's Rs.15,600 per year — and that's if you're catching it before it becomes a legal and reputational problem.

Do a one-time shelf audit this week. Pull every item forward and check the expiry date. Write down everything within 30 days of expiry and everything already expired. Calculate the total cost at your purchase price.

This number is your baseline. Most shop owners who do this for the first time find Rs.5,000–20,000 of at-risk stock they didn't know about. Now you know what you're managing.

Rs.300 of expired stock per week is Rs.15,600 per year — before the reputational cost.

02
FIFO works — but only if you enforce it at receiving, not at selling

First-in-first-out is universally understood and almost never practised because the natural behaviour is to put new stock at the front (easiest to reach) and push old stock back. New stock goes in front, old stock gets buried. It's automatic.

Change your receiving habit: new stock goes BEHIND existing stock, always. Not a memo, not a policy — a physical discipline. Every time you receive a delivery, pull the old stock forward first, then stack the new stock behind it.

Train your staff on this once, explicitly. Show them physically. 'New behind old, every time.' Watch the first three deliveries to confirm it's happening. After that, the habit is set.

FIFO is enforced at receiving, not at selling. New stock behind old, every delivery.

03
Do a monthly shelf scan — it takes 20 minutes

There is no automated expiry alert system in small retail. You scan manually. The question is whether you do it systematically or randomly — and systematic catches things random misses.

Set the first Monday of every month as expiry scan day. Walk every shelf. Pull anything expiring within 30 days and put it in a designated 'clearance corner.' This takes 15–20 minutes in most small shops.

Anything expiring within 14 days gets a 30–40% discount sticker immediately. Don't wait until it expires. A product sold at 60p in the rupee is better than a product thrown away at 0p.

Distributors will not accept back expired stock that resulted from slow movement. That cost stays with you. The only leverage you have is to catch items before expiry and discount them fast enough to sell.

Near-expiry discounting is standard in Sri Lankan retail and customers respond positively.

04
A simple expiry tracker prevents the worst losses

For products with short shelf lives — dairy, imported goods with remaining shelf life issues, some FMCG categories — a written expiry tracker catches what visual shelf scanning misses.

For your 10–15 highest-risk items, keep a log: product name, batch number, expiry date, quantity received. Check this log monthly against the physical shelf. When an item approaches its flag date, it goes to the clearance corner immediately.

For pharmacy specifically, this is not optional — NMRA compliance requires proper expiry management. The record you keep protects you in an audit and keeps your license.

A 30% discount sticker on a 14-day item recovers 70% of cost. Zero discount on an expired item recovers nothing.

05
Turn near-expiry stock into a promotion — customers respond well

Near-expiry discounting is standard practice in Sri Lankan retail. Customers understand it and respond positively when the price is right. The key is timing — discount early enough that the customer has a genuinely useful product, not a 3-day item.

Create a dedicated 'clearance corner' with consistent, visible signage. 'Expiring soon — buy now at lower price.' No need to explain further. Customers who are price-sensitive specifically look for these sections.

Send a WhatsApp broadcast when you have a clearance batch. 'We have [X] items on clearance this week — [short list with prices]. Come in or WhatsApp for delivery.' This moves stock faster than shelf placement alone.

Clearance this week at Glow Beauty Corner — 3 face serums expiring end of month, LKR 950 each (was LKR 1,850). Products are completely fine — we're clearing to make room for new stock. WhatsApp Rashmi to hold one.

The distributor will not take back expired stock from slow movement. That cost is yours.

06
Most expired stock was bought wrong — fix buying to prevent expiry

Expiry problems are usually a buying problem disguised as a storage problem. You bought more of something than you could sell before it expired. This happens because distributors push near-expiry stock onto smaller retailers who have less power to refuse, and because buying in bulk for a price discount overestimates demand.

For anything with a short shelf life, refuse distributor allocations above what you can sell in half the remaining shelf life. If a product expires in 60 days, only accept what you can sell in 30 days. Be explicit with the rep: 'I'll take 2 cases, not 4.'

Track how fast each near-expiry category moves in your specific shop. After 3 months of tracking, you'll know your true velocity and can buy accordingly.

Most expired stock was bought wrong — overbought against an optimistic demand forecast.

Expired stock is a buying problem first and a storage problem second. The fix is: buy only what you can sell in half the remaining shelf life, enforce FIFO at receiving, and scan monthly. One afternoon of shelf audit this week tells you exactly what you're managing.
