A customer asked for Anchor milk powder at 7pm. I know I had it yesterday. Had to turn her away — and she'll be at Keells tomorrow.
The distributor rep doesn't come for another week and I've got an empty shelf where the Milo should be.
I feel like I'm always buying the wrong things — too much of what moves slowly and not enough of what moves fast.

Stockouts are the most expensive problem in small retail — a customer who can't find what they need doesn't wait, they go elsewhere. Here's how to prevent that without overbuying.

01
Understand the distributor cycle — and order before you run out

FMCG distributors visit approximately 600 outlets per rep on a 2-week cycle. When the rep comes, you place your order. If you miss an order window or order too little, you wait 14 days for the next visit. This is the fundamental rhythm of small retail supply.

Most stockouts happen for one reason: the visual reorder trigger ('I see only 2 packets, I should order more') fires too late. By the time you see 2 packets, you may have already sold through before the rep's next visit.

Set reorder levels BEFORE you hit low stock. For a fast-moving item, reorder when you have 5–7 days of stock left, not 2. Write the reorder level on a sticky label on the shelf edge.

A reorder list takes one afternoon to build and prevents 80% of stockouts.

02
Build a simple reorder list — this is the minimum system every shop needs

No formal system. No software. Just a sheet of paper with four columns: Product name — Minimum stock level (reorder when below this) — Supplier — Lead time in days.

Create this list for your top 30 fastest-moving items this week. Walk the shelves, note each product, set the minimum level based on how fast it sells and how long the supplier takes. This list takes one afternoon to build and prevents 80% of stockouts.

Check this list every Monday morning. Any item below its minimum level goes on the order call or WhatsApp to the supplier that day. Not when the rep comes — when you notice.

A reorder list doesn't need to be digital. The shops that do this well use a laminated A4 sheet taped to the stockroom wall. The discipline is checking it, not the format it's in.

The lost sales notebook reveals the invisible gap in your range — items customers ask for that you never stocked.

03
Track what customers ask for that you don't have

Stockouts you know about are the visible problem. But there's a hidden problem: customers who ask for something, don't find it, and leave without saying anything. You never find out what you missed.

Keep a lost sales notebook at the counter. Every time a customer asks for something you don't have, write it down: item name, date, quantity requested. After 2 weeks, look at the list. Anything that appears more than 3 times is a gap in your range.

Add the top 5 most requested missing items to your next order. This converts lost sales into found revenue in the most direct way possible.

Set reorder levels before you visually see low stock — not after.

04
Use Pettah wholesale as a backup source for urgent restocking

When you run out of a fast-moving item mid-cycle and can't wait for the rep, Pettah wholesale is your backup. Cash, no minimum orders, wide range, and prices that allow resale at a margin. Not a primary source for everything — logistics cost and time — but invaluable for urgent restocking.

Make one Pettah run per month if you have critical gaps. Group your urgent needs across categories for one efficient trip rather than multiple small runs. Pettah covers clothing, electronics accessories, household goods, imported food, stationery, cosmetics — most categories except cold chain FMCG.

Know which wholesale supplier in Pettah covers your category. Build one relationship with a Pettah wholesaler in your main category. When you're in trouble, you can WhatsApp them and confirm stock before making the trip.

Pettah wholesale is your backup when the distributor cycle fails you.

05
Stock seasonal items 4 weeks before the peak — not after it starts

The most common seasonal stocking mistake: buying Avurudu stock in the last week of March when demand is already building. By then, the best wholesale prices are gone and you're competing for allocation with every other shop that also waited.

Add seasonal stock dates to your calendar now. Avurudu goods ordered by 1 March. School supplies ordered by 1 December. Christmas items by 1 November. Wedding season clothing ordered by 1 May.

Order smaller quantities of seasonal items than you think you need for the first year. Until you know how much your specific location sells during each peak, underbuy and go back for more rather than overbuy and discount at the end.

Seasonal stock ordered 4 weeks early gets better prices and better allocation.

06
Manage the complexity that comes with the sachet economy

Post-2022, consumers permanently shifted to smaller packs and sachets. FMCG brands now offer more sachet SKUs than ever. This means more individual items to track, more shelf labels to maintain, and more opportunities for stockouts on the small sizes while the large sizes sit full.

Track your top 10 sachet SKUs separately from their larger-pack equivalents. Sachet sizes move differently and run out faster. They need their own minimum stock levels and their own order tracking.

If a sachet consistently runs out before the rep visit, order double on the next cycle. Two weeks of extra safety stock on a Rs.15 sachet costs you Rs.150 of capital. The cost of a customer going to Keells for the sachet and doing the rest of their shopping there is far higher.

A sachet that runs out before the rep visit needs double safety stock on the next order.

A stock reorder list is the minimum inventory system every shop needs. It takes one afternoon to build. It pays for itself the first week you catch a stockout before it happens. Build it. Check it every Monday. The empty shelf is the most expensive thing in small retail.
